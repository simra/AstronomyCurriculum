ASTR 120 Problem Set 05

Lectures 09-10: Giant planets, icy moons, oceans, and rings

Due and Scope

Due: [insert date]. Submit calculations, evidence tables, and short interpretations.

Learning objectives: compare giant planets using density, sunlight, heat flow, rotation, and magnetic fields; assess icy-ocean evidence; use orbital scaling for rings and moons.

Instructions: Keep units visible. State when values are rounded and when evidence is indirect. Revisions may be resubmitted with a change note.

Problems

1. Giant-Planet Density and Composition 25 points

Use this lecture table: Jupiter \(M=318M_E, R=11.2R_E\); Saturn \(M=95M_E, R=9.45R_E\); Uranus \(M=14.5M_E, R=4.01R_E\); Neptune \(M=17.1M_E, R=3.88R_E\). Use \(\rho/\rho_E=(M/M_E)/(R/R_E)^3\) and \(\rho_E=5.51\,\mathrm{g\ cm^{-3}}\).

  1. Compute each mean density.
  2. Identify which is less dense than water.
  3. Explain why low density does not mean the planet is physically small or low mass.
  4. Interpret Uranus and Neptune relative to Jupiter and Saturn in terms of H/He versus ice/rock enrichment.

Deliverables: density table and composition interpretation.

2. Sunlight and Internal Heat in the Outer Solar System 25 points

Solar flux scales as \(1/r^2\). Use distances 5.2, 9.5, 19.2, and 30.0 AU for Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

  1. Compute sunlight relative to Earth for each planet.
  2. If Neptune emits about 2.6 times as much energy as it absorbs from the Sun while Uranus emits roughly the same as it absorbs, explain what this implies about internal heat.
  3. Describe one observation that helps distinguish absorbed sunlight from internal heat.

Deliverables: relative-flux table and interpretation.

3. Europa Ocean Evidence Chain 25 points

A mission observes an induced magnetic signal, a young fractured surface with few craters, and possible plume material. Build an evidence chain for a subsurface ocean.

  1. For each observation, state the measurement and the ocean-related interpretation.
  2. Give one non-ocean ambiguity or systematic uncertainty for each observation.
  3. Rank the observations from most direct to least direct for a present ocean.
  4. Explain why tidal heating and resonance are relevant to keeping liquid water possible.

Deliverables: evidence chain table and ranked conclusion.

4. Ring-Particle Orbital Periods 25 points

For particles orbiting the same planet, \(P\propto r^{3/2}\). Saturn's main rings extend roughly from \(75,000\) km to \(137,000\) km from Saturn's center.

  1. Compute \(P_{outer}/P_{inner}\).
  2. If the inner edge period is 5.0 hours, estimate the outer edge period.
  3. Explain why different orbital periods cause shear and why narrow rings need confinement or replenishment.

Deliverables: period-ratio calculation and ring-dynamics explanation.
