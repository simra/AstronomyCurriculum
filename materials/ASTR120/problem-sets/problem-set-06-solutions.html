Instructor-facing worked solutions

Instructor Notes

Problem 1. Impact Energy and Hazard Scaling

Worked solution: Radius is 40 m. Volume \(V=4\pi r^3/3=4\pi(40)^3/3=2.68\times10^5\,\mathrm{m^3}\). Mass \(m=\rho V=3000\times2.68\times10^5=8.04\times10^8\) kg. Speed \(v=2.0\times10^4\,\mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}\). Energy \(E=0.5(8.04\times10^8)(2.0\times10^4)^2=1.61\times10^{17}\) J. TNT equivalent \(=1.61\times10^{17}/4.184\times10^{15}=38.5\) Mt. If speed doubles, energy increases by \(2^2=4\).

Problem 2. Comet Orbit and Activity Window

Worked solution: Semimajor axis \(a=(q+Q)/2=(1.2+35)/2=18.1\) AU. Period \(P=a^{3/2}=18.1^{1.5}=77\) yr. Activity increases near perihelion because sunlight heats surface ices, causing sublimation, coma formation, jets, and dust release. Compared with an asteroid, a comet nucleus is typically more ice-rich, lower density/porosity, and activity-prone. Ambiguity: dormant comets can resemble dark asteroids, and some asteroids show activity after impacts or volatile release.

Common errors: using aphelion as semimajor axis or assuming all asteroids are dry and all comets active.

Problem 3. Transit Depths and Planet Radii

Worked solution: Jupiter-size transit: \(\delta=(0.100)^2=0.010\), or 1.0% = 10,000 ppm. Earth-size transit: \(\delta=(0.00916)^2=8.39\times10^{-5}\), or 83.9 ppm. The Jupiter-size planet is easier to detect because the signal is far larger than 200 ppm single-transit precision. Transit geometry favors close-in planets with higher transit probability, and instrumental bias favors large planets, quiet stars, short periods, and repeated transits with high signal-to-noise.

Problem 4. Solar-System Expectations versus Exoplanet Architecture

Worked solution: Convert days to years: 3.0 d = 0.00821 yr, 8.5 d = 0.0233 yr, 31 d = 0.0849 yr. Then \(a=P^{2/3}\): b = 0.0407 AU, c = 0.0816 AU, d = 0.193 AU. All three are much closer than Mercury and larger than Earth, unlike the solar system's small inner rocky planets and distant giants. This architecture is easy for transit surveys because close-in planets transit more often and with higher probability. Close-in sub-Neptunes may form farther out and migrate inward, form in compact disks, or accrete/lose atmospheres after formation.
