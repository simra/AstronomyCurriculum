ASTR 120 Problem Set 02

Lectures 03-04: Orbital dynamics, resonances, interiors, and differentiation

Due and Scope

Due: [insert date]. Submit calculations, a short interpretation for each problem, and any spreadsheet or code used to check arithmetic.

Learning objectives: use Kepler scaling; identify resonances; infer interior properties from mass, radius, density, heat, and magnetic evidence; distinguish calculation from interpretation.

Instructions: Show equations, units, assumptions, and reasoning. Collaboration is allowed for discussion only. Revisions may be resubmitted with a change note.

Problems

1. Kepler Scaling and Resonance Check 25 points

Three small bodies orbit the Sun with semimajor axes 2.50 AU, 3.28 AU, and 5.20 AU. Use \(P^2=a^3\), with \(P\) in years and \(a\) in AU.

  1. Compute each orbital period.
  2. Compute the period ratios relative to the 5.20 AU object.
  3. Identify whether either inner body is close to a 2:1 or 3:1 mean-motion resonance with the outer body.
  4. Explain why resonance can either remove objects from some orbits or maintain tidal heating in others.

Deliverables: period table, resonance-ratio calculation, and 120-160 word interpretation.

2. Tidal Heating as an Energy Chain 25 points

Two moons have the same radius, density, and parent planet, but Moon A has eccentricity \(e=0.010\) and Moon B has \(e=0.004\). In a simplified model, tidal heating scales as \(e^2\) when all other properties are fixed.

  1. By what factor is Moon A heated more strongly than Moon B?
  2. If Moon B dissipates \(2.0\times10^{12}\) W, estimate Moon A's dissipation.
  3. Explain why a resonance is needed to keep eccentricity from damping away.
  4. Name one observation that would support active tidal heating and one ambiguity in that observation.

Deliverables: scaling calculation with units, a causal energy-chain diagram in words, and evidence/ambiguity pair.

3. Density, Differentiation, and Core Fraction 25 points

World X has \(M=0.055M_E\) and \(R=0.383R_E\). World Y has \(M=0.815M_E\) and \(R=0.949R_E\). Use \(\rho/\rho_E=(M/M_E)/(R/R_E)^3\).

  1. Compute \(\rho_X/\rho_E\) and \(\rho_Y/\rho_E\).
  2. Using \(\rho_E=5.51\,\mathrm{g\ cm^{-3}}\), compute both densities in g cm\(^{-3}\).
  3. Which world is more suggestive of a large metal core? Explain without claiming density alone proves the full interior.
  4. State two measurements, beyond mass and radius, that would help test differentiation.

Deliverables: density calculations, comparison, and evidence paragraph.

4. Interior Evidence Matrix 25 points

A spacecraft returns the following for a rocky planet: weak present magnetic field; large volcanic plains younger than nearby highlands; heat-flow estimate twice the lunar value but below Earth's; gravity anomalies over major basins; no global plate tectonics.

  1. Build a four-row evidence matrix using magnetic field, volcanism, heat flow, and gravity anomalies.
  2. For each row, state what interior property it constrains and one alternative explanation.
  3. Write a conclusion: is this world likely fully dead, weakly active, or Earth-like active? Defend your category.

Deliverables: evidence matrix and 180-220 word conclusion.
