ASTR 120 Problem Set 03

Lectures 05-06: Terrestrial surfaces, atmospheres, and climate

Due and Scope

Due: [insert date]. Submit calculations, one relative-age sketch or table, and concise written interpretations.

Learning objectives: infer surface ages from crater counts and cross-cutting relations; compare atmospheric escape; apply albedo and energy balance; distinguish observation from climate interpretation.

Instructions: Use units throughout. State assumptions about equal areas, impact rates, preservation, and greenhouse forcing. Revisions may be resubmitted with a change note.

Problems

1. Crater Counts and Resurfacing 25 points

Two equal-area image tiles on the same planet cover \(1.0\times10^5\,\mathrm{km^2}\) each. Tile A has 84 craters larger than 2 km. Tile B has 12 craters larger than 2 km and a smooth lava plain crossing older crater rims.

  1. Compute crater density for each tile in craters per \(10^5\,\mathrm{km^2}\).
  2. Compute the ratio of crater densities.
  3. Rank the tiles by relative surface age and cite the evidence.
  4. State two assumptions that could make the age inference unreliable.

Deliverables: crater-density table, ratio, and age interpretation.

2. Cross-Cutting Sequence on a Terrestrial Surface 25 points

An image shows these relationships: a large crater cuts highland terrain; a lava flow fills the crater floor; a wrinkle ridge crosses the lava flow; a small fresh crater sits on top of the ridge with bright rays; wind streaks extend from the fresh crater.

  1. List the five events from oldest to youngest.
  2. For each ordering step, state the stratigraphic rule or observation used.
  3. Explain how illumination angle or image scale could mislead the interpretation.

Deliverables: ordered event list with evidence notes and one imaging caveat paragraph.

3. Escape Speed and Atmospheric Retention 25 points

Use \(v_{esc}=11.2\,\mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}\sqrt{(M/M_E)/(R/R_E)}\). Compare Earth, Mars \((M=0.107M_E, R=0.532R_E)\), and Venus \((M=0.815M_E, R=0.949R_E)\).

  1. Compute escape speed for Mars and Venus.
  2. Rank Earth, Mars, and Venus by escape speed.
  3. Explain why escape speed alone does not determine present atmosphere thickness.
  4. Name two additional variables that control atmospheric loss or retention.

Deliverables: escape-speed calculations, ranking, and interpretation.

4. Albedo, Solar Flux, and Greenhouse Interpretation 25 points

Use absorbed solar flux \(F_{abs}=S(1-A)/4\). At Earth's orbit \(S=1361\,\mathrm{W\ m^{-2}}\). Planet C orbits at 0.72 AU with albedo 0.75. Planet D orbits at 1.52 AU with albedo 0.25. Assume solar flux scales as \(1/r^2\).

  1. Compute \(S\) at each planet.
  2. Compute absorbed flux for each planet.
  3. Which absorbs more sunlight per square meter?
  4. Explain why the planet that absorbs more sunlight need not have the higher surface temperature once greenhouse effect and atmospheric pressure are included.

Deliverables: flux calculations with units and a climate-interpretation paragraph.
