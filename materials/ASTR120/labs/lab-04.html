Lab 04: Atmospheres, Escape, and Climate

Compare greenhouse warming and atmospheric retention for Venus, Earth, Mars, Titan, and Mercury using measured planetary properties.

Learning Objectives

  • Calculate greenhouse offset from effective and observed surface temperatures.
  • Estimate thermal molecular speeds and compare them with escape velocity.
  • Evaluate why pressure, composition, albedo, and gravity must be interpreted together.

Preparation

Review Lecture 6 on radiative balance, greenhouse warming, atmospheric pressure, and atmospheric escape.

Materials and Data

  • ../data/lab-04-atmospheres-climate.csv, containing solar flux, albedo, effective temperature, surface temperature, pressure, escape velocity, and atmospheric composition.
  • Spreadsheet or calculator with square-root function.
  • NASA Planetary Fact Sheet values and Titan satellite fact-sheet values; see ../data/lab-data-provenance.md.

Apparatus and Setup

  1. Open the CSV and create columns for greenhouse offset, molecular thermal speed, and escape-speed ratio.
  2. For mean molecular mass, convert atomic mass units to kg using 1 amu = 1.6605 x 10-27 kg.
  3. Use surface temperature for molecular-speed estimates and mark Mercury as exosphere-only rather than a stable lower atmosphere.

Procedure

  1. Calculate greenhouse offset as \(T_{surface} - T_{effective}\) for every body with both temperatures listed.
  2. Estimate root-mean-square molecular speed using \(v_{rms}=\sqrt{3kT/m}\), where \(k=1.380649\times10^{-23}\) J K-1.
  3. Compute \(v_{escape}/v_{rms}\) for Venus, Earth, Mars, and Titan. Exclude Mercury from the ratio table if you cannot assign a single atmospheric molecular mass.
  4. Make a two-axis comparison table: greenhouse offset and escape-speed ratio.
  5. Classify each world as greenhouse-dominated, escape-limited, pressure-limited, or exosphere-only, and defend the classification with measurements.

Analysis and Uncertainty

Use +/-3 K for effective and surface temperatures unless your instructor supplies more precise values. Propagate greenhouse-offset uncertainty by adding temperature uncertainties in quadrature. For molecular speed, evaluate sensitivity by recalculating with mean molecular mass +/-1 amu; for mixed atmospheres this is a model uncertainty, not an instrumental measurement error.
