Lab 01: Planetary Scale, Density, and Architecture

Use measured planetary properties to test the lecture claim that the solar system is ordered by formation temperature, composition, and distance from the Sun.

Learning Objectives

  • Compute and compare bulk density from mass and radius for terrestrial, giant, and ice-giant planets.
  • Identify architecture trends with distance, size, composition class, surface gravity, and satellite count.
  • Separate robust trends from trends that depend on rounding, classification, or small-number comparisons.

Preparation

Review Lecture 1 on scale and comparative planetology and Lecture 2 on solar-system formation. Bring a spreadsheet or calculator that can evaluate powers and unit conversions.

Materials and Data

  • ../data/lab-01-planetary-properties.csv, a classroom table of planetary semimajor axis, radius, mass, density, gravity, and known moons.
  • Spreadsheet software or a scientific calculator.
  • NASA Planetary Fact Sheet values, rounded to classroom precision; see ../data/lab-data-provenance.md.

Apparatus and Setup

  1. Open the CSV and freeze the header row.
  2. Create columns for volume, calculated density, density residual, planet class, and architecture note.
  3. Use SI units: convert radius from km to m and mass from 1024 kg to kg before calculating density.
  4. Record the access date as 2026-09-23 and mark the table as real public reference data rounded for instruction.

Procedure

  1. For Mercury, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, calculate \(\rho = M/(4\pi R^3/3)\) and convert kg m-3 to g cm-3.
  2. Compare your calculated densities with the supplied bulk-density column. Flag any residual greater than 0.05 g cm-3 and explain whether rounding can account for it.
  3. Make a scatter plot of semimajor axis versus radius, using symbol color for planet class and symbol size for known moon count.
  4. Rank the eight planets by density and by surface gravity. Identify one pair whose ranking changes between the two lists and explain why.
  5. Write a two-sentence architecture claim that uses at least three measured quantities, not just the planet names.

Analysis and Uncertainty

Treat radius values as rounded to the last listed digit and mass values as rounded to the last listed decimal place. Propagate density uncertainty with \(\Delta\rho/\rho \approx \Delta M/M + 3\Delta R/R\). In the conclusion, identify which comparisons are insensitive to this uncertainty and which depend on category boundaries such as terrestrial versus ice giant.

Deliverables

  • Completed spreadsheet with calculated density, residual, and uncertainty columns.
  • One scatter plot of planetary architecture with a caption that names the data file.
  • A 250-word evidence paragraph explaining the strongest and weakest architecture trend.
  • Provenance note citing NASA Planetary Fact Sheet and lab-01-planetary-properties.csv.

Assessment Criteria

  • Correct unit conversion and density calculation: 30%.
  • Plot communicates at least three measured variables clearly: 20%.
  • Uncertainty treatment uses rounding limits and identifies robust comparisons: 20%.
  • Interpretation connects measurements to formation architecture without overclaiming: 20%.
  • Complete provenance note: 10%.
