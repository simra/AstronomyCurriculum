Lab 07: Mission Design Trade Study

Use a weighted decision matrix to choose a solar-system mission concept and defend the choice with evidence, risk, and uncertainty.

Learning Objectives

  • Translate science questions into measurement payload requirements.
  • Balance science return, technical risk, planetary protection, operations complexity, and flight time.
  • Test whether a mission recommendation is robust to alternate stakeholder weights.

Preparation

Review Lectures 11-14 on small bodies, exoplanet context, habitability, planetary protection, and mission design. Bring notes from one previous ASTR 120 lab that could motivate a science question.

Materials and Data

  • ../data/lab-07-mission-trade-matrix.csv, an instructor-created trade table with named mission precedents and NASA source pages.
  • Spreadsheet for weighted scoring and sensitivity tests.
  • Course lab results from at least one earlier activity to use as science rationale.

Apparatus and Setup

  1. Open the CSV and add columns for normalized benefit, normalized cost/risk, baseline weighted score, and alternate weighted score.
  2. Use baseline weights: science return 40%, technical risk 20%, planetary protection 15%, operations complexity 15%, flight time 10%.
  3. For risk, protection, complexity, and flight time, invert the score so lower burden increases the total: converted score = 6 - listed score for 1-to-5 columns; for flight time, rank concepts from 5 for shortest to 1 for longest.

Procedure

  1. For each concept, identify the primary science question and the payload measurement that directly tests it.
  2. Calculate a baseline weighted score using the setup weights.
  3. Create an alternate stakeholder scenario: astrobiology-first, low-cost-first, or planetary-protection-first. Define the new weights before seeing the result.
  4. Recalculate scores and record whether the preferred mission changes.
  5. Choose one mission and write a traceability chain: science question -> measurement -> instrument -> target -> risk or uncertainty.

Analysis and Uncertainty

Treat all 1-to-5 ratings as uncertain by +/-1 unless the precedent mission already demonstrated the relevant capability. Run a one-at-a-time sensitivity test for the winning concept: lower its science score by 1, raise its dominant risk by 1, and lengthen flight time by one rank. Report whether it still wins. Discuss any ethical or planetary-protection consequence that a numerical score hides.

Deliverables

  • Completed decision matrix with baseline and alternate stakeholder weights.
  • One ranked chart of mission scores with the winning concept highlighted.
  • One-page mission recommendation with traceability chain and sensitivity result.
  • Provenance note citing NASA mission/program pages and lab-07-mission-trade-matrix.csv.

Assessment Criteria

  • Decision matrix calculations and inverted risk scoring are correct: 30%.
  • Science traceability connects question, measurement, instrument, and target: 25%.
  • Sensitivity analysis tests real uncertainty rather than just restating preference: 20%.
  • Planetary-protection or ethical constraint is addressed concretely: 15%.
  • Complete provenance note: 10%.
