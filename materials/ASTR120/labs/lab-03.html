Lab 03: Crater Counting and Relative Surface Age

Use crater density from named lunar and Martian image products to infer relative surface ages and uncertainty from counting statistics.

Learning Objectives

  • Calculate crater density for equal-area samples of planetary surfaces.
  • Use Poisson counting uncertainty to compare relative ages.
  • Explain why crater counts require provenance, diameter threshold, and geologic context.

Preparation

Review Lectures 5 and 7 on terrestrial surfaces, airless worlds, crater saturation, resurfacing, and relative chronology.

Materials and Data

  • ../data/lab-03-crater-counts.csv, an instructor-count table from named real mosaics: LROC WAC global lunar mosaic and Mars Odyssey THEMIS daytime IR mosaic.
  • Calculator or spreadsheet.
  • Optional instructor-provided image windows for annotation; the numerical task can be completed from the CSV.

Apparatus and Setup

  1. Open the crater-count CSV and confirm that all four samples use 10,000 km2 counted areas.
  2. Create columns for crater density above 1 km, crater density above 5 km, Poisson uncertainty, and relative-age rank.
  3. Record the processing state as instructor-count table from real NASA mosaics, not a published crater chronology catalog.

Procedure

  1. For each region, calculate crater density \(N/A\) for D >= 1 km and D >= 5 km craters.
  2. Calculate 1-sigma Poisson uncertainty as \(\sqrt{N}/A\) for both diameter thresholds.
  3. Rank the four surfaces from youngest to oldest using D >= 1 km density. Repeat using D >= 5 km density.
  4. Identify whether either ranking is ambiguous once uncertainty bars are considered.
  5. Write a process interpretation for each region: volcanic resurfacing, ancient highlands, young plains, or heavily cratered terrain.

Analysis and Uncertainty

Report crater density in craters per 10,000 km2 and craters per km2. Use \(\sqrt{N}\) as the counting uncertainty, then divide by area. Discuss at least one non-statistical uncertainty: secondary craters, eroded crater rims, illumination angle, image resolution, or geologic-unit boundary placement.

Deliverables

  • Completed crater-density table with uncertainty for both crater thresholds.
  • Bar chart or dot plot with uncertainty bars for D >= 1 km crater density.
  • Annotated ranking statement that identifies any overlap in uncertainty.
  • Provenance note naming LROC WAC, Mars Odyssey THEMIS, and lab-03-crater-counts.csv.

Assessment Criteria

  • Correct crater-density and Poisson calculations: 30%.
  • Graph includes uncertainty bars and units: 20%.
  • Relative-age interpretation follows the evidence and notes ambiguity: 25%.
  • Non-statistical uncertainty is specific to crater counting: 15%.
  • Complete data provenance: 10%.
