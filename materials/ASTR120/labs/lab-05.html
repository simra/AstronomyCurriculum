Lab 05: Mars Water Evidence

Classify multiple Mars observations by the strength and ambiguity of their evidence for past or present water.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish geomorphic, mineralogical, stratigraphic, and in situ chemical evidence for water.
  • Assign confidence levels while acknowledging ambiguous interpretations.
  • Connect Mars mission measurements to habitability claims without overstating the evidence.

Preparation

Review Lecture 8 on Mars water, climate evolution, rover exploration, and habitability evidence.

Materials and Data

  • ../data/lab-05-mars-water-evidence.csv, a curated table of named Mars sites, missions, evidence types, measured quantities, and uncertainty notes.
  • Colored pens or annotation software for an evidence matrix.
  • NASA Mars mission science summaries for Mars 2020, MER Opportunity, MRO CRISM/HiRISE, and MSL Curiosity.

Apparatus and Setup

  1. Open the CSV and create columns for evidence class, water timescale, confidence score, and alternate explanation.
  2. Use this confidence scale: 3 = strong evidence for sustained past liquid water, 2 = water-related but environment or duration uncertain, 1 = ambiguous or contested, 0 = not water evidence by itself.
  3. Prepare a one-page matrix with rows for sites and columns for geomorphology, mineralogy, chemistry, stratigraphy, and ambiguity.

Procedure

  1. Classify each site or feature by evidence type: geomorphic, mineralogical, stratigraphic, chemical, or seasonal surface-change observation.
  2. Assign a confidence score using the scale in the setup. Justify every score with a measured quantity from the CSV.
  3. Identify the strongest case for a long-lived habitable environment and the weakest case for present liquid water.
  4. For Jezero delta and Gale crater mudstone, compare whether the evidence is primarily shape-based, composition-based, or stratigraphic.
  5. Write a mission recommendation: which one site should receive a follow-up sample or measurement, and what measurement would reduce the largest uncertainty?

Analysis and Uncertainty

Treat numerical uncertainty as listed in the CSV. Treat confidence score as classification uncertainty: if two students could reasonably differ by one point, mark the score as plus or minus 1 and explain the disputed assumption. Present-water claims require stricter evidence than ancient-water claims because salts, slope streaks, and dry granular flows can mimic liquid-related features.

Deliverables

  • Completed evidence matrix with confidence scores and ambiguity notes.
  • Ranked list of the five sites from strongest to weakest water evidence.
  • One 300-word recommendation for a follow-up measurement or sample target.
  • Provenance note naming the NASA mission pages and lab-05-mars-water-evidence.csv.

Assessment Criteria

  • Evidence classes and confidence scores are justified with data: 30%.
  • Uncertainty discussion distinguishes measurement error from interpretive ambiguity: 25%.
  • Ranking separates ancient water evidence from present liquid-water claims: 20%.
  • Follow-up recommendation targets a real unresolved measurement: 15%.
  • Complete provenance note: 10%.
