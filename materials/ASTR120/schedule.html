ASTR 120 Schedule

14-week review-release trajectory

WeekLectureFocus
1Why Study the Solar System?Use scale, density, and mission evidence to compare worlds.
2Solar System Architecture and FormationExplain how disk temperature and angular momentum shape planetary architecture.
3Orbital Dynamics and ResonancesUse period, semi-major axis, and resonance ratios to interpret orbital systems.
4Planetary Interiors and DifferentiationInfer interior structure from mass, radius, density, and magnetic evidence.
5Terrestrial Planet SurfacesUse surface features to infer relative age and geological process.
6Atmospheres and ClimateApply energy balance and atmospheric evidence to compare climates.
7The Moon, Mercury, and Airless WorldsInterpret airless surfaces using craters, rays, regolith, and composition.
8Mars: Water, Climate, and ExplorationEvaluate evidence for past water and habitability on Mars.
9Giant PlanetsCompare giant planets using composition, heat flow, rotation, and magnetic fields.
10Moons, Rings, and Icy Ocean WorldsAssess evidence for subsurface oceans and active icy-world geology.
11Small Bodies: Asteroids, Comets, and MeteoritesUse composition, orbit, and impact evidence to interpret small bodies.
12Exoplanetary Systems in Solar-System ContextCompare exoplanet architectures with solar-system formation expectations.
13Habitability and Planetary ProtectionEvaluate habitability claims using environment, energy, chemistry, and evidence quality.
14Mission Design, Synthesis, and Open QuestionsDesign a simple mission concept tied to testable solar-system science.
