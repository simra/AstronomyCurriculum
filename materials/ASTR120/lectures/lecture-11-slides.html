Small Bodies: Asteroids, Comets, and Meteorites

Solar-system evidence, physics, and exploration

Why This Question Matters

Meteorite laboratories and missions such as Rosetta, Hayabusa, OSIRIS-REx, and DART connect samples, orbits, and planetary defense.

Learning Goals

  • Use composition, orbit, and impact evidence to interpret small bodies.
  • Connect a mission or observation to the physical interpretation.
  • Use a quantitative or comparative argument with explicit assumptions.

Opening Phenomenon

Small bodies preserve primitive material and record collisions, migration, and delivery of volatiles.

Small Bodies Are Archives and Hazards

Asteroids: rocky and metallic remnants shaped by collisions and heating.

Comets: volatile-rich bodies that become active near the Sun.

Meteorites: hand-sample evidence linking laboratory chemistry to parent bodies.

Why Leftovers Matter

Planets are processed worlds. Small bodies can preserve earlier material, record collisional evolution, and deliver impact energy that reshapes planetary surfaces and atmospheres.

Evidence We Need

  • Meteorite chemistry preserves primitive and differentiated materials.
  • Comet activity reveals volatile sublimation.
  • DART demonstrated measurable asteroid deflection.

Composition, Orbit, and Activity

Classify a small body by combining orbit, spectrum, density, and activity. A tail indicates present volatile loss; it does not automatically tell you where the object formed.

Impact Energy Calculation

\[ E={1\over2}mv^2 \]

A 1,000 kg meteoroid at 20 km/s carries about 200 billion joules of kinetic energy. Doubling the speed quadruples the energy, so orbital encounter velocity is central to hazard assessment.

Worked Case: Same Mass, Different Velocity

  • At 10 km/s, the energy scale is one quarter of the 20 km/s case.
  • At 30 km/s, the energy scale is 2.25 times the 20 km/s case.
  • Impact consequences depend on speed, entry angle, fragmentation, composition, and target surface.

Meteorites as Ground Truth

Laboratory measurements of meteorites reveal ages, isotopes, minerals, and thermal histories that cannot be extracted from telescope images alone. The challenge is connecting each sample to a parent body.

Small-Body Pitfall: Names Are Not Physics

Asteroid, comet, meteoroid, meteor, and meteorite describe context as well as composition. A dormant comet can look asteroid-like until heating reveals volatile activity.

Reasoning Prompt: Primitive or Processed?

You observe a dark object with a main-belt orbit, low albedo, and hydrated minerals. Is it primitive, aqueously altered, or comet-like? Choose two follow-up observations that would sharpen the classification.

Synthesis: Small Bodies Connect Time Scales

  • Composition records early condensation and alteration.
  • Orbits record delivery pathways and resonant transport.
  • Impacts connect small bodies to planetary surface evolution and risk.
