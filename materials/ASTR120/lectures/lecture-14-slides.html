Mission Design, Synthesis, and Open Questions

Solar-system evidence, physics, and exploration

Why This Question Matters

Every mission design encodes scientific priorities: what to measure, at what precision, from what orbit or flyby, with what risk.

Learning Goals

  • Design a simple mission concept tied to testable solar-system science.
  • Connect a mission or observation to the physical interpretation.
  • Use a quantitative or comparative argument with explicit assumptions.

Opening Phenomenon

Space missions turn questions into payloads, trajectories, measurements, and tradeoffs.

Mission Design Is Scientific Traceability

Question: one testable claim that changes planetary understanding.

Measurement: observable quantity with needed precision, coverage, and context.

Payload and trajectory: instruments and operations that can actually acquire the measurement.

Review the Course Arc

Every topic in ASTR 120 used the same discipline: connect a physical model to evidence, name the uncertainty, and choose the next observation that could discriminate among explanations.

Evidence We Need

  • Science traceability links question, measurement, instrument, and trajectory.
  • Mission design trades risk, cost, resolution, and coverage.
  • A payload should test a hypothesis, not just take pretty pictures.

Traceability Matrix as a Reasoning Tool

Read the mission chain from left to right and right to left. If the instrument cannot make the needed measurement, the science question has not been converted into an executable mission.

Trade Metric from the Local Mission Table

\[ \mathrm{priority}\sim {\mathrm{science\ return}\over \mathrm{technical\ risk}+\mathrm{operations\ complexity}+\mathrm{protection\ burden}} \]

This simplified score is not a NASA formula. It helps compare why a high-return plume sample return can still be harder to justify than a lower-risk flyby or scout mission.

Worked Concept: Europa Plume Flythrough

  • Question: can plume material reveal ocean composition?
  • Measurements: dust composition, ultraviolet plume structure, magnetic context.
  • Risks: uncertain plume activity, planetary protection burden, and limited flyby geometry.

Evidence Comparison Across Mission Concepts

A Venus cloud microprobe tests chemistry quickly but in a harsh environment. A Trojan asteroid flyby is lower risk but answers a more archival formation question. An Enceladus sample-return concept has high scientific return and high complexity.

Mission Pitfall: Payload Lists Are Not Science Cases

A mission concept is weak if it starts with favorite instruments and only later invents science. Start with the discriminating question, then derive measurement requirements and payload.

Capstone Prompt: Defend One Mission

Choose Venus clouds, Europa plume flythrough, Enceladus plume sample return, Mars polar ice drill, or Trojan asteroid flyby. State the question, decisive measurement, biggest risk, and the result that would most change the field.

Synthesis: ASTR 120 Reasoning Toolkit

  • Use scale, density, spectra, dynamics, geology, and climate as linked evidence systems.
  • Separate what was measured from what was inferred.
  • Design the next observation to discriminate among physical explanations.
