ASTR 120 · Lecture 02

Solar System Architecture and Formation

Solar-system evidence, physics, and exploration

Why This Question Matters

Kant and Laplace proposed nebular ideas; modern disks around young stars and meteorites now let us test formation models.

Learning Goals

  • Explain how disk temperature and angular momentum shape planetary architecture.
  • Connect a mission or observation to the physical interpretation.
  • Use a quantitative or comparative argument with explicit assumptions.

Opening Phenomenon

Regularities in orbits, rotation, composition, and small bodies record formation in a protoplanetary disk.

Architecture Clues to Formation

Nearly flat orbits: planets remember a rotating disk, not random capture.

Composition gradient: rock and metal dominate close to the Sun; ices become available farther out.

Leftover bodies: asteroids, comets, and meteorites preserve incomplete accretion.

From Collapsing Cloud to Disk

  • A large cloud can shrink dramatically while keeping most of its angular momentum.
  • Rotation prevents direct collapse into the young Sun, so gas and dust spread into a disk.
  • Collisions damp vertical motion, which makes the planet-forming region thin and ordered.

Evidence We Need

  • Planet orbits are nearly coplanar and mostly prograde.
  • Rocky and volatile-rich bodies separate with distance from the Sun.
  • Meteorites preserve early solids from the disk.

Disk Sorting: Temperature Becomes Composition

Read the diagram as a causal chain: high temperature near the Sun restricts solids to metal and silicate grains; beyond the frost line, ice adds mass and speeds core growth.

Quantitative Model: A Moving Frost Line

\[ T(r)\approx 280\ \mathrm{K}\left({1\ \mathrm{AU}\over r}\right)^{1/2} \]
  • Water ice becomes stable near the radius where disk temperatures fall below roughly 170 K.
  • This simple scaling gives a frost-line distance of about 2.7 AU.
  • The early solar nebula was evolving, so the boundary moved with time and accretion rate.

Worked Formation Argument

At 1 AU, silicates and metals provide the main solid inventory; at 5 AU, ice adds abundant solid material. A larger solid core can form faster, capture gas before the disk disperses, and become a giant planet rather than a terrestrial planet.

Evidence Comparison: Meteorites and Planet Orbits

  • Meteorites preserve early condensed solids and thermal processing.
  • Coplanar prograde orbits preserve disk-scale angular momentum.
  • Together they test both the chemical and dynamical parts of the nebular model.

Model Limit: Order Is Not Perfection

The nebular model explains broad architecture, but migration, impacts, resonances, and disk turbulence modify the final system. Do not treat present distance from the Sun as a perfect record of formation distance.

Reasoning Prompt: What Would Break the Model?

Suppose a system has giant planets close to the star and rocky planets farther out. Which part of the simple nebular story fails first: condensation chemistry, gas capture timing, or later orbital migration?

Synthesis: Formation Leaves Multiple Fingerprints

  • Angular momentum explains flat, ordered planetary orbits.
  • Temperature controls which solids are available for planet building.
  • Modern architectures must be read as formation plus later migration and collision history.
