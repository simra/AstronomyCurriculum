Exoplanetary Systems in Solar-System Context

Solar-system evidence, physics, and exploration

Why This Question Matters

Radial-velocity and transit surveys revealed hot Jupiters, super-Earths, and compact systems that forced revisions to formation theory.

Learning Goals

  • Compare exoplanet architectures with solar-system formation expectations.
  • Connect a mission or observation to the physical interpretation.
  • Use a quantitative or comparative argument with explicit assumptions.

Opening Phenomenon

Exoplanets test whether our solar system is typical or unusual.

Exoplanets Test Solar-System Assumptions

Transits: planet size from fractional starlight blocked.

Radial velocity: planet mass constraints from stellar wobble.

Demographics: populations reveal architectures the solar system does not contain.

The Solar System Is One Outcome

Hot Jupiters, compact multi-planet systems, and super-Earths show that planet formation and migration produce a wider range of architectures than our local example alone suggested.

Evidence We Need

  • Hot Jupiters exist very close to their stars.
  • Transit and radial velocity methods favor different planets.
  • Compact multi-planet systems challenge simple solar-system analogies.

Detection Bias Shapes the Catalog

The diagram links signal size to what surveys find first. Large planets close to small stars are easier to detect by transit, so raw discoveries are not an unbiased census.

Transit Depth Calculation

\[ \delta\approx\left({R_p\over R_*}\right)^2 \]
  • A Jupiter-radius planet transiting a Sun-like star blocks about 1 percent of the light.
  • An Earth-radius planet transiting a Sun-like star blocks about 0.008 percent.
  • The same Earth-size planet is easier to detect around a smaller star.

Worked Architecture Comparison

A close-in giant planet is hard to reconcile with simple in-place frost-line formation. It points toward migration, scattering, or disk-driven movement after formation.

Evidence Pairing: Radius Needs Mass

Transit data can give size, but density requires mass. A 2-Earth-radius planet could be rocky, water-rich, or gas-enveloped unless radial velocity or transit timing helps constrain mass.

Exoplanet Pitfall: Discovery Counts Are Not Occurrence Rates

A catalog dominated by easy detections does not mean the universe is dominated by those planets. Survey geometry, cadence, stellar type, and noise all filter the sample.

Reasoning Prompt: Solar-System Context

You discover a compact system of five sub-Neptunes inside Mercury's orbit. Which solar-system formation expectation does it challenge most: frost-line composition, long-term stability, or migration history?

Synthesis: One System Becomes a Comparative Baseline

  • The solar system supplies physical intuition and constraints.
  • Exoplanets reveal outcomes absent from our local architecture.
  • Detection methods must be modeled before population claims are made.
