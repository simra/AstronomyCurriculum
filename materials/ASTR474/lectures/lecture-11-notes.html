Lecture 11: Likelihoods, Residuals, and Parameter Estimation — Lecture Notes

What evidence distinguishes a fitted parameter from a visually plausible curve?

Problem and physical meaning

Least-squares fitting minimizes a chosen residual objective, not a universal truth criterion. Heteroscedastic observations require uncertainty-scaled residuals; structured residuals reveal model inadequacy even when an optimizer reports success.

What evidence distinguishes a fitted parameter from a visually plausible curve? Begin from the scientific observable or state variable, and name what is directly specified versus inferred. The model should be simple enough to validate, but not quietly presented as the entire astrophysical system.

Model and derivation

The governing expression for this lecture is:

\[\chi^2(\theta)=\sum_i\left[\frac{y_i-m(t_i;\theta)}{\sigma_i}\right]^2\]

Define the symbols and the independent variable before numerical evaluation. Dimensional analysis checks that both sides have matching units; if the model is dimensionless, state the adopted scale factors and the transformation back to physical units.

Least-squares fitting minimizes a chosen residual objective, not a universal truth criterion. Heteroscedastic observations require uncertainty-scaled residuals; structured residuals reveal model inadequacy even when an optimizer reports success.

Numerical implementation

Define a physically interpretable model; scale parameters; optimize weighted residuals with SciPy; inspect residual versus time and phase; estimate covariance from the Jacobian only under local linearity and stated noise assumptions.

Keep physical quantities and numerical arrays conceptually separate. Convert units at well-defined boundaries, preserve raw inputs, and record dtype, solver, tolerances/timestep, grid, and software versions. A numerical method is part of the model evidence, not an invisible implementation detail.

Error budget and limitations

The inverse Jacobian curvature is a local approximation, not a robust interval when parameters are degenerate, boundaries bind, noise is non-Gaussian, or the model is wrong.

Classify each uncertainty before combining terms: local/global solver error, timestep or spatial discretization, Monte Carlo sampling, parameter uncertainty, measurement uncertainty, and model discrepancy are not interchangeable. Conservation can test internal consistency but does not prove the model is physically complete.

Connection to evidence and research practice

Lab 06 fits a seeded synthetic radial-velocity series and requires parameter uncertainty and residual diagnostics.

For real parameters, the source record distinguishes the fetched product and epoch from derived values. For synthetic experiments, the initial conditions, generator, seed, and exact file are recorded. A simulation is not an observation, even when its parameters resemble a real object.

Misconceptions to avoid

  • More displayed digits do not imply more physical accuracy.
  • A successful solver exit or smooth plot does not establish convergence.
  • Agreement between two solvers with the same wrong model is not independent physical validation.
  • A formal covariance or Monte Carlo interval is conditional on the adopted model and noise process.

Lecture-specific derivation and assumptions

With fixed angular frequency omega, write v(t)=gamma+K sin(omega*t+phi)=gamma+a sin(omega*t)+b cos(omega*t), where a=K cos(phi), b=K sin(phi). The design matrix is linear in (gamma,a,b); weighted least squares solves (X^T W X)c=X^T W y. Recover K=sqrt(a^2+b^2) and phi=atan2(b,a). This independent linear parameterization is a useful cross-check on nonlinear optimization and avoids the amplitude/phase curvature during the fit.

Algorithm trace and computed-output interpretation

Fit the 64 seeded observations with sigma=3 m/s and fixed P=11.2 d. Record K, phase, offset, local standard errors, weighted chi-square, 61 degrees of freedom, residual RMS, and maximum residual. Plot residual versus time and orbital phase. The supplied nonlinear solver's covariance rescales local curvature by reduced chi-square; an alternative treating sigma as known uses (J^T W J)^-1 without that scale factor, and must state its assumption.

Computed result: The seeded fit returns K=20.8286 m/s, phase=0.69746 rad, gamma=3.1325 m/s, residual RMS=3.0897 m/s, and chi-square=67.882 for 61 degrees of freedom.

A low residual RMS does not validate the period or sinusoidal model; phase coverage and residual structure matter. Standard errors are local and conditional on fixed period, Gaussian independent errors, and the model. A trend, additional periodicity, or phase-dependent scatter would motivate a richer model rather than merely a different optimizer.

Two-meeting teaching arc

Each weekly module is taught in two 75-minute meetings. The sequence below includes a formative check in each meeting and is intended to use the lecture's worked computation, not merely present it.

  1. Meeting 1 (75 min): 10 min inference setup; 20 min likelihood and weighted residuals; 25 min sinusoid linearization; 15 min identifiability/phase coverage; 5 min design-matrix check.
  2. Meeting 2 (75 min): 10 min recap; 20 min run nonlinear and linear fits; 25 min interpret covariance and residuals; 15 min model criticism; 5 min state a falsifiable extension.
