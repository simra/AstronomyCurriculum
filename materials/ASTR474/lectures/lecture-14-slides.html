Motivation

Which checks are decisive enough to support the final scientific conclusion?

A research computation needs a model argument, numerical evidence, and a bounded interpretation. The strongest claim is often the one that survives changes in resolution, timestep, seed, and plausible physical assumptions.

Today’s result must be interpretable as a physical statement with declared data, algorithm, uncertainty, and a check that could fail.

Numerical method

Algorithm choice is a modeling decision

State the scientific question; identify inputs and provenance; choose a domain solver; design a convergence and uncertainty plan before running; compare an independent method or analytic limit; archive code, environment, data, and validation report.

  1. Write the model and its assumptions.
  2. Choose a solver and identify its stability, accuracy, and conservation properties.
  3. Record configuration, tolerances or timestep, and output sampling.
  4. Compare against an analytic limit, independent method, or controlled refinement.

Verification

What would falsify this result?

  • Repeat with a smaller timestep, tighter tolerance, or finer spatial grid.
  • Track conservation or residual structure in addition to visual agreement.
  • Check units, coordinate frame, boundary conditions, and invariants independently.
  • Change a random seed when the method is stochastic; report the resulting sampling uncertainty.

Do not report a smoother curve as proof of convergence.

Synthesis

Three durable checks

  1. Does the implemented equation match the intended physical model?
  2. Does the numerical result converge under a controlled method change?
  3. Does the interpretation stay inside the validity and provenance limits?
