Lecture 03: Kepler Orbits with REBOUND — Lecture Notes

What does a symplectic integrator preserve, and what does it not promise?

Problem and physical meaning

For a dominant central mass and weak perturbations, Wisdom-Holman splitting separates Keplerian drift from perturbing kicks. WHFast is fixed-step and symplectic; IAS15 is adaptive and high-order. Their errors have different structure and should be selected by the problem, not by a single accuracy slogan.

Model and derivation

The governing expression for this lecture is:

\[H=\sum_i\left(\frac{p_i^2}{2m_i}-\frac{GMm_i}{r_i}\right)+H_{\rm interaction}\]

Define the symbols and the independent variable before numerical evaluation. Dimensional analysis checks that both sides have matching units; if the model is dimensionless, state the adopted scale factors and the transformation back to physical units.

For a dominant central mass and weak perturbations, Wisdom-Holman splitting separates Keplerian drift from perturbing kicks. WHFast is fixed-step and symplectic; IAS15 is adaptive and high-order. Numerical implementation

Initialize units before particles; validate the two-body orbit; sweep fixed timesteps; record energy and angular momentum; compare a one-day Sun-Earth propagation with the JPL state in a consistent heliocentric frame.

Keep physical quantities and numerical arrays conceptually separate. Convert units at well-defined boundaries, preserve raw inputs, and record dtype, solver, tolerances/timestep, grid, and software versions. A numerical method is part of the model evidence, not an invisible implementation detail.

Worked, programmatically computed example

Using Astropy GM_sun, the circular 1-AU Kepler period is 365.25690 d. After one day, REBOUND and independent SciPy two-body runs differ from the DE441 Earth-center vector by 126.0 km and 126.1 km respectively; the residual is model/frame scope, not an accuracy claim.

These values are generated in src/astr474_computations.py, then shared with lecture/lab/assignment renderers. Re-run the generator after changing an input; do not hand-edit a displayed result without recomputing dependent artifacts.

At least one independent check is required: an analytic solution, a second numerical method, a conservation law, or a controlled convergence sequence. When these disagree, diagnose units, frame, discretization, source terms, and model scope before selecting a result.

Error budget and limitations

An ideal two-body propagation is not an ephemeris. Earth-center state, Sun motion, Moon, other planets, and relativistic terms matter when comparing against DE441.

Classify each uncertainty before combining terms: local/global solver error, timestep or spatial discretization, Monte Carlo sampling, parameter uncertainty, measurement uncertainty, and model discrepancy are not interchangeable. Conservation can test internal consistency but does not prove the model is physically complete.

Connection to evidence and research practice

Lab 02 uses the level-2 JPL vector record and distinguishes integrator error from model discrepancy.

For real parameters, the source record distinguishes the fetched product and epoch from derived values. For synthetic experiments, the initial conditions, generator, seed, and exact file are recorded. A simulation is not an observation, even when its parameters resemble a real object.

Misconceptions to avoid

  • More displayed digits do not imply more physical accuracy.
  • A successful solver exit or smooth plot does not establish convergence.
  • Agreement between two solvers with the same wrong model is not independent physical validation.
  • A formal covariance or Monte Carlo interval is conditional on the adopted model and noise process.

Lecture-specific derivation and assumptions

For a circular orbit, equate centripetal acceleration v^2/r to GM/r^2, obtaining v^2=GM/r. With v=2*pi*r/P, rearrangement gives P^2=4*pi^2*r^3/(GM). For a two-body system use relative separation and G(M+m); the test-particle approximation drops m only when m/M is negligible. Real ephemeris vectors require matching target, center, epoch scale, reference plane, correction convention, and units before a residual has meaning.

Convert Astropy GM_sun to AU^3/day^2, evaluate the circular 1-AU period, and compare with the 365.25-day calendar convention. Two-meeting teaching arc

  1. Meeting 1 (75 min): 10 min state-vector context; 20 min Newtonian two-body reduction; 20 min Kepler-period derivation and units; 20 min Astropy constant check; 5 min frame-convention exit ticket.
