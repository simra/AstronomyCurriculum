Motivation

When does a numerically returned digit carry physical information?

Numerical method

Algorithm choice is a modeling decision

Define dimensional inputs; nondimensionalize where useful; record dtype and versions; establish analytic limits; perturb inputs and precision; report only stable significant digits.

  1. Write the model and its assumptions.
  2. Choose a solver and identify its stability, accuracy, and conservation properties.
  3. Record configuration, tolerances or timestep, and output sampling.
  4. Compare against an analytic limit, independent method, or controlled refinement.

Verification

What would falsify this result?

  • Repeat with a smaller timestep, tighter tolerance, or finer spatial grid.
  • Track conservation or residual structure in addition to visual agreement.
  • Check units, coordinate frame, boundary conditions, and invariants independently.
  • Change a random seed when the method is stochastic; report the resulting sampling uncertainty.

Do not report a smoother curve as proof of convergence.

Failure modes

Numerical agreement has a domain

Machine precision is not the same as scientific accuracy. A stable calculation can still solve a misspecified physical model.

Solver error, discretization error, stochastic error, parameter uncertainty, and model discrepancy are different quantities. Diagnose and report them separately.
