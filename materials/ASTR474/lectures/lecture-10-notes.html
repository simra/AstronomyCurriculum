Lecture 10: Monte Carlo Radiation and Rare Events — Lecture Notes

Can importance sampling reduce variance without changing the target expectation?

Problem and physical meaning

Importance sampling draws from a proposal distribution that oversamples rare outcomes, then corrects every draw by the target-to-proposal likelihood ratio. The estimator stays unbiased when support is adequate, but variance can rise if weights are unstable.

Can importance sampling reduce variance without changing the target expectation? Begin from the scientific observable or state variable, and name what is directly specified versus inferred. The model should be simple enough to validate, but not quietly presented as the entire astrophysical system.

Model and derivation

The governing expression for this lecture is:

\[\mathbb E_p[f(X)]=\mathbb E_q\left[f(X)\frac{p(X)}{q(X)}\right]\]

Numerical implementation

Define target and proposal distributions; derive weights before sampling; compare estimates and standard errors under independent seeds; inspect weight concentration and effective sample size; retain an exact benchmark.

Keep physical quantities and numerical arrays conceptually separate. Convert units at well-defined boundaries, preserve raw inputs, and record dtype, solver, tolerances/timestep, grid, and software versions. A numerical method is part of the model evidence, not an invisible implementation detail.

Error budget and limitations

A lower standard error in one seeded run is not a guarantee; compare repeated independent seeds or analytic variance and never omit likelihood weights.

Classify each uncertainty before combining terms: local/global solver error, timestep or spatial discretization, Monte Carlo sampling, parameter uncertainty, measurement uncertainty, and model discrepancy are not interchangeable. Conservation can test internal consistency but does not prove the model is physically complete.

Misconceptions to avoid

  • More displayed digits do not imply more physical accuracy.
  • A successful solver exit or smooth plot does not establish convergence.
  • Agreement between two solvers with the same wrong model is not independent physical validation.
  • A formal covariance or Monte Carlo interval is conditional on the adopted model and noise process.

Lecture-specific derivation and assumptions

For target density p(x) and proposal q(x), require q(x)>0 wherever f(x)p(x) is nonzero. Then E_q[f(X)p(X)/q(X)]=integral f(x)p(x) dx=E_p[f(X)], proving unbiasedness. For a Bernoulli success indicator under target probability p and proposal q, the weighted contribution is p/q on success and zero on failure. Weight variability controls variance; effective sample size is often summarized by (sum w)^2/sum(w^2), but it does not replace direct uncertainty analysis.

Algorithm trace and computed-output interpretation

At tau=4, compute the exact rare-event probability and compare N=10,000 ordinary draws with proposal q=0.2. Apply p/q only to proposal successes, retain the zero contribution for failures, and calculate the sample standard error from weighted contributions. Derive the likelihood ratio before sampling and repeat independent seeds; a single result with lower error is not proof that the proposal is universally superior.

Computed result: At N=10000, ordinary-sampling standard error is 0.0013723; the likelihood-weighted proposal gives 0.00036502. One run is evidence for this seed, not a universal variance guarantee.

The proposal changes the sampling law, not the target scientific quantity. A proposal with insufficient support creates bias; extreme weights can make nominally large N behave like a much smaller effective sample. For this two-outcome example, all weights and variance can be calculated exactly, providing a useful audit of the implementation.

Two-meeting teaching arc

Each weekly module is taught in two 75-minute meetings. The sequence below includes a formative check in each meeting and is intended to use the lecture's worked computation, not merely present it.

  1. Meeting 1 (75 min): 10 min rare-event problem; 20 min change-of-measure derivation; 20 min Bernoulli likelihood weights; 20 min support and variance; 5 min predict a bad proposal failure.
  2. Meeting 2 (75 min): 10 min recap; 20 min compute ordinary and weighted estimates; 25 min compare independent seeds and ESS; 15 min diagnose weight concentration; 5 min identify when to reject the estimator.
