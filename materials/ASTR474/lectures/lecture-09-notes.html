Problem and physical meaning

Monte Carlo estimates expectations by sampling a probability law. Independent Bernoulli transmission trials have standard error approximately sqrt[p(1-p)/N], so precision improves only as N^-1/2.

Misconceptions to avoid

  • More displayed digits do not imply more physical accuracy.
  • A successful solver exit or smooth plot does not establish convergence.
  • Agreement between two solvers with the same wrong model is not independent physical validation.
  • A formal covariance or Monte Carlo interval is conditional on the adopted model and noise process.

Lecture-specific derivation and assumptions

For independent Bernoulli transmission indicators X_i with probability p=e^-tau, E[X_i]=p and Var(X_i)=p(1-p). The sample mean p_hat has variance p(1-p)/N and estimated standard error sqrt(p_hat(1-p_hat)/N). Therefore doubling precision requires about four times as many independent histories. A fixed seed makes a run repeatable in a recorded environment; it does not turn one realization into a confidence guarantee or ensure identical streams across arbitrary library versions.

Algorithm trace and computed-output interpretation

For tau=2, compute p analytically, then use the seeded 20,000-trial vector and inspect its nested prefixes at N=100, 500, 2,000, and 20,000. For each N report k, p_hat, plug-in standard error, and absolute deviation from p. Repeat with independent seeds and compare empirical spread with the predicted N^-1/2 scaling; do not mistake a one-standard-error band for a 68% guarantee at small counts.

Computed result: For tau=2, analytic transmission is 0.135335; the seeded N=20000 estimate is 0.137800 +/- 0.002437 (one standard error).

The nested-prefix estimates are correlated because they reuse earlier trials; their differences are not independent replications. The Bernoulli standard error is an asymptotic plug-in estimate, while exact binomial intervals are preferable near p=0 or p=1. Record the seed, generator, package version, N, and event definition.
