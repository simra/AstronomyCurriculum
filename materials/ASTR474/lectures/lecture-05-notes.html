Why should a discretization conserve fluxes across cell boundaries?

Problem and physical meaning

The compressible Euler equations evolve mass, momentum, and total energy through flux divergence. Shocks are weak solutions: conservation and entropy admissibility matter even when derivatives are discontinuous.

Why should a discretization conserve fluxes across cell boundaries? Begin from the scientific observable or state variable, and name what is directly specified versus inferred. The model should be simple enough to validate, but not quietly presented as the entire astrophysical system.

Model and derivation

The governing expression for this lecture is:

\[\partial_t\mathbf U+\partial_x\mathbf F(\mathbf U)=0,\quad \mathbf U=(\rho,\rho u,E)^T\]

Numerical implementation

Keep physical quantities and numerical arrays conceptually separate. Convert units at well-defined boundaries, preserve raw inputs, and record dtype, solver, tolerances/timestep, grid, and software versions. A numerical method is part of the model evidence, not an invisible implementation detail.

Error budget and limitations

The course's first-order Rusanov shock-tube code is a pedagogical finite-volume demonstrator, not a production hydrodynamics solver. It omits multidimensional physics, reconstruction, well-balanced treatment, and production-grade boundary models.

Classify each uncertainty before combining terms: local/global solver error, timestep or spatial discretization, Monte Carlo sampling, parameter uncertainty, measurement uncertainty, and model discrepancy are not interchangeable. Conservation can test internal consistency but does not prove the model is physically complete.

Misconceptions to avoid

  • More displayed digits do not imply more physical accuracy.
  • A successful solver exit or smooth plot does not establish convergence.
  • Agreement between two solvers with the same wrong model is not independent physical validation.
  • A formal covariance or Monte Carlo interval is conditional on the adopted model and noise process.

Lecture-specific derivation and assumptions

In one dimension, mass conservation is partial_t rho + partial_x(rho*u)=0. Momentum conservation is partial_t(rho*u)+partial_x(rho*u^2+p)=0. Total energy E=p/(gamma-1)+rho*u^2/2 obeys partial_t E+partial_x[u(E+p)]=0 for an ideal gas without external sources. Combining the three conserved densities gives U=(rho,rho*u,E) and flux F=(rho*u,rho*u^2+p,u(E+p)). Across a shock, these equations hold in integral/weak form; entropy admissibility selects the physical branch.
