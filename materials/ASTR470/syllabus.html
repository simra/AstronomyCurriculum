ASTR 470: High-Energy Astrophysics

3 credits · Year 4 elective · Prerequisite: ASTR 330 · PHYS 341 recommended

Catalog description

Examines compact objects, accretion disks, supernova remnants, pulsars, black holes, active galactic nuclei, cosmic rays, X-ray and gamma-ray instrumentation, and high-energy transient phenomena.

Learning outcomes

  • Derive and apply radiation, accretion, compactness, shock, timing, and detector-response relations.
  • Interpret X-ray, gamma-ray, particle, and timing evidence while separating measurement from model.
  • Analyze real high-energy systems including the Crab pulsar, Cygnus X-1, supernova remnants, AGN, and transient alerts.
  • Compare mission specifications and select an instrument for a science requirement.
  • Quantify uncertainty, background, selection effects, and approximation limits.
  • Design and defend a reproducible multimessenger research plan.

Resources and preparation

Required reading uses exact OpenStax Astronomy 2e sections 20.4, 23.1-23.4, 24.1, 24.5-24.7, and 27.1-27.2, with indexed exercise anchors recorded in the reference log. Official Chandra, Swift, Fermi, NICER, and IceCube documentation supplies instrument context. PHYS 341 is recommended because several lectures use electromagnetic radiation, synchrotron emission, and detector response.

Cadence rationale

This 3-credit elective uses 14 lectures and 7 matched two-lecture units. Each lab and problem set follows the second lecture of its unit because high-energy inference requires both the source physics and the instrument or statistical context before students analyze a record. The final unit is a multimessenger dossier rather than a separate eighth lab, leaving time for synthesis and revision. The 14/7/7 structure is therefore intentional and documented here and in the manifest.
