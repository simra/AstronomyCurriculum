Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Compton scattering

Inelastic scattering in which a photon transfers energy and momentum to an electron. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for Compton scattering, pair conversion, coded masks, effective area, and triggers.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Burst trigger and Pair conversion. The governing relation \(S/N=S\\sqrt{t}/\\sqrt{S+B}\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The Poisson expression assumes independent counts and a stable background estimate. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Conceptual route

Connect detector geometry to localization, background, and rapid alerts Begin with the observable and work backward: source process, propagation, instrument response, calibration, inference, and uncertainty. This ordering prevents a physically attractive model from being mistaken for a direct measurement.

Evidence chain

Boundary and sensitivity check

Repeat the calculation after perturbing the dominant input by 10 percent. Explain whether the conclusion changes because of measurement uncertainty, calibration, or model choice. A limiting-case check is more informative than a second unexamined decimal place.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Do not equate an energy band with a source temperature, a pulse period with a surface rotation speed without checking geometry, or an observed correlation with a unique mechanism. Keep measured, calibrated, inferred, and model-dependent quantities in separate columns of your notes.

Study by redrawing the evidence chain and annotating every arrow with a unit, response, or assumption.

Laboratory and problem-set bridge

The paired two-lecture activity uses a concrete data table or mission specification. Preserve provenance, calculate an uncertainty or sensitivity, and write a caption that distinguishes observation from interpretation. The problem set asks for transfer, not simple repetition.
