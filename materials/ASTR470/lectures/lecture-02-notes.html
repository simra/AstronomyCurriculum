Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Accretion disk

A rotating flow that transports angular momentum outward while matter loses orbital energy and moves inward. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for thin-disk energetics, Roche geometry, boundary layers, and spectral states.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Accretion efficiency and Roche lobe. The governing relation \(L_{acc}=GM\\dot{M}/R\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The radius is an illustrative inner-disk scale; spin and relativistic efficiency are omitted. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Boundary and sensitivity check

Repeat the calculation after perturbing the dominant input by 10 percent. Explain whether the conclusion changes because of measurement uncertainty, calibration, or model choice. A limiting-case check is more informative than a second unexamined decimal place.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Do not equate an energy band with a source temperature, a pulse period with a surface rotation speed without checking geometry, or an observed correlation with a unique mechanism. Keep measured, calibrated, inferred, and model-dependent quantities in separate columns of your notes.

Study by redrawing the evidence chain and annotating every arrow with a unit, response, or assumption.
