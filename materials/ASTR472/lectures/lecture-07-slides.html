Observation before interpretation

Calibration solves for antenna-based complex gains by comparing measured correlations with a source model.

  • A baseline visibility is multiplied by one antenna gain and the complex conjugate of the other.
  • A flux calibrator establishes amplitude scale; a nearby phase calibrator tracks rapid atmospheric/instrumental phase changes.
  • Residual phase decorrelation reduces coherent amplitude approximately as exp(-sigma_phi^2/2) for Gaussian phase noise.

Keep four layers distinct in your notebook: raw correlation or spectrum, calibrated product, model parameter, and physical claim.

Physical model

For antennas p and q, V_pq^obs=g_p g_q* V_pq^true+n_pq. With g=a exp(i phi), phase differences and amplitude products are coupled across baselines, so solve a connected antenna graph against a calibrator model. Bandpass calibration estimates frequency dependence; delay is the phase slope dphi/dnu divided by 2 pi.

\(V_{pq}^{\rm obs}(\nu,t)=g_p(\nu,t)g_q^*(\nu,t)V_{pq}^{\rm true}(\nu,t)+n_{pq}\)

Define each quantity and convention before substitution. A relation is useful only inside its stated regime.

Where the inference can fail

  • Check the frequency, velocity frame, beam definition, and calibration convention before comparing values.
  • Use residuals and alternate models to distinguish a weak signal from a plausible but non-unique interpretation.
  • Report thermal noise separately from calibration, selection, and model uncertainty.
