Observation before interpretation

Longer integrations help only as the square root of time when receiver and sky noise are stationary and samples are independent.

  • Thermal noise is reduced by averaging independent time-frequency-polarization samples.
  • Narrow spectral channels improve velocity resolution but reduce the number of samples per channel.
  • Real observations depart from the ideal through flagged data, correlated noise, gain drift, and atmospheric fluctuations.

Keep four layers distinct in your notebook: raw correlation or spectrum, calibrated product, model parameter, and physical claim.

Physical model

For system temperature T_sys and bandwidth Delta-nu, one independent sample has fractional thermal uncertainty proportional to T_sys. Averaging n_pol polarizations for time tau produces sigma_T=T_sys/sqrt(n_pol Delta-nu tau). With SEFD=2 k T_sys/A_eff expressed in flux-density units, sigma_S=SEFD/sqrt(n_pol Delta-nu tau); correlator conventions can introduce an efficiency factor eta_s in the denominator.

\(\sigma_S=\frac{\mathrm{SEFD}}{\eta_s\sqrt{n_{\rm pol}\,\Delta\nu\,\tau}}\)

Define each quantity and convention before substitution. A relation is useful only inside its stated regime.

Worked calculation

Inputs: SEFD=300 Jy, n_pol=2, Delta-nu=1 MHz, tau=3600 s, eta_s=1.

sigma_S = 3.536 mJy

The radiometer result is a thermal lower bound. RFI flagging, quantization/correlator efficiency, calibration drift, and confusion can dominate, so ideal sqrt(t) extrapolation eventually fails.

Where the inference can fail

The radiometer result is a thermal lower bound. RFI flagging, quantization/correlator efficiency, calibration drift, and confusion can dominate, so ideal sqrt(t) extrapolation eventually fails.

  • Check the frequency, velocity frame, beam definition, and calibration convention before comparing values.
  • Use residuals and alternate models to distinguish a weak signal from a plausible but non-unique interpretation.
  • Report thermal noise separately from calibration, selection, and model uncertainty.
