Observation before interpretation

A spectral cube combines two-dimensional angular response with a frequency axis whose calibration becomes velocity only after a rest frequency and convention are chosen.

  • A narrow line can be diluted by channels wider than its intrinsic width.
  • The radio convention v=c(nu_0-nu)/nu_0 differs slightly from optical and relativistic conventions at high speeds.
  • Bandpass ripple, baseline subtraction, and sideband/LO conventions can imitate broad weak lines.

Keep four layers distinct in your notebook: raw correlation or spectrum, calibrated product, model parameter, and physical claim.

Physical model

For |v| much less than c, the first-order Doppler relation is Delta-nu/nu_0 approximately -v_rad/c. Therefore delta-v=c delta-nu/nu_0. At larger speeds use the chosen relativistic Doppler definition and document the frame correction (topocentric, barycentric, LSR).

\(v_{\rm rad}\simeq c\frac{\nu_0-\nu}{\nu_0},\qquad\delta v\simeq c\frac{\delta\nu}{\nu_0}\)

Define each quantity and convention before substitution. A relation is useful only inside its stated regime.

Where the inference can fail

  • Check the frequency, velocity frame, beam definition, and calibration convention before comparing values.
  • Use residuals and alternate models to distinguish a weak signal from a plausible but non-unique interpretation.
  • Report thermal noise separately from calibration, selection, and model uncertainty.
