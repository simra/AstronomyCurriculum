ASTR 230: Stars, Galaxies, and the Universe

3 credits · Prerequisites: ASTR 101; PHYS 171 or concurrent enrollment · Year 2, Spring

Description

Introduces stellar properties, stellar evolution, the interstellar medium, the Milky Way, galaxy classification, galaxy evolution, large-scale structure, dark matter, dark energy, and the observational foundations of modern cosmology. Quantitative reasoning builds on ASTR 101's introductory survey and on the mechanics and electromagnetism developed concurrently in PHYS 171, using algebra-and-trigonometry-level physics (no calculus is required, though students concurrently in PHYS 171 will see the same force and energy concepts from a calculus-based perspective).

Learning Outcomes

  1. Derive and apply the physical relationships among stellar luminosity, temperature, radius, and distance (Stefan–Boltzmann law, inverse-square law, distance modulus, parallax) to real stellar data.
  2. Explain the physical basis of stellar spectral classification and construct and interpret Hertzsprung–Russell diagrams for real star samples and clusters.
  3. Trace the evolutionary path of low-mass and high-mass stars from the interstellar medium through the main sequence to white dwarfs, neutron stars, and black holes, with quantitative mass-luminosity and mass-radius reasoning.
  4. Describe the structure of the Milky Way and the observational evidence (rotation curves, velocity dispersions) for dark matter.
  5. Classify galaxies using the Hubble tuning fork and connect galaxy morphology to stellar populations, gas content, and evolutionary history, including galaxy interactions and active galactic nuclei.
  6. Apply Hubble's law to redshift-distance data to estimate the age and expansion history of the universe, and summarize the observational case for dark energy and large-scale cosmic structure.

Lecture, Lab, and Problem-Set Cadence

The course runs 14 lectures, 7 labs, and 7 problem sets — a matched 1:1 biweekly cadence in which each lab and its paired problem set both follow the same two-lecture unit, so students immediately apply a unit's quantitative tools to a hands-on data set (the lab) and to independent numeric problems (the problem set) before the next unit begins. This mirrors the biweekly structure used in ASTR 101 and ASTR 120 rather than ASTR 210's specialized capstone-synthesis cadence, because ASTR 230's units introduce a new physical regime (stellar structure, then stellar death, then galaxies, then cosmology) every two lectures rather than building toward a single technical report.

Prerequisites Check

ASTR 101 provides the descriptive foundation (celestial sphere, light, telescopes, a first look at stars/galaxies/cosmology) that ASTR 230 makes quantitative. PHYS 171 (or concurrent enrollment) supplies Newtonian mechanics, energy, and momentum, used here for orbital and escape-velocity reasoning (binary stars, black holes) and for the physics of radiation pressure and gravitational collapse in star formation.
