ASTR 370 Problem Set 03: Flare Energetics and CME/Flare Timescales

Extend this unit's free-energy and timescale tools to new field strengths, GOES classes, and transit-time scenarios.

Problems

1. Free energy at a different field strength 15 points

Recompute the free-energy estimate using a weaker active-region field of 2000 G (rather than this unit's 2800 G) at the same representative volume used in the lab. Compute the factor by which the free energy changes and confirm it matches the B^2 scaling in E_free=(B^2/2 mu0)V.

2. GOES class arithmetic 15 points

NOAA classifies a flare as X9.3 if its peak 1-8 Angstrom flux is 9.3 x 10^-4 W/m^2. Compute the peak flux corresponding to an X1.8 flare and a C4.5 flare, and compute the ratio of the X1.8 flare's flux to the C4.5 flare's flux.

3. Timescale ratio sensitivity 10 points

If the real CME transit time had instead been exactly 24 hours (rather than this unit's ~36 hours), recompute the ratio between this transit time and the light travel time (1 AU/c, computed in the lab), and state whether the flare-radiation-to-CME-arrival lag would be longer or shorter than in the real event.

4. Naive vs observed CME speed implied by transit time 15 points

Using the real observed transit time (~36 hours) and the 1 AU Sun-Earth distance, compute the implied average CME speed over its full transit, and compare this "effective" speed to the CME's initial LASCO-measured speed (1571 km/s). State what this comparison implies about the CME's average deceleration during transit.

Due and Scope

Submit a complete derivation with equations, units, labeled quantities, and a short narrative interpretation for each problem. Show every step; a correct final number without a visible derivation receives partial credit at most, and quoting a lecture number without adapting it to this problem's specific inputs receives no credit for that step.
