The Solar Magnetic Field and the 11-Year Cycle

Differential rotation, the dynamo, and the real sunspot-number record

Learning Goals

  1. Describe the Babcock-Leighton dynamo mechanism qualitatively: differential rotation winding up a poloidal field into a toroidal field.
  2. Interpret the sunspot butterfly diagram and the 11-year sunspot cycle using real SILSO sunspot-number data.
  3. Compare Solar Cycle 24 and Solar Cycle 25’s real published maximum sunspot numbers.

Why This Matters

Lecture 02 identified the magnetic field as the missing agent behind coronal heating; this lecture derives where that field comes from and how its large-scale behavior (the 11-year cycle) is tracked using the longest continuously maintained series of astronomical measurements, sunspot counts, setting up Lecture 04’s detailed sunspot physics.

Opening Phenomenon

Solar Cycle 25, which began around December 2019, reached a smoothed maximum monthly sunspot number of 160.8 in October 2024 (not-smoothed monthly peak 216 in August 2024) -- moderately stronger than the preceding Solar Cycle 24’s smoothed maximum of 116.4, both real, tracked values from the SILSO World Data Center sunspot-number catalogue (WDC-SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium).

Evidence We Need to Explain

  • The Sun rotates differentially: its equator (period ~25 days) rotates faster than its poles (period ~35 days), a real, directly observed effect (from sunspot tracking and, more precisely, helioseismology) that provides the shearing motion the dynamo mechanism requires.
  • Sunspot counts have been maintained continuously since the mid-18th century (numbered "solar cycles," with Solar Cycle 25 being the 25th since systematic numbering began), making the sunspot-number record one of the longest continuous quantitative datasets in all of science.
  • A "butterfly diagram" (sunspot latitude plotted against time) shows new-cycle sunspots first emerging at mid-latitudes (~30 degrees) and progressively emerging closer to the equator as each cycle matures -- a real, well-documented pattern first noted by Maunder in 1904.

Derivation and Model

  • The Babcock-Leighton dynamo model explains the cyclic magnetic behavior qualitatively as follows: differential rotation stretches and winds up a large-scale poloidal (roughly north-south, dipole-like) field into a much stronger, wound-up toroidal (east-west) field over time; buoyant magnetic flux tubes from this toroidal field rise to the surface, forming sunspot pairs (Lecture 04), and the decay/diffusion of these sunspot pairs’ magnetic flux gradually regenerates a new poloidal field of opposite polarity, restarting the cycle roughly every 11 years (22 years for a full magnetic-polarity cycle).
  • This dynamo process operates in and around the base of the convective zone (the boundary Lecture 01 placed at r=0.71 R_sun), where the shear between the differentially rotating convective envelope and the more rigidly rotating radiative zone below is strongest -- directly connecting this lecture’s dynamo mechanism to Lecture 01’s interior structure.
  • The sunspot number (a standardized count combining the number of individual spots and sunspot groups, following the Wolf/international sunspot-number convention used by SILSO) is an imperfect but robust and consistently defined proxy for the overall strength of the Sun’s magnetic activity at any given time.

Quantitative Tool

\[ \text{Poloidal field} \xrightarrow{\text{differential rotation (shear)}} \text{Toroidal field} \xrightarrow{\text{buoyant rise, Babcock-Leighton decay}} \text{New poloidal field (reversed)} \]

Worked Example

  1. Comparing the two most recent cycles’ real published smoothed maxima: Solar Cycle 25’s 160.8 is 1.38 times Solar Cycle 24’s 116.4 -- a moderately stronger cycle, consistent with Solar Cycle 25 substantially exceeding most pre-cycle predictions (many of which anticipated a cycle comparable to or weaker than Cycle 24).
  2. Solar Cycle 25 began from a smoothed minimum sunspot number of only 1.8 (essentially spot-free) around December 2019, illustrating the enormous dynamic range (a factor of 89) between solar minimum and maximum conditions that this course’s later space-weather units must account for.

Common Misconception

Treating the 11-year sunspot cycle as strictly periodic or fully predictable. Real cycles vary substantially in amplitude and length (compare Cycle 24’s comparatively weak maximum to Cycle 25’s stronger, and largely under-predicted, maximum), and the dynamo mechanism, while capturing the essential physics, does not yet allow precise, first-principles prediction of a cycle’s exact strength in advance -- a genuine, disclosed limitation of current solar-cycle science.

Synthesis

The Babcock-Leighton dynamo, powered by the Sun’s differential rotation, produces the 11-year sunspot cycle tracked in the world’s longest continuous quantitative astronomical record -- and the individual, buoyantly emerged flux tubes responsible for that record are exactly the sunspots Lecture 04 now examines in detail.
