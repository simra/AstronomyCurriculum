Lecture 12: The Sun-Earth Connection II: Geomagnetic Storms and the Dst Index

ASTR 370 Solar Physics and Space Weather

Context and Why This Matters

Lecture 11 derived how far the solar wind compresses Earth’s magnetopause; this lecture derives the complementary, and more physically fundamental, measure of geomagnetic-storm severity used throughout space-weather science: the Dst index, and the ring-current energy it encodes.

Learning Goals

  1. Explain the physical origin of the magnetospheric ring current and its relationship to the Dst index.
  2. Derive and apply the Dessler-Parker-Sckopke relation between Dst and total ring-current energy.
  3. Compare the real 6 September 2017 storm’s Dst to the Carrington Event’s live-verified estimated Dst range.

Opening Phenomenon

The real, published minimum Dst index for the storm following this course’s 6 September 2017 case-study CME was -142 nT (a G4, "severe" storm) -- substantially smaller in magnitude than either published estimate for the Carrington Event of 1-2 September 1859 (Dst estimated between -800 and -1750 nT, live-verified this session), the most intense geomagnetic storm in the era of ground-based magnetometer records.

Evidence

  • During a geomagnetic storm, energetic ions and electrons trapped in Earth’s inner magnetosphere are injected and energized (ultimately drawing energy from the solar wind via magnetic reconnection at the magnetopause and in the magnetotail), forming an enhanced "ring current" that circles Earth westward at a few Earth radii’ distance.
  • This ring current’s own magnetic field opposes (partially cancels) Earth’s main dipole field at the equator, producing the negative magnetic-field depression measured by a global network of low-latitude magnetometer stations and combined into the standard Dst (Disturbance storm time) index, continuously available since 1957 (and reconstructed further back using historical magnetometer records, as for the Carrington Event).
  • The Carrington Event’s Dst is necessarily an estimate rather than a direct index value, since the standardized Dst index did not yet exist in 1859; Tsurutani et al. (2003) and the more conservative Cliver & Svalgaard (2005) estimate arrived at somewhat different values from the same underlying historical magnetometer records, both live-verified via this course’s Wikipedia fetch this session, illustrating genuine, disclosed scientific uncertainty in reconstructing a pre-instrumental event.

Derivation and Model

  • The Dessler-Parker-Sckopke (DPS) relation is a standard textbook result (Baumjohann & Treumann, Basic Space Plasma Physics) connecting the fractional depression of Earth’s equatorial magnetic field to the ring current’s total kinetic energy: Dst*/B0 = -(2/3)(E_ring/E0), where E0 = B0^2 R_E^3/(6 mu0) is a fixed reference energy (the dipole field’s own magnetic energy outside Earth’s surface, down to R_E), giving E_ring = -(3/2)(Dst/B0) E0.
  • This relation directly connects an easily measured, purely magnetic quantity (Dst, in nanotesla) to the physically fundamental quantity of interest (the total kinetic energy stored in the ring current’s trapped particle population), without requiring detailed knowledge of the ring current’s particle-by-particle composition or spatial distribution.
  • Because Dst enters this relation linearly (for a fixed B0 and E0), a storm with twice the Dst magnitude corresponds to twice the ring-current energy -- a simple, direct proportionality this lecture’s worked example uses to compare the real 2017 storm to the much larger historical Carrington estimates.

Working Equation

\[ \frac{Dst^*}{B_0} = -\frac{2}{3}\frac{E_{\rm ring}}{E_0}, \qquad E_0 = \frac{B_0^2 R_E^3}{6\mu_0}, \qquad E_{\rm ring} = -\frac{3}{2}\frac{Dst}{B_0}E_0 \]

Worked Example

  1. Earth’s reference dipole energy is E0=3.087e+16 J (a fixed geophysical constant, computed once from B0=30000 nT and R_E=6,371 km).
  2. Applying the DPS relation to the real 6 September 2017 storm’s published Dst_min=-142 nT gives a ring-current energy of E_ring=2.19e+14 J.
  3. Applying the same relation to the Carrington Event’s live-verified estimated Dst range gives E_ring between 1.23e+15 J (Cliver & Svalgaard 2005’s more conservative -800 nT estimate) and 2.70e+15 J (Tsurutani et al. 2003’s larger -1750 nT estimate) -- a factor of 12 to 6 more ring-current energy than the real, well-documented 2017 storm, honestly presented as a range reflecting genuine, disclosed scientific uncertainty in the historical reconstruction rather than a single precise number.

Common Misconception

Treating the Carrington Event’s Dst value as a directly measured historical quantity with the same certainty as the 2017 storm’s Dst. The 1859 event predates the standardized Dst index by nearly a century, so its "Dst" is a modern reconstruction from historical magnetometer traces, with two independent published estimates differing by more than a factor of two -- this course states both values and their factor-of-two-plus disagreement explicitly, rather than picking one number and presenting it as certain.

Active-Learning Guidance

Using the DPS relation’s linear dependence on Dst, explain why a storm with a Dst of -500 nT has exactly ten times the ring-current energy of a storm with a Dst of -50 nT, and connect this to why NOAA’s G-scale storm classification (G1 through G5, Unit 7) is itself roughly logarithmic in physical severity even though Dst is linear in energy.
