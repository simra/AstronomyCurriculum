# ASTR 320: Planetary Science

3-credit, 300-level, one-semester course. Year 3, Fall, in the four-year curriculum. Prerequisites: ASTR 120; CHEM 102; PHYS 172.

See [syllabus.html](syllabus.html) for the full syllabus, [schedule.html](schedule.html) for the lecture/lab/problem-set schedule, and [index.html](index.html) for the full course package landing page.

## Structure

- `lectures/` - 14 lecture slide decks and paired lecture notes.
- `labs/` - 7 labs (biweekly, matched 1:1 with problem sets).
- `problem-sets/` - 7 problem sets, each with a solution key and assessment instructions.
- `src/` - generator scripts that compute every worked example programmatically and produce the HTML lecture/lab/problem-set content.
- `data/` - course datasets (see `data/README.md`).
- `reference-log.md` - reference, dataset, and visual verification log.
- `review-report.md` - review status and history.

## What This Course Covers

Comparative planetology across the solar system and beyond: the solar nebula and condensation sequence, planetary differentiation and interior structure, heat budgets and convection, comparative surface geology and impact cratering, atmospheric hydrostatic structure and radiative equilibrium, atmospheric escape and climate evolution, planetary magnetic fields and dynamo theory, giant-planet interiors, rings and the Roche limit, tidally heated icy satellites, and exoplanet detection and characterization. Every physical tool used here (density/gravity/escape velocity, the radiative-equilibrium frost-line and equilibrium-temperature derivations, the moment-of-inertia interior-structure formalism, the hydrostatic scale-height derivation, the Jeans-escape parameter, the magnetopause pressure-balance derivation, the Roche limit, and tidal acceleration/heating) is derived from first principles and applied to real solar-system data and, in the capstone unit (Lecture 14, Lab 07, Problem Set 07), to the real, live-verified TRAPPIST-1 exoplanet system.
