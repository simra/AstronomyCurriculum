Optical Depth Attenuation and the Boltzmann Equation for Vega

Worked Solutions

1. Transmitted fraction

I(τ)/I(0) = e⁻⁵·⁵ = 0.0111, so about 1.1% of the incident intensity is transmitted and about 98.9% is absorbed or scattered away.

2. Inverted optical depth

0.10 = e⁻τ ⇒ τ = −ln(0.10) = 2.303. Since 2.30 > 4.5, this wavelength has higher opacity (more absorbing/scattering material along the line of sight) than the τ = 4.5 wavelength in Problem 1.

3. Boltzmann ratio for Vega

n2/n1 (Vega, 9602 K) = 1.780e-05; n2/n1 (Sun, 5778 K) = 5.113e-09. Ratio = 3481.6, i.e., Vega's n=2 population fraction is about 3482 times the Sun's.

4. Physical interpretation

The much larger n2/n1 ratio at Vega's temperature directly predicts much stronger Balmer absorption, consistent with Vega's observed strong Balmer lines. Correctly predicting the further decline in Balmer strength above roughly 10,000 K requires the Saha equation (Lecture 04), which accounts for hydrogen ionization removing the bound electron needed for a Balmer transition.
