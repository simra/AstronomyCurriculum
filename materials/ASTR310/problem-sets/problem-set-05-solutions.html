2. Europa’s Roche limit

3. Europa’s tidal acceleration

Europa's surface gravity: g = GM_Europa/R_Europa² = 1.315 m/s². Δa(Europa) = 1.308e-03 m/s² = 0.10% of Europa's own surface gravity. Io's corresponding fraction (Lecture 10/Lab 05) is 0.34% of Io's own surface gravity. Io experiences the stronger relative tidal stress, because Io orbits much closer to Jupiter and tidal acceleration falls off as 1/d³, more steeply than the modest difference in the two moons' surface gravities can offset.

4. Physical interpretation

Because tidal acceleration falls steeply (as 1/d³) with orbital distance and Europa orbits substantially farther from Jupiter than Io, Europa's tidal flexing and resulting heating is weaker than Io's, consistent with Europa retaining an icy crust (implying much less surface heat flow than Io's continuous resurfacing by active volcanism) while still plausibly sustaining a subsurface ocean from a smaller but non-negligible tidal heat source.
