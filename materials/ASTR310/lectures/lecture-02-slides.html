ASTR 310 · Lecture 02

The Blackbody Radiation Law: From Planck’s Function to Stefan-Boltzmann and Wien

Deriving, not just applying, the law that makes stellar astrophysics quantitative

Learning Goals

  1. State the Planck radiation law and describe, without full derivation, the physical assumptions (quantized oscillator energies) that produce it.
  2. Derive Wien’s displacement law and the Stefan-Boltzmann law as integrals/limits of the Planck function.
  3. Apply both laws together to a real star and cross-check the result against an independently measured luminosity.

Reading anchor: OpenStax Astronomy 2e, Chapter 5.4 (the electromagnetic spectrum, blackbody radiation); this course derives σ and b from the Planck function rather than presenting them as given constants.

Why This Matters

ASTR 230 used L = 4πR²σT⁴ and Wien’s law as given tools. Max Planck derived the underlying spectral law in 1900 by proposing that oscillators in a cavity wall could only exchange energy in discrete quanta hf — a proposal that resolved the "ultraviolet catastrophe" of classical (Rayleigh-Jeans) theory and opened the door to quantum mechanics. Seeing where Stefan-Boltzmann and Wien actually come from is what distinguishes an astrophysics course from an applied-formulas course.

Opening Phenomenon

Classical (Rayleigh-Jeans) theory predicts that a blackbody’s emitted intensity should grow without bound at short wavelengths, a divergence dubbed the ultraviolet catastrophe. Real blackbodies, including the Sun (T_eff = 5778 K), instead show intensity rising, peaking near a characteristic wavelength, and falling off exponentially at short wavelengths, exactly as the Planck function predicts and the classical theory does not.

First question: what here is directly observed, and what follows only once a physical law is derived and applied?

Evidence We Need to Explain

  • Every star’s continuous spectrum closely follows a Planck curve of some characteristic temperature, regardless of the star’s size, composition, or distance, exactly as expected if the emergent radiation is set by the emitting surface’s temperature alone.
  • The Planck function’s peak wavelength shifts to shorter wavelengths for hotter blackbodies in exact proportion to 1/T (Wien’s law), verified in laboratory blackbody sources across many decades of temperature.
  • The total area under the Planck function (integrated over all wavelengths) grows as T⁴, verified directly for the Sun by comparing the Stefan-Boltzmann prediction from its measured radius and temperature to its independently measured total luminosity.

Derivation and Model

  • The Planck function B_ν(T) = (2hν³/c²) × 1/(e^{hν/kT} - 1) follows from assuming that the electromagnetic field modes in a cavity in thermal equilibrium can only carry energy in discrete quanta of size hν, with the mean occupation number of each mode given by Bose-Einstein statistics.
  • Differentiating B_λ(T) with respect to λ and setting the result to zero gives the wavelength of maximum emission, λ_max T = b (Wien’s displacement law), with b = hc/(4.965k) a fixed constant — the "4.965" is the numerical root of a transcendental equation from the derivative, not an arbitrary fit parameter.
  • Integrating B_ν(T) over all frequencies and over the outward hemisphere gives the total emitted power per unit area, σT⁴ (the Stefan-Boltzmann law), with σ = 2π⁵k⁴/(15h³c²) expressible entirely in terms of more fundamental constants (h, k, c) rather than being an independent empirical constant.

Quantitative Tool

\[ B_\nu(T) = \dfrac{2h\nu^{3}}{c^{2}}\dfrac{1}{e^{h\nu/kT}-1}, \qquad \lambda_{\max}T = b, \qquad L = 4\pi R^{2}\sigma T^{4} \]

Worked Example

  1. Wien’s law for the Sun: λ_max = b/T = 2.8971e-03 m·K / 5778 K = 501 nm, in the visible-green part of the spectrum — consistent with human vision having evolved to be most sensitive near this wavelength.
  2. Wien’s law for Alpha Centauri A (T_eff = 5748 K, live-verified this session): λ_max = 504 nm, close to the Sun’s peak, consistent with its near-solar G2V spectral type.
  3. Stefan-Boltzmann applied to Alpha Centauri A: R = 1.223 R☉ = 8.508e+08 m, T = 5748 K, giving L = 4πR²σT⁴ = 5.631e+26 W = 1.471 L☉.
  4. The independently measured (interferometric + asteroseismic) luminosity of Alpha Centauri A is 1.5059 L☉; the Stefan-Boltzmann prediction from its measured radius and temperature agrees to within 2.3%, an independent cross-check of the same law verified for the Sun in ASTR 230.

Common Misconception

Treating σ in the Stefan-Boltzmann law as an arbitrary empirical fitting constant. It is fully determined by h, k, and c through the Planck-function integral; the same is true of Wien’s constant b. Neither law is an independent postulate once the Planck function is accepted.

Synthesis

The Stefan-Boltzmann and Wien laws are not independent empirical rules but specific limits and integrals of the single underlying Planck function, itself a consequence of quantized field-mode energies.
