Lecture 07: Gravitational Potentials: The Shell Theorem, Potential Energy, and Escape Velocity

ASTR 310 Astrophysics I: Radiation, Matter, and Orbits

Context and Why This Matters

Every orbital-mechanics result in the rest of this course (Kepler’s laws, binary-star mass determination, tidal effects, orbital energy) rests on two facts established here: that a spherical mass acts gravitationally, from outside its surface, exactly like a point mass at its center, and that gravitational potential energy provides a clean energy-conservation route to escape velocity and orbital speed.

Learning Goals

  1. State and justify the shell theorem for the gravitational field of a spherically symmetric mass distribution.
  2. Derive the gravitational potential energy of a two-body system and the escape-velocity formula from energy conservation.
  3. Compare escape velocities from very different real objects to build physical intuition for compact-object gravity.

Opening Phenomenon

Sirius B, a white dwarf with about 1.02 times the Sun’s mass compressed into roughly Earth’s radius (from ASTR 230), has a surface escape velocity of 6926 km/s — more than 619 times Earth’s escape velocity, despite Sirius B’s mass being only about 339049 times larger than Earth’s.

Vocabulary

  • shell theorem
  • gravitational potential energy
  • escape velocity
  • specific orbital energy
  • gravitational binding energy
  • point-mass equivalence

Evidence

  • Spacecraft trajectories computed by treating Earth, the Sun, and other planets as point masses located at their centers match tracked spacecraft positions to extraordinary precision, confirming the shell theorem’s prediction for the external field of a (nearly) spherical body.
  • Objects launched from Earth at speeds below about 11.2 km/s fall back or remain in orbit, while objects at or above that speed escape Earth’s gravity entirely (ignoring atmospheric drag), exactly matching the escape-velocity formula derived from energy conservation.
  • Compact objects (white dwarfs, neutron stars) show escape velocities dramatically higher than main-sequence stars of similar mass, consistent with escape velocity’s 1/√R dependence on radius at fixed mass.

Derivation and Model

  • The shell theorem states that a spherically symmetric shell of mass exerts zero net gravitational field on any point mass inside it, and exerts the same field outside itself as if all its mass were concentrated at its center; integrating over concentric shells extends this to any spherically symmetric mass distribution.
  • The gravitational potential energy of two point masses (or spherical bodies) separated by distance r is U(r) = -GMm/r, defined so that U → 0 as r → ∞; because U is negative and grows toward zero with increasing separation, it takes positive energy input to increase r.
  • Escape velocity is the minimum launch speed for which total mechanical energy (1/2)mv² + U(r) is exactly zero, i.e., just enough kinetic energy to reach r → ∞ with zero speed remaining; solving this for v gives v_esc = √(2GM/R).

Working Equation

\[ U(r) = -\dfrac{GMm}{r}, \qquad \tfrac{1}{2}mv_{\rm esc}^{2} + U(R) = 0 \implies v_{\rm esc} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}} \]

Worked Example

  1. Earth: M = 5.972e+24 kg, R = 6.371e+06 m, v_esc = √(2GM/R) = 11.19 km/s.
  2. The Sun: M = 1.989e+30 kg, R = 6.957e+08 m, v_esc = 617.8 km/s — about 55 times Earth’s escape velocity, driven by the Sun’s much larger mass despite its also much larger radius.
  3. Sirius B (white dwarf, from ASTR 230, provenance already established there): M = 1.018 M☉, R = 0.008098 R☉, v_esc = 6926 km/s, illustrating how escape velocity is far more sensitive to an object’s radius (through 1/√R) than to its mass, when comparing objects of similar mass but wildly different compactness.

Common Misconception

Assuming escape velocity means "the speed needed to permanently leave the object’s gravitational influence entirely" in some absolute sense. Gravity from any mass technically extends to infinite distance (falling only as 1/r²); escape velocity is instead the minimum speed to reach arbitrarily large distance with zero kinetic energy remaining, in the idealized two-body problem with no other masses present.

Active-Learning Guidance

Using v_esc = √(2GM/R), predict qualitatively what happens to the escape velocity of a fixed-mass object if it is compressed to one-tenth its original radius, and connect this to why compact objects (white dwarfs, neutron stars, black holes) have such extreme escape velocities.
