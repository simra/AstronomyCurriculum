Galactic Astronomy

Objectives and route

Why this question is hard

A color-magnitude diagram is a time-integrated record of star formation, enrichment, and selection.

Galactic astronomy observes projected light, velocities, and abundances from one moving vantage point. The inverse problem is underdetermined until geometry, selection, and a physical model are made explicit.

Terms for This Lecture

Color-magnitude diagram

Definition: A plot of stellar luminosity or absolute magnitude against a color index that traces temperature.

Why it matters here: Its morphology encodes the age, composition, distance, and selection of a stellar population.

Relationship: Isochrones map stellar-evolution models onto the observed main sequence, turnoff, and giant branch.

Limitation or common confusion: A CMD is not a literal evolutionary track followed by one star.

Main-sequence turnoff

Definition: The CMD location where stars of a population begin exhausting core hydrogen and leave the main sequence.

Why it matters here: It is one of the most age-sensitive features available for resolved stellar populations.

Relationship: Turnoff luminosity is compared with isochrones at an assumed metallicity and distance.

Limitation or common confusion: Age, metallicity, binaries, and photometric errors can shift or broaden the turnoff.

Metallicity [Fe/H]

Definition: A logarithmic iron-to-hydrogen abundance relative to the Sun: \([\mathrm{Fe/H}]=\log_{10}(N_{\rm Fe}/N_{\rm H})-\log_{10}(N_{\rm Fe}/N_{\rm H})_\odot\).

Why it matters here: It distinguishes broad Galactic populations and records chemical enrichment.

Relationship: A value of -1.55 means about \(10^{-1.55}\) of the solar iron-to-hydrogen ratio.

Limitation or common confusion: It is not the fraction of all heavy elements and does not uniquely specify an age.

Isochrone

Definition: A model locus in a CMD for stars of one age and initial chemical composition but different masses.

Why it matters here: It links stellar-evolution theory to observed population ages and metallicities.

Relationship: Fitting an isochrone requires distance, extinction, abundance, and a treatment of binaries and completeness.

Limitation or common confusion: A visually good fit is not unique when age-metallicity-extinction degeneracies are present.

Selection function

Definition: The probability that an object with given properties enters the observed sample.

Why it matters here: Population fractions and CMD shapes cannot be interpreted without knowing which stars the survey could detect.

Relationship: It connects the underlying stellar distribution to the catalogue likelihood and completeness corrections.

Limitation or common confusion: The observed sample is not automatically representative of the Galactic population.

Physical tool

The central relation for this lecture is:

\([\mathrm{Fe/H}]=\log_{10}(N_{Fe}/N_H)-\log_{10}(N_{Fe}/N_H)_\odot\)

Define every symbol before substitution. State the coordinate system, units, and approximation. A formula is evidence only when its assumptions match the tracer and regime.

Worked calculation

[Fe/H]=-1.55 means 10^(-1.55) = 0.02818 times the solar iron-to-hydrogen ratio.

This is an abundance ratio, not a statement that the star has 3% of every element.

Interpret the pattern

  • The observable is not identical to the inferred quantity: distinguish measurement, calibration, and model.
  • A comparison across tracers is more informative than a single fitted parameter.
  • The result should be reported with its assumptions and an uncertainty or sensitivity statement.

Boundary condition and misconception

Do not overread the model: Age-metallicity degeneracy means one photometric color rarely identifies an age uniquely.

Ask whether changing the distance prior, selection function, or mass decomposition could move the conclusion. This is where a plausible diagram can become a misleading argument.

In-class reasoning

Prompt: Compare the iron abundance ratio for [Fe/H]=-1.55 with solar composition.
