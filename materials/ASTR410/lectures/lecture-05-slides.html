Lecture 05: The Rotation Curve and the Missing Mass

Galactic Astronomy

Rotation curves made the mass problem quantitative: outer disk speeds do not fall as a luminous point-mass model predicts.

Objectives and route

  • Derive enclosed mass from circular motion and identify the flat-curve anomaly.
  • Connect circular speed and mass to observations and model assumptions.
  • Make one auditable calculation and interpret its limitation.
  • Use an evidence chain rather than treating a visualization as a direct measurement.

Why this question is hard

Rotation curves made the mass problem quantitative: outer disk speeds do not fall as a luminous point-mass model predicts.

Galactic astronomy observes projected light, velocities, and abundances from one moving vantage point. The inverse problem is underdetermined until geometry, selection, and a physical model are made explicit.

Terms for This Lecture

Rotation curve

Definition: The circular speed \(v_c(R)\) inferred as a function of Galactocentric radius.

Why it matters here: Its outer shape is the central dynamical evidence for mass beyond the luminous disk.

Relationship: Under an idealized spherical interpretation, \(M(\lt R)=Rv_c^2/G\).

Limitation or common confusion: A rotation curve is inferred from tracers and geometry; it is not a direct plot of all stellar orbital speeds.

Circular speed

Definition: The speed required for a circular orbit in a specified gravitational potential at a given radius.

Why it matters here: It links the measured kinematics to the radial gravitational force.

Relationship: It satisfies \(v_c^2=R\,\partial\Phi/\partial R\) in an axisymmetric mid-plane model.

Limitation or common confusion: It differs from the mean azimuthal speed of a population with random motion.

Enclosed mass

Definition: The mass inside radius \(R\) that produces the inferred gravitational acceleration under a stated geometry.

Why it matters here: It converts a flat outer rotation curve into a growing dynamical mass estimate.

Relationship: For spherical symmetry, \(M(\lt R)=Rv_c^2/G\), so constant \(v_c\) implies \(M\propto R\).

Limitation or common confusion: The simple formula is not exact for a flattened disk and does not separate baryons from dark matter.

Baryonic mass

Definition: Mass in ordinary matter, chiefly stars and gas, that emits, absorbs, or otherwise interacts electromagnetically.

Why it matters here: Its predicted gravitational contribution can be compared with the observed rotation curve.

Relationship: Stellar mass-to-light ratios and gas maps build the baryonic rotation model.

Limitation or common confusion: Baryonic mass is not identical to visible light because faint stars and cold gas also contribute.

Dark matter

Definition: A non-luminous mass component inferred from gravity whose distribution extends beyond the stellar disk.

Why it matters here: It provides the standard explanation for flat outer rotation curves and multiple independent dynamical probes.

Relationship: A halo contribution is added in quadrature to bulge, disk, and gas contributions to \(v_c^2\).

Limitation or common confusion: A mass discrepancy establishes missing gravity under the model, not a direct image or unique particle identity.

Physical tool

The central relation for this lecture is:

\(M(\lt R)=Rv_c^2/G\)

Worked calculation

M(<8.2 kpc)=Rv^2/G = 1.0262e+11 solar masses.

This is an enclosed dynamical mass; it combines stars, gas, and dark matter.

Write the units beside each intermediate quantity. The numerical result is generated from the shared course constants and recomputed independently during review.

Interpret the pattern

  • The observable is not identical to the inferred quantity: distinguish measurement, calibration, and model.
  • A comparison across tracers is more informative than a single fitted parameter.
  • The result should be reported with its assumptions and an uncertainty or sensitivity statement.

Boundary condition and misconception

Do not overread the model: The inference is mass within radius, not a direct photograph of dark matter.

Ask whether changing the distance prior, selection function, or mass decomposition could move the conclusion. This is where a plausible diagram can become a misleading argument.

In-class reasoning

Prompt: Use R=8.2 kpc and v_c=232 km s^-1 to estimate the enclosed mass.
