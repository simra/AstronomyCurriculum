Galactic Astronomy

A galaxy is not only a present-day equilibrium: disrupted satellites and chemically tagged stars preserve its assembly record.

Why this question is hard

A galaxy is not only a present-day equilibrium: disrupted satellites and chemically tagged stars preserve its assembly record.

Galactic astronomy observes projected light, velocities, and abundances from one moving vantage point. The inverse problem is underdetermined until geometry, selection, and a physical model are made explicit.

Terms for This Lecture

Stellar stream

Definition: A coherent, elongated structure of stars stripped from a cluster or dwarf galaxy along its orbit.

Why it matters here: Streams preserve phase-space evidence of accretion and probe the Galactic potential.

Relationship: Their track, distance, velocities, and width are compared with orbit or disruption models.

Limitation or common confusion: A stream is not exactly one orbit because stripping occurs over time and stars have finite dispersion.

Accretion event

Definition: The incorporation and tidal disruption of a smaller stellar system by the Milky Way.

Why it matters here: It contributes stars, clusters, and dark matter while leaving kinematic and chemical substructure.

Relationship: Common integrals of motion and abundance patterns can associate debris with a progenitor.

Limitation or common confusion: A named debris structure may combine multiple events or overlap in projected phase space.

Chemical tagging

Definition: The probabilistic association of stars through multidimensional elemental-abundance patterns.

Why it matters here: It can connect dispersed stars to shared formation environments after spatial coherence is lost.

Relationship: Abundance vectors complement ages and orbital information in archaeology.

Limitation or common confusion: Similar chemistry is not a unique birth certificate because enrichment pathways overlap.

Radial migration

Definition: A lasting change in a disk star's guiding-center radius, often driven by resonant interactions.

Why it matters here: It mixes stars born at different radii and blurs present-day chemical gradients.

Relationship: Migration connects spiral or bar resonances to the age-metallicity-radius distribution.

Limitation or common confusion: It differs from epicyclic blurring, which changes instantaneous radius without shifting the guiding center.

Lookback time

Definition: The elapsed time between emitted light or a past formation event and the present epoch.

Why it matters here: It places stellar ages and assembly episodes on a common temporal axis.

Relationship: For a formation time \(t_{\rm formation}\), \(t_{\rm lookback}=t_0-t_{\rm formation}\).

Limitation or common confusion: For Galactic stars it is usually an age inference, not a cosmological light-travel measurement.

Physical tool

The central relation for this lecture is:

\(t_{\rm lookback}=t_0-t_{\rm formation}\)

Interpret the pattern

  • The observable is not identical to the inferred quantity: distinguish measurement, calibration, and model.
  • A comparison across tracers is more informative than a single fitted parameter.
  • The result should be reported with its assumptions and an uncertainty or sensitivity statement.

Boundary condition and misconception

Do not overread the model: Chemical tagging is probabilistic; migration and selection can blur the original birth environment.

Ask whether changing the distance prior, selection function, or mass decomposition could move the conclusion. This is where a plausible diagram can become a misleading argument.
