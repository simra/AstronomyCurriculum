Lecture 12: Inference with Galactic Surveys

Galactic Astronomy

Survey astronomy is an inverse problem: the catalogue is filtered by the instrument and by the scientist's cuts.

Objectives and route

  • Build a reproducible model-data comparison and quantify uncertainty.
  • Connect likelihoods, priors, and selection functions to observations and model assumptions.
  • Make one auditable calculation and interpret its limitation.
  • Use an evidence chain rather than treating a visualization as a direct measurement.

Why this question is hard

Survey astronomy is an inverse problem: the catalogue is filtered by the instrument and by the scientist's cuts.

Galactic astronomy observes projected light, velocities, and abundances from one moving vantage point. The inverse problem is underdetermined until geometry, selection, and a physical model are made explicit.

Terms for This Lecture

Likelihood

Definition: A model for the probability of the observed data as a function of parameters, \(p(D|\theta)\).

Why it matters here: It encodes the measurement uncertainties used to compare Galactic models with survey data.

Relationship: For independent Gaussian rotation points it is related to a chi-square residual sum.

Limitation or common confusion: A convenient likelihood can be precise yet wrong if errors are correlated or non-Gaussian.

Prior

Definition: A probability distribution for parameters before conditioning on the current data.

Why it matters here: It regularizes underconstrained distance and Galactic-structure inferences and makes assumptions explicit.

Relationship: Bayes' theorem combines it with the likelihood to form \(p(\theta|D)\).

Limitation or common confusion: A prior is not an additional observation, and an apparently weak prior can matter with sparse data.

Posterior

Definition: The probability distribution of model parameters after combining likelihood and prior.

Why it matters here: It summarizes parameter estimates, uncertainty, and covariance for the adopted model.

Relationship: Posterior predictive checks connect inferred parameters back to observable catalogues.

Limitation or common confusion: A narrow posterior does not protect against a misspecified model or selection function.

Selection function

Definition: The probability that an astronomical source is detected and retained as a function of its properties.

Why it matters here: It is part of the data-generating process and must enter population inference.

Relationship: The predicted underlying population is multiplied by selection before comparison with a catalogue.

Limitation or common confusion: Cuts applied by the analyst are part of the selection, not harmless bookkeeping.

Posterior predictive check

Definition: A comparison between data simulated from the fitted posterior model and the actual observations.

Why it matters here: It tests whether the model reproduces features beyond the fitted parameter summary.

Relationship: Residual distributions, trends, and replicated catalogues reveal model-data mismatch.

Limitation or common confusion: Passing selected checks does not prove the model unique or physically true.

Physical tool

The central relation for this lecture is:

\(p(\theta|D)\propto p(D|\theta)p(\theta)\)

Define every symbol before substitution. State the coordinate system, units, and approximation. A formula is evidence only when its assumptions match the tracer and regime.

Interpret the pattern

  • The observable is not identical to the inferred quantity: distinguish measurement, calibration, and model.
  • A comparison across tracers is more informative than a single fitted parameter.
  • The result should be reported with its assumptions and an uncertainty or sensitivity statement.

Boundary condition and misconception

Do not overread the model: A precise posterior can still be wrong when the selection function or likelihood is misspecified.

Ask whether changing the distance prior, selection function, or mass decomposition could move the conclusion. This is where a plausible diagram can become a misleading argument.

Takeaways

  • Build a reproducible model-data comparison and quantify uncertainty.
  • Use \(p(\theta|D)\propto p(D|\theta)p(\theta)\) only in its stated regime.
  • Separate real data, published values, and instructor-provided illustrative values.
  • Report both the result and the limitation that controls its credibility.
