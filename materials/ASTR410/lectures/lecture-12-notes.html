ASTR 410 Lecture 12: Inference with Galactic Surveys

Galactic Astronomy · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Build a reproducible model-data comparison and quantify uncertainty.
  • Explain the role of likelihoods, priors, and selection functions.
  • Audit an inference from observable to physical conclusion.

Lecture arc

Survey astronomy is an inverse problem: the catalogue is filtered by the instrument and by the scientist's cuts. Start by asking students what the telescope actually measures. Then introduce likelihoods, priors, and selection functions; delay the equation until the geometry and assumptions are visible. Use the visual as an argument: students should identify what is data, what is a model, and what is inferred.

The lecture should return to the one-dimensional worked calculation and ask whether the answer is stable under a plausible change in distance, tracer selection, or model family.

Derivation and interpretation

\(p(\theta|D)\propto p(D|\theta)p(\theta)\)

Define the symbols and units aloud. The relation is useful because it turns an observable into a dynamical or population constraint, but it is conditional on the stated approximation. In discussion, distinguish a parameter that is directly measured from one that is inferred after adopting a model.

Worked example

A flat 232 km s^-1 model has chi^2 = 24.891 for the seven listed points when each uncertainty is 8 km s^-1.

A chi-square comparison needs a stated number of fitted parameters and a justified error model. Have students estimate the order of magnitude before revealing the computed value. Require units and one sentence of physical interpretation. This example is generated by the course source and can be regenerated if a shared constant changes.

Misconceptions and evidence

A precise posterior can still be wrong when the selection function or likelihood is misspecified. A map or fitted curve is not a direct inventory. Ask students to list a calibration, selection, or projection effect that could mimic the displayed trend. Then connect the answer to the paired data activity.

Study guidance

Students should be able to reproduce the calculation, explain the approximation, and compare the result with at least one independent tracer.
