ASTR 410 Lecture 04: Stellar Motions and the Galactic Phase Space

Galactic Astronomy · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Interpret proper motions, radial velocities, and velocity ellipsoids.
  • Explain the role of 6D phase space and asymmetric drift.
  • Audit an inference from observable to physical conclusion.

Lecture arc

The disk is not a rigid carousel: random motions encode heating, birth conditions, and resonances. Start by asking students what the telescope actually measures. Then introduce 6D phase space and asymmetric drift; delay the equation until the geometry and assumptions are visible. Use the visual as an argument: students should identify what is data, what is a model, and what is inferred.

The lecture should return to the one-dimensional worked calculation and ask whether the answer is stable under a plausible change in distance, tracer selection, or model family.

Derivation and interpretation

\(v_t=4.74047\,\mu d\), \quad \sigma_R>\sigma_\phi>\sigma_z\)

Define the symbols and units aloud. The relation is useful because it turns an observable into a dynamical or population constraint, but it is conditional on the stated approximation. In discussion, distinguish a parameter that is directly measured from one that is inferred after adopting a model.

Worked example

For mu=1.8 mas yr^-1 and d=2.4 kpc, v_t = 20.479 km s^-1.

The conversion assumes the proper motion is calibrated and the distance is the adopted estimate. Have students estimate the order of magnitude before revealing the computed value. Require units and one sentence of physical interpretation. This example is generated by the course source and can be regenerated if a shared constant changes.

Misconceptions and evidence

A large proper motion can mean proximity rather than extreme space velocity. A map or fitted curve is not a direct inventory. Ask students to list a calibration, selection, or projection effect that could mimic the displayed trend. Then connect the answer to the paired data activity.

Study guidance

Students should be able to reproduce the calculation, explain the approximation, and compare the result with at least one independent tracer.
