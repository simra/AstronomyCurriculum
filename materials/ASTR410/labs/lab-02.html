ASTR 410 Lab 02: Stellar Populations as Fossils of Formation and Stellar Motions and the Galactic Phase Space

Lectures 03–04 · 3-credit Year 4 laboratory/data-analysis activity

Objectives

  • Apply the stellar populations as fossils of formation and stellar motions and the galactic phase space toolkit to a traceable dataset.
  • Quantify uncertainty or model sensitivity rather than reporting a bare fit.
  • Communicate a result with a figure, units, and provenance.

Preparation and materials

Use Python 3, NumPy, Matplotlib, Astropy, and the supplied CSV in data/. Read the paired lecture notes and inspect the CSV header before analysis. No live observing is required; the activity is reproducible on a laptop.

Dataset: published stellar-population abundance summaries in data/population-abundances.csv

Setup and procedure

  1. Copy the CSV into a working directory and record its filename, row count, columns, and units.
  2. Plot the raw observables before applying a model. Label axes and preserve the supplied uncertainties.
  3. Fit or transform the data using the equation from Lectures 03–04; show one intermediate calculation by hand and reproduce it in code.
  4. Repeat the result after a stated perturbation: remove one outer-radius point, vary the distance by 10%, or change the model family as appropriate.
  5. Write a short interpretation separating measured values, adopted literature values, and derived quantities.

Uncertainty and limitations

Use the tabulated uncertainty where available and propagate it through the stated formula. If the dataset lacks per-row errors, report sensitivity to a defensible perturbation and label the result model-dependent. Do not treat a standard published value as a new measurement.

Deliverables and assessment

  • Reproducible notebook or script with environment and input filename.
  • One labeled figure and one table of derived values.
  • 600–900 word interpretation with an uncertainty statement and provenance paragraph.
  • Short limitations section naming at least two threats to inference.

Grading: analysis correctness 35%, uncertainty/model critique 25%, reproducibility 20%, figure and scientific communication 20%. Revisions are encouraged under the course resubmission policy.
