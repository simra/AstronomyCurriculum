Lab 06: Planetary Surfaces and Small Bodies

Use image evidence to infer cratering, surface age, geological process, and habitability context.

Learning Objectives

  • Use image evidence to infer cratering, surface age, geological process, and habitability context.
  • Make measurements or classifications with explicit uncertainty.
  • Separate direct evidence from model-dependent interpretation.

Preparation

  • Review terrestrial planet and small-body lectures. Read relevant OpenStax solar-system chapters assigned by the instructor.

Materials and Data

  • Instructor-provided planetary surface images
  • Crater-count grid or digital annotation tool
  • Planetary image source notes

Apparatus and Setup

  • Provide two planetary surface images at comparable scale, plus one icy-moon or Mars image for habitability evidence.
  • Supply scale bars or state that only relative crater density may be inferred.
  • Give students a counting grid or digital annotation layer.

Procedure

  1. Inspect two planetary or moon surface images at comparable scale and resolution.
  2. Count craters in at least two equal-area regions and record obvious resurfacing features such as flows, tectonic cracks, smooth plains, or ejecta blankets.
  3. Classify each region as relatively older or younger and justify the classification.
  4. For one icy moon or Mars image, identify evidence relevant to past or present liquid environments.
  5. Record source, mission/instrument if supplied, scale, wavelength/color notes, and any processing caveats.

Analysis and Uncertainty

  • Explain the crater-count assumption and one way it can fail.
  • Distinguish surface observation from geological interpretation.
  • Rank the strength of habitability evidence as weak, moderate, or strong, and justify the ranking.

Deliverables

  • Annotated image or crater-count table
  • Relative-age interpretation
  • Habitability evidence paragraph
  • Source/provenance note

Assessment Criteria

  • Use of image evidence
  • Crater-count reasoning
  • Uncertainty and caveat handling
  • Source/provenance documentation

References, Software, and Data Provenance
