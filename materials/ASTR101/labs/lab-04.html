Lab 04: Spectra and Light

Interpret emission and absorption spectra and connect spectral features to composition and motion.

Learning Objectives

  • Interpret emission and absorption spectra and connect spectral features to composition and motion.
  • Make measurements or classifications with explicit uncertainty.
  • Separate direct evidence from model-dependent interpretation.

Preparation

  • Read OpenStax Astronomy 2e sections 5.1-5.6 on light, spectra, spectral lines, and Doppler shifts.

Materials and Data

  • Instructor-provided spectra or OpenStax spectral diagrams
  • Known rest wavelengths for hydrogen lines
  • Calculator or spreadsheet

Apparatus and Setup

  • Provide three labeled data files or printed spectra: continuous, absorption-line, and emission-line examples.
  • Provide a rest-wavelength table including H-alpha 656.3 nm, H-beta 486.1 nm, and at least one [O III] line.
  • If using software, lock axes so students read wavelengths rather than pixel positions.

Procedure

  1. Classify three spectra as continuous, absorption-line, or emission-line and justify each classification from visual evidence.
  2. Identify at least two spectral lines in a provided spectrum using a rest-wavelength table.
  3. For one shifted line, measure observed wavelength and compute radial velocity using v/c = Delta lambda / lambda.
  4. Compare two spectra of the same object type and identify what changes could be due to temperature, composition, or motion.
  5. Record whether each inference is direct observation or model-dependent interpretation.

Analysis and Uncertainty

  • Show wavelength shift calculation with units.
  • State whether the object is redshifted or blueshifted.
  • Explain one reason why calibration matters before interpreting line positions.

Deliverables

  • Annotated spectra
  • Line-identification table
  • Doppler calculation
  • Short interpretation paragraph

Assessment Criteria

  • Correct spectrum classification
  • Correct line identification
  • Dimensional calculation
  • Evidence/inference distinction

References, Software, and Data Provenance

  • OpenStax Fig. 5.21 Three Kinds of Spectra
