Lab 01: Observing Notebook and Angular Measurement

Build reproducible naked-eye observing habits and calibrate hand-angle estimates.

Learning Objectives

  • Build reproducible naked-eye observing habits and calibrate hand-angle estimates.
  • Make measurements or classifications with explicit uncertainty.
  • Separate direct evidence from model-dependent interpretation.

Preparation

  • Read OpenStax Astronomy 2e section 2.1 on the sky above and angular measure.
  • Bring a notebook or digital observing log, pencil, red-light-safe illumination if observing at night, and weather-appropriate clothing.

Materials and Data

  • Naked-eye observing site or simulated sky if weather prevents outdoor work
  • Printed or digital horizon diagram
  • Optional: Stellarium for post-observation verification

Apparatus and Setup

  • Prepare a horizon sheet with a 0-90 degree altitude scale and cardinal direction labels.
  • Use a compass or landmark map to establish north before observations begin.
  • If using a simulated sky, set location, date, and time before students enter measurements.

Procedure

  1. Record date, clock time, time zone, observing location, approximate latitude/longitude if known, weather, transparency, and horizon obstructions.
  2. Identify north, east, south, and west using landmarks, compass, phone compass, or instructor-provided site orientation. Note the method used.
  3. Calibrate your hand at arm's length: estimate the width of one finger, three fingers, fist, and thumb-to-little-finger span using the class reference values, then record your personal values.
  4. Choose three bright objects. Estimate each object's altitude and azimuth using hand angles and cardinal directions.
  5. Wait at least 20 minutes or use two observations separated by class time. Re-estimate one object's position and describe the apparent motion.
  6. Sketch a horizon diagram with at least three objects, cardinal directions, altitude estimates, and obstructions.

Analysis and Uncertainty

  • Compute the difference between two altitude estimates for the repeated object.
  • Estimate uncertainty for each hand-angle measurement, using at least +/- one finger width unless your repeated measurements justify a different value.
  • State which details would allow another observer to reproduce your observation.

Deliverables

  • One complete observing log entry
  • One labeled horizon diagram
  • A 150-250 word reflection distinguishing direct observation from interpretation

Assessment Criteria

  • Completeness of observing metadata
  • Reasonable angular estimates with uncertainty
  • Clear labeled diagram
  • Correct distinction between observation and model

References, Software, and Data Provenance
