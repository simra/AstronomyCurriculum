ASTR 101: Introduction to Astronomy with Observing Lab

Fall term · 4 credits · Lecture and integrated observing laboratory

Course Information

Prerequisites: None.

Catalog description: Introduces the night sky, astronomical scales, gravity, light, telescopes, the solar system, stars, galaxies, and cosmology. Laboratory work develops naked-eye observing, telescope use, sky coordinates, simple image capture, and scientific notebook practice.

Instructor: To be assigned.

Course role: First-year foundation for astronomy majors and a gateway into observational practice, quantitative reasoning, and scientific communication.

Learning Outcomes

  1. Use sky coordinates, angular measure, timekeeping, and seasonal geometry to describe and predict observable sky phenomena.
  2. Explain how gravity, light, matter, and telescopes connect observations to physical models of planets, stars, galaxies, and the universe.
  3. Apply order-of-magnitude reasoning, units, graph interpretation, and simple equations such as \(v = H_0 d\), \(F = L/(4\pi d^2)\), and Kepler's third law in introductory astronomical contexts.
  4. Plan and document basic naked-eye, binocular, or telescope observations with attention to uncertainty, conditions, calibration, and ethical observing practice.
  5. Interpret astronomical images, spectra, charts, and simple datasets while distinguishing observation, inference, model, and speculation.
  6. Communicate astronomical reasoning in clear prose, labeled figures, worked calculations, and concise lab reports.

Required and Recommended Resources

  • OpenStax, Astronomy 2e. Free online textbook.
  • Stellarium Web or Stellarium desktop for sky simulation and observing preparation.
  • NASA Solar System Exploration and NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database pages as guided reference resources.
  • Instructor-provided observing forms, star charts, datasets, and safety guidance.

Revision and resubmission policy: The course uses high grading standards, but revision is part of learning scientific practice. Eligible problem sets and lab reports may be revised and resubmitted after feedback. Revised work must include a short change note explaining what was corrected and why. Resubmissions can improve the grade when they demonstrate improved reasoning, clearer evidence, correct units, and stronger communication.

Course Policies

Collaboration

Students may discuss concepts, observing strategy, and general problem-solving approaches. Submitted calculations, prose, plots, code, and observing logs must be each student's own work unless a group artifact is explicitly assigned.

Academic Integrity and References

All external information, images, datasets, software, and AI-assisted drafting must be acknowledged according to instructor guidance. Fabricated references, unverifiable claims, copied work, or undocumented code are serious defects.

Observing Safety

Never point optical instruments at the Sun unless using instructor-approved solar observing equipment and procedures. Night observing must follow campus safety rules, weather guidance, and buddy-system expectations where applicable.

Accessibility

Students should contact the instructor and appropriate campus office early for disability accommodations, alternative observing arrangements, or accessible formats for visual materials.
