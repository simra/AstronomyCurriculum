ASTR 210 Schedule

WeekLectureFocus
1From Pretty Pictures to MeasurementsGood observations require calibration, metadata, and an explicit uncertainty model.
2Observing Planning and Error BudgetsPlanning is the first stage of data reduction and a major source of reliability.
3CCD Detectors, Bias, Dark, and Flat FieldsCalibration turns detector readout into a defensible measurement.
4Noise, Signal-to-Noise, and Detection LimitsDetection is a probability statement, not merely a visual impression.
5Aperture PhotometryPhotometry is as much a measurement-design problem as a calculation problem.
6Differential Photometry and Light CurvesDifferential photometry reveals relative variability by removing common-mode effects.
7Astrometry and Plate SolutionsAstrometry is geometry plus calibration and residual analysis.
8Spectroscopy and Wavelength CalibrationSpectral calibration is what turns an image into a scientific measurement.
9Radial Velocity and RedshiftVelocity comes from calibrated wavelength shifts, not from the visual appearance of the line.
10Survey Archives and Reproducible QueriesArchive science requires the query record to be as careful as the interpretation.
11Image Stacking, Registration, and Cosmic RaysStacking is a statistical operation, not a magical way to remove artifacts.
12Time-Domain ObservingTime is an observing dimension that needs deliberate planning and analysis.
13Uncertainty, Reporting, and Scientific ClaimsGood reporting tells the reader what was measured, how it was reduced, and what remains uncertain.
14Technical Observing Report WorkshopThe final report is where the evidence becomes a defendable scientific argument.
