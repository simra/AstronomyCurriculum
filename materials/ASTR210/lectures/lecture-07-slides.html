ASTR 210 · Lecture 07

Astrometry and Plate Solutions

Geometry plus calibration plus residual analysis

Learning Goals

  1. Explain how a plate solution links pixel coordinates to sky coordinates.
  2. Derive a plate scale from two matched image/catalog coordinate pairs.
  3. State what residual analysis after a plate solution tells you about systematic error.

Reading anchor: OpenStax Astronomy 2e, Chapter 19.2 (surveying the stars, parallax and astrometric fundamentals).

Why This Matters

Pixels become right ascension and declination only after an image is matched to a reference catalog through a plate solution (World Coordinate System). Astrometry underlies proper motions, orbit determination for asteroids and comets, and the reference frames used for every other kind of positional measurement.

Opening Phenomenon

An asteroid imaged twice, minutes apart, shifts noticeably in pixel coordinates. Converting that pixel shift into an angular sky motion requires knowing the plate scale (arcseconds per pixel), which is itself derived from the same data.

First question: what here is directly measured, and what is inferred from a model?

Terms for This Lecture

centroid

Definition: An estimated subpixel position of a source, usually computed from the intensity distribution around its image.

Why it matters here: Accurate centroids are the measured image coordinates used to fit a plate solution and track motion.

Relationships: Centroid uncertainty depends on point-spread function, SNR, sampling, and background, and propagates into astrometric residuals.

Boundary / common confusion: A centroid is not necessarily the brightest pixel and can be biased by blends, saturation, or asymmetric PSFs.

plate scale

Definition: The angular sky distance represented by one detector pixel, typically in arcseconds per pixel.

Why it matters here: It converts measured pixel separations into angular separations and sets the sampling of the seeing disk.

Relationships: Locally, \(s=\Delta\theta/\Delta p\); a full WCS also includes rotation and distortion.

Boundary / common confusion: A single constant scale is only an approximation when optics introduce significant field distortion.

World Coordinate System (WCS)

Definition: Metadata and a transformation that map image pixel coordinates to celestial coordinates.

Why it matters here: WCS lets an image be compared with catalogs and allows positions to be reported on the sky rather than in detector pixels.

Relationships: A plate solution fits transformation parameters using matched reference stars and records them in image headers.

Boundary / common confusion: A WCS can be present but inaccurate; residuals and reference coverage must be inspected.

astrometric residual

Definition: The positional difference between a reference catalog coordinate and the coordinate predicted by the fitted plate solution.

Why it matters here: Residuals diagnose whether the transformation is accurate enough for the intended position or motion measurement.

Relationships: Residual vectors reveal translation, rotation, scale, and distortion errors; their RMS summarizes typical fit scatter.

Boundary / common confusion: A small global RMS can hide local patterns or a few misidentified reference stars.

proper motion

Definition: The angular change in a star’s sky position per unit time after accounting for coordinate conventions.

Why it matters here: It is an astrophysical signal measured by comparing calibrated positions across epochs.

Relationships: Proper motion is \(\mu=\Delta\theta/\Delta t\) and must be distinguished from parallax and plate-solution error.

Boundary / common confusion: It is only the transverse angular motion; converting to tangential speed requires distance.

reference catalog

Definition: A tabulated set of sources with externally calibrated celestial positions and associated quality information.

Why it matters here: It anchors the pixel-to-sky transformation and supplies the standards against which residuals are computed.

Relationships: Matched catalog stars constrain translation, scale, rotation, and distortion in the plate solution.

Boundary / common confusion: Catalog positions have uncertainties, epochs, flags, and selection effects; they are not error-free truth.

pixel-to-sky transformation

Definition: A mathematical mapping from detector coordinates \((x,y)\) to celestial coordinates such as right ascension and declination.

Why it matters here: It is the model that turns measured centroids into scientifically comparable sky positions.

Relationships: The transformation may range from a linear scale/rotation model to a polynomial distortion model encoded in WCS.

Boundary / common confusion: A transformation fitted over one region or instrument configuration should not be extrapolated without checking residuals.

Evidence We Need to Explain

  • Image 1: (x, y) = (520.3, 411.2) px corresponds to (RA, Dec) = (132.1021°, 12.5510°).
  • Image 2, taken shortly after: (x, y) = (534.7, 415.8) px corresponds to (RA, Dec) = (132.0988°, 12.5521°).
  • The pixel shift and the sky-coordinate shift both scale together, which is exactly the relationship a plate solution is built to capture.

Model

  • A plate solution is a geometric transformation, calibrated using catalog stars, from pixel coordinates to sky coordinates.
  • Plate scale (arcsec/pixel) converts a measured pixel displacement into a physically meaningful angular displacement.
  • A believable astrometric position always comes with a residual: the leftover scatter between predicted and catalog positions of the calibration stars.

Quantitative Tool

\[ \text{plate scale} = \dfrac{\Delta(\text{sky separation, arcsec})}{\Delta(\text{pixel separation, px})} \]

Worked Example

  1. Pixel displacement: Δx = 14.4 px, Δy = 4.6 px, so the total pixel separation is √(14.4² + 4.6²) = 15.12 px.
  2. Sky displacement: ΔRA·cos(Dec) = 11.596 arcsec, ΔDec = 3.960 arcsec, so the total angular separation is 12.25 arcsec.
  3. Plate scale = 12.25 arcsec / 15.12 px = 0.811 arcsec/pixel.
  4. This plate scale, once derived, can be applied to any other pixel measurement on the same image without re-deriving it from scratch — but only if the optical setup has not changed.

Visual Reasoning

Compare the pixel-plane vector to the matched sky-plane vector.

  • Why must both vectors correspond to the same physical displacement?
  • What would a mismatch between the two vector lengths imply about the plate solution?

Common Pitfall

Treating a measured centroid as the final position without asking about the plate-solution residual. A centroid can be very precise (small statistical uncertainty) yet still biased by an uncorrected optical distortion.

Active Learning Segment

Given a third image/catalog coordinate pair, check whether it predicts the same plate scale derived above to within about 5%, and discuss what a large discrepancy would imply.

Lab or Observing Connection

Synthesis

Astrometry is geometry plus calibration plus residual analysis — a plate scale is only as good as the consistency check that follows it.
