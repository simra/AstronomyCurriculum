Physical and observational argument

Let r be the vector of standardized observed magnitudes minus model magnitudes and C their covariance. Correlated calibration modes mean that a residual in one object predicts part of the residual in another. Cholesky factorization C=LL^T permits whitening by solving Ly=r; then chi-square is the squared norm of y. A common intercept can be profiled analytically using the same covariance-weighted solves. The selected block makes that operation tangible, but omitted survey rows, selection and full nuisance treatment prevent generalizing its intercept or chi-square to the parent analysis.

Shared calibration, photometric, and population uncertainties correlate objects. A diagonal-only fit changes the information content and can bias uncertainty; use factorization and linear solves instead of explicitly inverting a covariance matrix.

The public Pantheon+ release documents a 1701 by 1701 statistical-plus-systematic covariance; the included 18 by 18 block is aligned to the selected source rows.
