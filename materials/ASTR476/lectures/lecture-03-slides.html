Driving question: How does a light curve become a distance indicator?

Learning objectives

  1. Explain standardization with stretch, color, and host-galaxy information.
  2. Convert luminosity distance to distance modulus and propagate small magnitude offsets.
  3. Separate relative-distance shape from absolute calibration.

Quantitative anchor

At z=1, the computed distance modulus is 44.1634 mag; 0.01 mag corresponds to about 0.462% in distance.

\[mu = m_B - M_B = 5 log_10(D_L/Mpc)+25\]

Interpretation and limits

A Type Ia supernova is standardized through empirical light-curve and color relations; it is not a perfectly identical explosion. The Hubble diagram constrains relative expansion history while absolute calibration is entangled with the standardized absolute magnitude.

Boundary: The excerpt is deliberately not representative and cannot reproduce a published cosmological constraint. Its diagonal errors are plotting-only.

Evidence: Pantheon+SH0ES provides 1701 light curves for 1550 supernovae; the teaching excerpt has 18 non-calibrator rows stratified in redshift and a matched covariance submatrix.
