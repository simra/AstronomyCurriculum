Physical and observational argument

A defensible cosmological claim is a chain: source records, selection, calibration, observable, covariance, forward model, inference, validation and interpretation. At each transition, record units, software, assumptions and what is not represented. An independent implementation or limiting case can expose numerical errors, while posterior predictive or null tests probe model failure. The final statement should separate directly measured quantities from derived values and model-dependent parameters. A complete artifact set is not itself a review verdict; source spot-checks and local policy adaptation remain required before instruction.

A defensible inference links raw or published evidence to calibrated observables, a transparent likelihood, validated computation, uncertainty, and a claim limited to the assumptions actually tested.

Students receive source-tagged published summaries, a live-verified Pantheon+ excerpt and covariance block, and clearly labeled model outputs; each supports a different inference task.

Study and retrieval practice

  1. Assemble a reproducible chain from source to quantitative result.
  2. Choose claims that match available likelihoods and provenance.
  3. Communicate limitations and an independent validation route.
