Physical and observational argument

A catalog is a selected sample, not a transparent window onto the parent population. Detection probability can depend on flux, color, cadence, host background and follow-up resources. Near a flux threshold, more numerous faint sources can scatter upward than bright sources scatter downward, producing Eddington bias; Malmquist bias describes related luminosity-selection effects. The likelihood must account for truncation and selection normalization. A redshift histogram mixes comoving volume, evolution and completeness, so the deliberately target-stratified Pantheon+ excerpt is useful for plotting practice but cannot estimate the survey selection function.

A flux-limited survey samples a population through a selection function. Detection thresholds, cadence, color cuts, spectroscopic follow-up, and analysis cuts shape the observed redshift and luminosity distributions.
