Physical and observational argument

In linear theory, density perturbations grow under self-gravity while cosmic expansion damps their motion. The growth equation's friction term depends on H(a), and its source depends on the clustering matter fraction. The logarithmic rate f=d ln D/d ln a links density growth to coherent peculiar velocities; redshift-space anisotropy therefore adds a growth observable to geometric distance measures. The supplied integral solves a scale-independent smooth-dark-energy approximation and normalizes D(0)=1. It excludes radiation, massive-neutrino scale dependence and nonlinear evolution, so its values are model calculations rather than survey measurements.

Linear density growth responds to the expansion history and clustering matter. Redshift-space distortions probe coherent peculiar velocities and constrain f sigma8, complementing geometric distance probes.

Growth curves in this package are model calculations, not measured galaxy power spectra; published DES S8 is used only as a separate summary constraint.
