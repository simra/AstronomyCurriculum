Lecture 09: Likelihoods, Priors, and Parameter Degeneracies

ASTR 476: Observational Cosmology · 2026-09-27

Driving question: What does a confidence contour really encode?

Learning objectives

  1. Write a likelihood for correlated Gaussian residuals and identify nuisance parameters.
  2. Distinguish likelihood, prior, posterior, and marginalized constraint.
  3. Explain why independent-looking published summaries may still be statistically dependent.

Terms for This Lecture

Likelihood

Definition: The probability model for the observed data conditional on parameters and nuisance assumptions.

Why it matters here: It is required to answer What does a confidence contour really encode? and to distinguish direct observation from cosmological inference.

Relationships: Read it with Prior, the model \(p(theta|d) proportional to L(d|theta) p(theta)\), and the evidence statement Planck, Pantheon+, BOSS, and DES summary results use different data vectors, nuisance models, and parameter spaces. Their error bars cannot simply be combined as independent Gaussians.

Interpretation and limits

Inference begins with a data model, a likelihood, and explicit nuisance parameters. Parameter degeneracies are geometric directions in the likelihood or posterior; priors and parameterization can change marginal summaries.

Boundary: A plotted ellipse in the lecture is a synthetic Gaussian geometry demonstration, not a digitized published posterior contour.

Evidence: Planck, Pantheon+, BOSS, and DES summary results use different data vectors, nuisance models, and parameter spaces. Their error bars cannot simply be combined as independent Gaussians.
