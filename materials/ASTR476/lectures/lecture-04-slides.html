Lecture 04: Supernova Covariance and Calibration

ASTR 476: Observational Cosmology · 2026-09-27

Driving question: Why is a list of error bars not a likelihood?

Learning objectives

  1. Construct residual vectors and interpret covariance off-diagonal terms.
  2. Evaluate correlated Gaussian chi-square using Cholesky factorization.
  3. Explain why the supplied subset cannot stand in for the full Pantheon+ likelihood.

Terms for This Lecture

Residual vector

Definition: The ordered differences between observed supernova magnitudes and model predictions.

Why it matters here: It is required to answer Why is a list of error bars not a likelihood? and to distinguish direct observation from cosmological inference.

Relationships: Read it with Covariance matrix, the model \(chi^2 = r^T C^{-1} r\), and the evidence statement The public Pantheon+ release documents a 1701 by 1701 statistical-plus-systematic covariance; the included 18 by 18 block is aligned to the selected source rows.

Boundary or common confusion: Subsetting covariance preserves selected-row correlations but does not preserve the full survey's parameter constraints or selection function.

Quantitative anchor

For the 18-row subset, GLS gives beta=-0.11997 +/- 0.04017 mag; this is conditional and not a survey fit.

\[chi^2 = r^T C^{-1} r\]

Interpretation and limits

Shared calibration, photometric, and population uncertainties correlate objects. A diagonal-only fit changes the information content and can bias uncertainty; use factorization and linear solves instead of explicitly inverting a covariance matrix.

Boundary: Subsetting covariance preserves selected-row correlations but does not preserve the full survey's parameter constraints or selection function.

Evidence: The public Pantheon+ release documents a 1701 by 1701 statistical-plus-systematic covariance; the included 18 by 18 block is aligned to the selected source rows.
