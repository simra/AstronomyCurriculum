Physical and observational argument

Two experiments disagree only after they estimate the same parameter under compatible assumptions. For a difference, the variance includes both individual variances and their covariance; shared calibration or common external data can make that covariance nonzero. Posterior shapes may also be non-Gaussian or prior-sensitive. A responsible comparison first harmonizes definitions, data cuts, nuisance models and parameterization, then evaluates a difference using joint samples or a justified covariance model. The course summary table deliberately preserves source labels and does not manufacture a combined tension statistic from incomplete marginal errors.

A tension claim requires harmonized parameter definitions, assumptions, and uncertainty models. Shared data, calibrations, or theory priors create covariance; scalar error-bar separation is only a diagnostic under strong independence assumptions.
