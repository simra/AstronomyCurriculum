Physical and observational argument

A spectrograph measures wavelength, so redshift begins with line identification and wavelength calibration. For a comoving source and observer in an expanding FLRW spacetime, photon wavelengths scale with the cosmic scale factor during propagation, giving 1+z=a0/ae. Peculiar motion adds a local Doppler component; at low redshift it can dominate the Hubble-flow contribution. The Hubble parameter defines an instantaneous expansion rate, and c/H0 is a characteristic present scale, not the particle horizon or a direct distance to every object.

Cosmological redshift is a ratio of scale factors, not a Doppler velocity except in the low-redshift limit. Distinguish cosmological redshift from peculiar velocity and gravitational shifts before interpreting a spectrum.
