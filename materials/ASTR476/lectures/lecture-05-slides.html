Lecture 05: Baryon Acoustic Oscillations as a Standard Ruler

Driving question: How do galaxy clustering measurements encode distance?

Learning objectives

Terms for This Lecture

Baryon acoustic oscillation

Definition: The late-time clustering imprint of sound waves in the pre-recombination photon-baryon plasma.

Why it matters here: It is required to answer How do galaxy clustering measurements encode distance? and to distinguish direct observation from cosmological inference.

Relationships: Read it with Sound horizon, the model \(D_V(z)=[D_M(z)^2 c z/H(z)]^(1/3)\), and the evidence statement Three published BOSS DR12 consensus points at z=0.38, 0.51, and 0.61 are included as literature-summary measurements, not raw clustering data.

Boundary or common confusion: Do not compare D_V in Mpc directly to D_V r_d,fid/r_d unless explicitly setting r_d=r_d,fid; BAO does not measure an absolute ruler without early-universe calibration.

Interpretation and limits

The pre-recombination sound horizon imprints a preferred clustering separation. Isotropic analyses compress transverse and radial information into D_V/r_d; anisotropic analyses constrain D_M/r_d and H(z)r_d separately.

Boundary: Do not compare D_V in Mpc directly to D_V r_d,fid/r_d unless explicitly setting r_d=r_d,fid; BAO does not measure an absolute ruler without early-universe calibration.

Evidence: Three published BOSS DR12 consensus points at z=0.38, 0.51, and 0.61 are included as literature-summary measurements, not raw clustering data.

Active check: Explain the degeneracy between late-time distances and the calibrated physical ruler.
