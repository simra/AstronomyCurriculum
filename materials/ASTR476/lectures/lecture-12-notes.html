Physical and observational argument

Energy conservation for a homogeneous component with pressure p=w rho gives rho proportional to a^{-3(1+w)} when w is constant. Curvature contributes both to the expansion rate and to the transverse-distance mapping, so changing Omega_k cannot be represented by modifying only H(z). Distances at different redshifts can share degeneracies among curvature, dark-energy behavior and the sound horizon; multiple calibrated probes help break them. Constant w is a low-dimensional test model, not an arbitrary dark-energy history, and its inferred value depends on the assumed early-time contents and priors.

Curvature changes the mapping from radial to transverse distance, while a constant-w model changes dark-energy density evolution. Multiple redshift ranges and calibrated rulers help break geometric degeneracies.
