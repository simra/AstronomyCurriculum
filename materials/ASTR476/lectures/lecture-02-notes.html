Physical and observational argument

For radial light propagation, ds=0 in the FLRW metric gives a comoving interval equal to the integral of c dt/a(t), or c dz/H(z). Curvature maps radial comoving distance into a different transverse distance; only in a flat geometry are they equal. Flux dilution and photon-energy/time stretching lead to luminosity distance, while apparent angular size uses angular-diameter distance. Etherington reciprocity links them when photon number is conserved and propagation is metric. Every distance label therefore encodes a measurement operation and assumptions.

The homogeneous-isotropic FLRW metric defines line-of-sight comoving, transverse comoving, angular-diameter, luminosity, and volume-averaged distances. They answer different measurement questions and are linked by reciprocity, not interchangeable by name.
