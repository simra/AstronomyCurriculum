Physical and observational argument

A supernova light curve supplies peak flux, color, and temporal width, but standardization is empirical and population-dependent. After corrections, an apparent magnitude still combines absolute luminosity and distance. The distance modulus isolates the luminosity distance only after the calibrated absolute magnitude is specified. Because a uniform shift in absolute calibration can mimic a change in H0, relative distances over redshift constrain the shape of expansion while an external anchor is needed for an absolute scale. The course subset exercises that distinction and intentionally cannot replace the full survey sample.

A Type Ia supernova is standardized through empirical light-curve and color relations; it is not a perfectly identical explosion. The Hubble diagram constrains relative expansion history while absolute calibration is entangled with the standardized absolute magnitude.
