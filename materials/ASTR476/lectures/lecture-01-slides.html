Driving question: What does a measured redshift actually tell us?

Terms for This Lecture

Redshift

Definition: The fractional change between observed and emitted wavelength after identifying the same spectral feature.

Why it matters here: It is required to answer What does a measured redshift actually tell us? and to distinguish direct observation from cosmological inference.

Relationships: Read it with Scale factor, the model \(1+z = a(t_0)/a(t_e)\), and the evidence statement Spectroscopic line identification supplies wavelength ratios; the Pantheon+ excerpt provides heliocentric/CMB-frame redshift fields for supernovae.

Interpretation and limits

Cosmological redshift is a ratio of scale factors, not a Doppler velocity except in the low-redshift limit. Distinguish cosmological redshift from peculiar velocity and gravitational shifts before interpreting a spectrum.

Boundary: At low z, peculiar velocities can be a substantial fraction of cz. Redshift alone is not a distance measurement.
