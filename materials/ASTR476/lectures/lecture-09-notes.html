Physical and observational argument

A likelihood states the probability of data conditional on parameters; a prior encodes information or regularization before the data; the posterior combines both. For correlated Gaussian data, the quadratic residual term must be accompanied by the covariance determinant unless it is constant over the parameters being varied. A contour depends on whether nuisance parameters are fixed, profiled or marginalized, and on parameter coordinates. Published one- or two-dimensional summaries discard much of the joint likelihood and may share calibration or data. Therefore a sum of inverse variances is not a valid multi-probe analysis without a justified independence model.

Inference begins with a data model, a likelihood, and explicit nuisance parameters. Parameter degeneracies are geometric directions in the likelihood or posterior; priors and parameterization can change marginal summaries.

Planck, Pantheon+, BOSS, and DES summary results use different data vectors, nuisance models, and parameter spaces. Their error bars cannot simply be combined as independent Gaussians.
