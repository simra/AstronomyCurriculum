Lecture 02: The FLRW Distance Ladder

Driving question: Why do cosmologists use several different distances?

Learning objectives

  1. Derive the radial comoving-distance integral from the FLRW line element.
  2. Use distance duality D_L=(1+z)^2 D_A under transparent metric propagation.
  3. Compute and label D_C, D_L, D_A, and D_V with units.

Quantitative anchor

At z=1, D_C=3401.263 Mpc, D_L=6802.526 Mpc, and D_A=1700.631 Mpc.

\[D_C(z)=c/H_0 integral_0^z dz'/E(z')\]

Interpretation and limits

The homogeneous-isotropic FLRW metric defines line-of-sight comoving, transverse comoving, angular-diameter, luminosity, and volume-averaged distances. They answer different measurement questions and are linked by reciprocity, not interchangeable by name.

Boundary: Curvature changes transverse distance; radiation matters at early times. Never apply the flat, late-time integral to recombination as if it were a precision CMB model.

Evidence: The computation is independently checked against Astropy FlatLambdaCDM with radiation disabled to match the explicitly matter-plus-Lambda integral.

Active check: Check that c/H0 is a length and that every reported distance is in Mpc.
