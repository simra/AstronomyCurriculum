Lecture 14: Observational Cosmology Synthesis

ASTR 476: Observational Cosmology · 2026-09-25

Driving question: What evidence is needed to defend a cosmological claim?

Learning objectives

  1. Assemble a reproducible chain from source to quantitative result.
  2. Choose claims that match available likelihoods and provenance.
  3. Communicate limitations and an independent validation route.
Slide 1 · 8 min

Start with the scientific claim

State the observable, target parameter, model assumptions, and alternative explanations.

Slide 2 · 10 min

Evidence-to-likelihood chain

Connect source rows, calibration, covariance, forward model, and parameter inference.

Slide 3 · 12 min

Independent recomputation

Run a second implementation or analytic limit and document the comparison tolerance.

Slide 4 · 12 min

Red-team the result

Ask what data selection, calibration, covariance, or model choice could reverse the interpretation.

Slide 5 · 10 min

Communicate scope

Separate measured result, derived quantity, model-dependent inference, and unresolved limitation.

Slide 6 · 8 min

Release checklist

Version data and code, expose assumptions, preserve environment, and link each claim to evidence.

Quantitative anchor

The audit recomputes the conditional subset intercept (-0.11997 mag) and checks the source-to-claim chain.

\[data -> calibrated observable -> likelihood -> posterior predictive check -> claim\]

Interpretation and limits

A defensible inference links raw or published evidence to calibrated observables, a transparent likelihood, validated computation, uncertainty, and a claim limited to the assumptions actually tested.

Boundary: Structural completeness is not scientific approval. A complete package remains provisional until independent human review and local policy adaptation.

Evidence: Students receive source-tagged published summaries, a live-verified Pantheon+ excerpt and covariance block, and clearly labeled model outputs; each supports a different inference task.

Active check: Version data and code, expose assumptions, preserve environment, and link each claim to evidence.
