Course information

ASTR 476: Observational Cosmology · 3 credits · Year 4 elective · 14 weeks.

Description: Observational foundations of modern cosmology, including redshift and distance measures, Type Ia supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations, CMB acoustic physics, weak lensing, structure growth, parameter inference, survey selection, and systematic uncertainty.

Prerequisites: ASTR 420 or concurrent enrollment, consistent with the curriculum catalog.

Planning assumption: two 75-minute meetings per week; each numbered item is a weekly lecture module taught across those meetings. Confirm institutional policies, instructor details, dates, accessibility, and local prerequisites before use.

Learning outcomes

  • Derive and compute cosmological redshift, FLRW distances, and distance-modulus relations with assumptions and units.
  • Explain how SNe Ia, BAO, CMB anisotropies, weak lensing, and clustering constrain different combinations of cosmological parameters.
  • Use covariance-aware likelihood calculations and distinguish measurement, derived quantity, and model-dependent inference.
  • Assess calibration, selection, nuisance parameters, and model assumptions in a published cosmological result.
  • Build reproducible analyses with source provenance, pinned software, numerical checks, and explicit limitations.
  • Communicate a claim whose strength matches the data and likelihood actually available.

Cadence and resources

Fourteen weekly lecture modules are paired into seven two-week units. One lab and one problem set cover each pair and are due at the end of the second week (Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14). This deliberate 14/7/7 cadence gives time for data work and uncertainty analysis; it is not a one-to-one lecture-to-assessment schedule. See schedule.html for the exact map.

CPython 3.14.6; pinned NumPy, SciPy, Astropy, and Matplotlib versions are listed in requirements.txt. No paid account or observatory access is assumed. The Pantheon+ excerpt is live-verified, redshift-stratified, and unsuitable for survey-level inference. Other data are published summaries or model illustrations as labeled in reference-log.md.

Students may discuss concepts, but submitted reasoning and analysis must be attributable. Cite data, software, and adapted code. Revisions and resubmissions are encouraged to improve mastery; high standards for method, evidence, uncertainty, and interpretation remain in force.
