ASTR 430 Lecture 09: Observational Planning and Time Allocation

Research Methods in Astronomy · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Turn a science question into an exposure, cadence, and feasibility plan.
  • Explain signal-to-noise, overheads, cadence, airmass, and scheduling with the formal object.
  • Separate evidence, transformation, inference, and limitation.

Method in context

A proposal is a constrained experiment: detector physics, weather, and calibration limit the claim before observing. Ask what a second researcher needs to reproduce the claim: raw input, processing state, software or database version, and inferential step.

Derivation and assumptions

\(SNR=S t/sqrt((S+B)t+n_pix RN^2)\)

Define every symbol and explain the failure mode. A method becomes scientific when assumptions are visible enough to be tested.

Worked example

A feasible 10-hour plan is 7.0 h integration + 1.0 h calibration + 1.0 h overhead + 1.0 h contingency.

This is a planning example, not an observatory performance claim. Require an independent recomputation and one perturbation.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Do not confuse a data service with a measurement, a catalog identifier with physical identity, a formal error with total uncertainty, or a polished figure with a validated inference. Reconstruct the evidence chain before interpreting.
