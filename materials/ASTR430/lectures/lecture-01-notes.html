Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Claim-evidence chain

A traceable sequence connecting a scientific statement to observations, transformations, and assumptions. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for claim-evidence chains and FAIR data.

Use and relationship: Read it together with FAIR data and Reproducibility. The governing relation \(R=(claim,data,code,environment,provenance)\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Method in context

Research methods is astronomy's error budget for knowledge: each conclusion needs a traceable path back to observation and assumption. Ask what a second researcher needs to reproduce the claim: raw input, processing state, software or database version, and inferential step.

Derivation and assumptions

\(R=(claim,data,code,environment,provenance)\)

Define every symbol and explain the failure mode. A method becomes scientific when assumptions are visible enough to be tested.

Worked example

R has five required fields: claim, data, code, environment, and provenance.

A missing field is a reproducibility defect, not merely a documentation preference. Require an independent recomputation and one perturbation.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Do not confuse a data service with a measurement, a catalog identifier with physical identity, a formal error with total uncertainty, or a polished figure with a validated inference. Reconstruct the evidence chain before interpreting.
