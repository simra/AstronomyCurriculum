Worked Solutions

1. HZ boundary scaling

d_inner=sqrt(0.5/1.1)=0.674 AU, d_outer=sqrt(0.5/0.32)=1.250 AU -- both closer in than the Sun's 0.953-1.768 AU by a factor of sqrt(0.5)=0.707, i.e. about 1.41× closer.

2. 51 Pegasi and HD 209458 habitable zones

Using L∝M^3.5: 51 Pegasi (M=1.11 Msun) gives L≈1.44 Lsun, HZ≈1.14-2.12 AU; HD 209458 (M=1.15 Msun) gives L≈1.63 Lsun, HZ≈1.22-2.26 AU. Both 51 Pegasi b (a=0.052 AU) and HD 209458 b (a=0.0471 AU) orbit far interior to their star's habitable zone -- neither is anywhere near habitable-zone distance, consistent with both being hot, gas-giant worlds.

3. Venus insolation check

S(Venus)/S(Earth) = 1.91 -- above the inner-edge threshold (1.1 S_Earth), meaning Venus lies inside (interior to) the classical habitable zone's inner edge, consistent with its real runaway-greenhouse outcome.

4. Habitable zone is necessary, not sufficient

The classical HZ answers only "is stellar insolation compatible with surface liquid water assuming an Earth-like atmosphere"; Europa's ocean is sustained by tidal heating, an entirely different energy source unrelated to stellar flux, so its existence outside the classical HZ does not contradict the HZ concept -- it simply falls outside the specific physical question the classical HZ was designed to answer, exactly as Lecture 10 states explicitly.
