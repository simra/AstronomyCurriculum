ASTR 420 Lecture 08: Inflation and the Early Universe

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Explain the horizon and flatness problems and identify what inflation does and does not predict.
  • Explain rapid expansion, horizon crossing, and primordial perturbations using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Lecture arc

Inflation addresses causal contact and near-flatness by changing the expansion history; it does not by itself fix every microphysical parameter. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce rapid expansion, horizon crossing, and primordial perturbations only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

This is an exponential scaling argument, not a measurement of the inflationary potential. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Inflation addresses causal contact and near-flatness by changing the expansion history; it does not by itself fix every microphysical parameter. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.

Preparation for the problem set: write a one-paragraph evidence chain from observation to conclusion and identify the weakest link.
