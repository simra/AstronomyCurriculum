ASTR 420 Lecture 11: Type Ia Supernovae and Dark Energy

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Fit a supernova Hubble diagram and infer acceleration from residual curvature.
  • Explain standardization, distance modulus, and acceleration using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Lecture arc

Acceleration is inferred from a distance-redshift relation relative to decelerating models; calibration and selection systematics matter. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce standardization, distance modulus, and acceleration only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(mu=m-M=5 log10(D_L / 10 pc)\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

For mu=43.16, D_L = 10^((mu-25)/5) = 4285.485 Mpc.

The distance is standardized-model dependent and inherits calibration, dust, and selection uncertainties. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Acceleration is inferred from a distance-redshift relation relative to decelerating models; calibration and selection systematics matter. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.

Preparation for the problem set: write a one-paragraph evidence chain from observation to conclusion and identify the weakest link.
