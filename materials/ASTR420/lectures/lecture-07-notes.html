Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Weak freeze-out

The epoch when weak-interaction rates fell below expansion, preventing the neutron-to-proton ratio from tracking equilibrium. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for reaction rates, freeze-out, and primordial light elements.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Baryon density and Neutron-to-proton ratio. The governing relation \(Y_p approximately 2(n/p) / [1+(n/p)]\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The estimate assumes nearly every surviving neutron enters helium-4 and omits the small deuterium, helium-3, and lithium channels. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Lecture arc

The helium prediction is a coupled nuclear-and-expansion calculation; stellar processing alone cannot account for the observed primordial baseline. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce reaction rates, freeze-out, and primordial light elements only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(Y_p approximately 2(n/p) / [1+(n/p)]\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

With n/p=1/7, Y_p = 2(n/p)/(1+n/p) = 0.25.

The estimate assumes nearly every surviving neutron enters helium-4 and omits the small deuterium, helium-3, and lithium channels. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

The helium prediction is a coupled nuclear-and-expansion calculation; stellar processing alone cannot account for the observed primordial baseline. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.
