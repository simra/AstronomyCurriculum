Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Convergence

The dimensionless projected surface-density field that describes isotropic gravitational-lensing magnification. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for lensing geometry, convergence, and mass mapping.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Mass-sheet degeneracy and Shear. The governing relation \(\\kappa=\\Sigma/\\Sigma_{\\rm crit}\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The value is dimensionless, but translating shear to a mass map requires source redshifts and an inversion convention. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Lecture arc

Lensing measures total projected mass, including dark matter, but inversion has a mass-sheet degeneracy and line-of-sight structure. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce lensing geometry, convergence, and mass mapping only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(\\kappa=\\Sigma/\\Sigma_{\\rm crit}\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

With Sigma=2.0e14 and Sigma_crit=3.0e15 solar masses Mpc^-2, kappa = 0.0666667.

The value is dimensionless, but translating shear to a mass map requires source redshifts and an inversion convention. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Lensing measures total projected mass, including dark matter, but inversion has a mass-sheet degeneracy and line-of-sight structure. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance.
