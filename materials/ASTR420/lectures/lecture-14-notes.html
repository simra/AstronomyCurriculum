Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Joint likelihood

The combined probability of multiple datasets given shared model parameters and stated dependence assumptions. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for multi-probe consistency, covariance, model comparison, and open questions.

Lecture arc

Cosmology is strongest when independent epochs and messengers agree; a tension can indicate statistical fluctuation, hidden covariance, calibration systematics, or model incompleteness. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce multi-probe consistency, covariance, model comparison, and open questions only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Cosmology is strongest when independent epochs and messengers agree; a tension can indicate statistical fluctuation, hidden covariance, calibration systematics, or model incompleteness. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.
