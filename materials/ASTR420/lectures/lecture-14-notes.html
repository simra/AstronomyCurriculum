ASTR 420 Lecture 14: Cosmological Synthesis, Tensions, and Tests

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Construct an evidence-weighted concordance model and compare parameter constraints without treating disagreement as an arithmetic error.
  • Explain multi-probe consistency, covariance, model comparison, and open questions using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Lecture arc

Cosmology is strongest when independent epochs and messengers agree; a tension can indicate statistical fluctuation, hidden covariance, calibration systematics, or model incompleteness. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce multi-probe consistency, covariance, model comparison, and open questions only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(\\chi^2=(\\mathbf d-\\mathbf m)^T C^{-1}(\\mathbf d-\\mathbf m)\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

The independent-error normalized difference is |73.04-67.4|/sqrt(1.04^2+0.5^2) = 4.88756 sigma.

This summary is conditional on independent Gaussian errors; shared calibration or model covariance changes the interpretation. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Cosmology is strongest when independent epochs and messengers agree; a tension can indicate statistical fluctuation, hidden covariance, calibration systematics, or model incompleteness. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.

Preparation for the problem set: write a one-paragraph evidence chain from observation to conclusion and identify the weakest link.
