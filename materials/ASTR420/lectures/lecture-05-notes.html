ASTR 420 Lecture 05: Thermal History and Recombination

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Track how temperature and ionization changed as the universe expanded.
  • Explain adiabatic cooling, recombination, and photon decoupling using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Adiabatic cooling

The decrease of the radiation temperature as wavelengths stretch during expansion without net heat exchange. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for adiabatic cooling, recombination, and photon decoupling.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Last-scattering surface and Recombination. The governing relation \(T_\\gamma(a)=T_{\\gamma,0}/a\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The scaling follows adiabatic expansion of radiation; it does not alone determine the ionization history. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Lecture arc

The CMB last-scattering surface is a time slice of photon decoupling, not a physical shell with a fixed material boundary. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce adiabatic cooling, recombination, and photon decoupling only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(T_\\gamma(a)=T_{\\gamma,0}/a\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

T_gamma(z=1100) = T0(1+z) = 3000.78 K.

The scaling follows adiabatic expansion of radiation; it does not alone determine the ionization history. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance.
