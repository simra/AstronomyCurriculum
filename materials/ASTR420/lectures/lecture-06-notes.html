ASTR 420 Lecture 06: The Cosmic Microwave Background

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Interpret the CMB spectrum and anisotropy amplitude as evidence for a hot early universe.
  • Explain blackbody spectrum, acoustic peaks, and parameter inference using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Lecture arc

The near-perfect blackbody spectrum tests thermal history; anisotropies carry information about geometry, baryons, matter, and initial perturbations. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce blackbody spectrum, acoustic peaks, and parameter inference only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(I_nu(T)=2h nu^3 / c^2 [e^(h nu/kT)-1]^-1\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

Wien's law gives nu_peak approximately 281.97 GHz for T=2.7255 K.

The frequency of the intensity peak depends on the spectral variable; wavelength and frequency peaks are not reciprocal labels. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

The near-perfect blackbody spectrum tests thermal history; anisotropies carry information about geometry, baryons, matter, and initial perturbations. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.

Preparation for the problem set: write a one-paragraph evidence chain from observation to conclusion and identify the weakest link.
