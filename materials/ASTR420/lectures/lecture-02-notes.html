ASTR 420 Lecture 02: Relativistic Redshift and Distance

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish velocity, redshift, luminosity distance, and angular-diameter distance.
  • Explain special-relativistic redshift and cosmological distance measures using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Doppler redshift

A wavelength shift produced by relative motion through spacetime between emitter and observer. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for special-relativistic redshift and cosmological distance measures.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Distance duality and Cosmological redshift. The governing relation \(1+z=\\sqrt{(1+\\beta)/(1-\\beta)}\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The difference is about 1.5%; cosmological redshift is not generally a simple Doppler velocity. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Lecture arc

At low redshift several distance definitions nearly agree; at cosmological redshift they separate because the scale factor changes during photon travel. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce special-relativistic redshift and cosmological distance measures only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

Derivation and interpretation

\(1+z=\\sqrt{(1+\\beta)/(1-\\beta)}\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

For z=0.10, the special-relativistic formula gives v = 28487.1 km s^-1, while cz = 29979.2 km s^-1.

The difference is about 1.5%; cosmological redshift is not generally a simple Doppler velocity. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

At low redshift several distance definitions nearly agree; at cosmological redshift they separate because the scale factor changes during photon travel. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.
