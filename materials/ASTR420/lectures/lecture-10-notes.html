Baryon acoustic oscillation

The preferred separation imprinted in matter clustering by pre-recombination sound waves. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for photon-baryon sound speed, drag epoch, and correlation peaks.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Alcock-Paczynski effect and Drag epoch. The governing relation \(r_s=integral_0^ad c_s(a) da / [a^2 H(a)]\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The ruler is inferred from a correlation feature and depends on the adopted sound-horizon calibration. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Lecture arc

BAO is not a fixed ruler in physical coordinates; the measured angular and redshift separations are translated through a cosmological model. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce photon-baryon sound speed, drag epoch, and correlation peaks only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

Derivation and interpretation

\(r_s=integral_0^ad c_s(a) da / [a^2 H(a)]\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

A 150 Mpc comoving ruler subtending an angle theta constrains D_M through theta = r_s/D_M; the same ruler in redshift constrains H(z).

The ruler is inferred from a correlation feature and depends on the adopted sound-horizon calibration. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

BAO is not a fixed ruler in physical coordinates; the measured angular and redshift separations are translated through a cosmological model. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance.
