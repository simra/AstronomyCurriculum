ASTR 420 Lecture 01: The Expanding Universe

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Infer expansion from redshift, distance, and the scale factor.
  • Explain redshift, comoving coordinates, and Hubble's law using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Lecture arc

Expansion is a change in the metric separation of unbound systems, not an explosion into pre-existing space. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce redshift, comoving coordinates, and Hubble's law only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

At z=0.01, the low-z estimate gives d = cz/H0 = 44.4796 Mpc.

This linear estimate is a low-redshift approximation; peculiar velocities can be a comparable correction for nearby galaxies. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

Expansion is a change in the metric separation of unbound systems, not an explosion into pre-existing space. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.

Preparation for the problem set: write a one-paragraph evidence chain from observation to conclusion and identify the weakest link.
