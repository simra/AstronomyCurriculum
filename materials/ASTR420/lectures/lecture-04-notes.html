ASTR 420 Lecture 04: Cosmic Time and Horizons

Cosmology · study notes

Learning objectives

  • Compute lookback time in a flat matter-plus-dark-energy model and explain horizons.
  • Explain lookback time, conformal time, and causal limits using the governing relation.
  • Separate measurement, calibration, inference, and model limitation.

Terms Developed in Context

These terms form the lecture's reasoning chain from observable through model to a bounded physical conclusion.

Lookback time

The elapsed cosmic time between emission at redshift z and observation today. In this lecture it supports the evidence-to-model chain for lookback time, conformal time, and causal limits.

Use and relationship: Read it together with Light cone and Conformal time. The governing relation \(t_L(z)=integral_0^z dz' / [(1+z')H(z') ]\) shows where the concept enters quantitatively; the student should state whether its value is measured, calibrated, adopted, or inferred.

Boundary: The result depends on H0 and the density parameters; the integral is not replaceable by 1/H0 at z=1. This caveat prevents the term from being treated as a unique explanation or a context-free number.

Lecture arc

A redshift is not itself an age; converting it to time requires a cosmological expansion history. Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students what is directly measured. Introduce lookback time, conformal time, and causal limits only after the data-model boundary is explicit.

The visual is a reasoning object: students should narrate what each line, region, or axis means before seeing the equation.

Derivation and interpretation

\(t_L(z)=integral_0^z dz' / [(1+z')H(z') ]\)

Define the symbols, units, and assumptions. Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails. Relate the result to the paired lab's data record rather than treating the equation as a detached recipe.

Worked example

Numerical Simpson integration gives t_L(z=1) = 7.9497 Gyr for the shared flat Lambda-CDM parameters.

The result depends on H0 and the density parameters; the integral is not replaceable by 1/H0 at z=1. Require students to estimate the answer, show units, and state whether the approximation is controlled, empirical, or merely illustrative.

Misconceptions and study guidance

A redshift is not itself an age; converting it to time requires a cosmological expansion history. Common traps include confusing redshift with a unique velocity, treating a model-dependent parameter as a direct measurement, and overlooking covariance. Study by reproducing the calculation, changing one assumption, and comparing the result with the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log.
