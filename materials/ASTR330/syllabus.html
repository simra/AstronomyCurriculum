ASTR 330: Astrophysics II: Stellar Structure and Evolution

4 credits · Prerequisites: ASTR 310; MATH 255; PHYS 271 · Year 3, Spring

Description

Develops the physics of stellar interiors, opacity, energy transport, nuclear burning, stellar atmospheres, main sequence evolution, late-stage evolution, compact remnants, supernovae, and nucleosynthesis. This is the direct sequel to ASTR 310, and is deliberately more quantitatively demanding: it completes the four-equation system of stellar structure that ASTR 310 began (mass continuity, hydrostatic equilibrium, energy conservation, energy transport), derives the equation of state, opacity law, radiative and convective transport criteria, the pp-chain/CNO-cycle Gamow-peak physics, the mass-luminosity-lifetime scaling, and the Chandrasekhar mass from first principles, and applies every derivation to real, precisely measured stars and compact objects (Alpha Centauri A/B and Sirius A/B, carried forward with established provenance from ASTR 230/ASTR 310; the Hyades open cluster and the neutron star PSR J0348+0432, both independently live-verified for this course; see reference-log.md).

Learning Outcomes

  1. State the four coupled stellar-structure equations (mass continuity, hydrostatic equilibrium, energy conservation, energy transport) and explain why all four, together with an equation of state and an opacity law, are required to determine a star's structure.
  2. Derive the equation of state and the electron-scattering and Kramers opacity laws, and apply the radiative-diffusion temperature gradient and the Schwarzschild criterion to determine whether a stellar layer is radiative or convective.
  3. Derive the Gamow-peak formalism explaining why nuclear fusion proceeds at real stellar-core temperatures, describe the proton-proton chain and CNO cycle, and compute the crossover temperature separating pp-dominated from CNO-dominated stars.
  4. Derive the approximate mass-luminosity relation and main-sequence lifetime scaling from homology reasoning, and apply both to real, precisely measured stars and to a real star cluster's main-sequence turnoff age.
  5. Describe post-main-sequence evolution (shell burning, the red giant branch, helium burning via the triple-alpha process, the asymptotic giant branch, and white dwarf formation) and derive the Chandrasekhar mass from the relativistic-degenerate n=3 polytrope.
  6. Describe the core-collapse supernova mechanism, compute its gravitational-binding-energy budget, and synthesize this course's real compact-object case studies (a white dwarf and a neutron star) into a single, quantitative physical picture of stellar-evolution endpoints and nucleosynthesis.

Lecture, Lab, and Problem-Set Cadence

The course runs 14 lectures, 7 labs, and 7 problem sets — a matched 1:1 biweekly cadence in which each lab and its paired problem set both follow the same two-lecture unit. This cadence directly follows the ASTR 310 precedent (itself following ASTR 210's 4-credit, 14-lecture structure and ASTR 230's matched-cadence pattern), because ASTR 330's units build a single cumulative toolkit — the four structure equations, then opacity/transport, then nuclear burning, then mass-luminosity-lifetime scaling and polytropes, then post-main-sequence evolution, then compact-object physics — that culminates in one capstone lab and problem set (Lab/PS 07, applying the full toolkit to the real neutron star PSR J0348+0432) rather than requiring an uneven, technical-report-style cadence. A matched cadence also lets each lab and problem set reinforce the immediately preceding lecture pair's derivation while the mathematics is still fresh, exactly as justified for ASTR 310.

Prerequisites Check

ASTR 310 derives hydrostatic equilibrium, the virial theorem, and the two-body Kepler problem from first principles; ASTR 330 uses all three directly (hydrostatic equilibrium as one of the four stellar-structure equations, the virial theorem's reasoning pattern for the Chandrasekhar-mass and core-collapse energy-budget derivations, and Kepler's third law for the PSR J0348+0432 capstone). MATH 255 (Differential Equations) supplies the ordinary-differential-equation background needed for the coupled stellar-structure system and the Lane-Emden equation. PHYS 271 (Modern Physics) supplies the quantum-mechanical background (the Pauli exclusion principle, quantum tunneling) needed for electron degeneracy pressure and the Gamow-peak formalism.
