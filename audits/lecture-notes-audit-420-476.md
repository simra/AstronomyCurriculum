# Baseline Lecture-Notes Supplementarity Audit: ASTR420–ASTR476

## Executive summary

This baseline audit compares every committed lecture-notes file directly with its matching committed slide deck in **ASTR420, ASTR430, ASTR470, ASTR472, ASTR474, and ASTR476**. The audit covers **84 of 84 expected lecture pairs** (14 per course; 168 HTML artifacts). To isolate the baseline from concurrent glossary-production work, every slide and notes file was retrieved from immutable `HEAD` with `git show HEAD:<repository-relative-path>`; mutable working-tree lecture files were not used.

**Overall disposition:** the notes set does not meet the repository's supplementarity standard. **74 pairs need substantial rewrite, 10 need targeted expansion, and 0 meet the supplementarity standard.** ASTR474 is the only course with targeted-expansion cases: its notes often add a real derivation or algorithm interpretation, but still fail course-wide glossary, resource, voice, and depth requirements. The other five courses show systematic near-paraphrase, placeholder derivations, copied examples, or insufficient independent-study depth across all lectures.

### Rating and verdict method

Each row rates **glossary coverage, explanatory depth, derivations/examples, context/limitations, resource guidance, and student-facing voice** as **Strong**, **Partial**, or **Weak**. Ratings judge the notes as a supplement to the matching deck, not the topic's intrinsic importance or the deck alone. Verdicts use the required categories:

- **Meets supplementarity standard:** the notes materially deepen the deck for independent study.
- **Needs targeted expansion:** useful lecture-specific depth exists, but identifiable gaps remain.
- **Needs substantial rewrite:** the notes predominantly repeat, lightly reframe, or promise rather than supply the required explanation.

No committed deck in the six-course scope contains an interactive glossary/term explorer. Consequently, glossary coverage is Weak for all 84 pairs: no term-button/panel mappings, keyboard behavior, one-panel-per-term behavior, or print-visible definitions exist to validate, and named technical terms are usually not developed in the notes through use, relationships, and boundaries/misconceptions.

## Repeated cross-course failure patterns

1. **Near-paraphrase is the dominant failure.** Most notes preserve the slide sequence and repeat objectives, claims, equations, figure captions, worked values, cautions, and prompts. Added prose is frequently reusable template language rather than lecture-specific reasoning.
2. **Derivation headings often contain instructions instead of derivations.** Repeated phrases such as “Define every symbol,” “Explain why the relation is useful,” or “Recompute independently” describe missing authoring work. Students are not shown intermediate steps, assumptions, limiting cases, uncertainty propagation, or the reasoning connecting observables to claims.
3. **Worked examples are usually copied rather than extended.** The same numerical anchor appears in slides and notes. Several “worked” sections are only symbolic formulas, quoted literature values, package-version lists, or recycled examples from another lecture.
4. **Glossary infrastructure is absent across all 84 decks.** This blocks the required one-to-one term mapping, accessibility checks, print behavior, and deeper note treatment.
5. **Resource guidance is not actionable.** Notes commonly name a textbook, mission, archive, software package, or `reference-log.md` without an exact section/table/figure/page and without telling students what to read, inspect, reproduce, or compare.
6. **Production- or instructor-facing language leaks into student notes.** Examples include timing scripts, “require students,” generator-file paths, authoring/retrieval failures, audit/release commentary, and directions to consult internal reference logs.
7. **Context is stronger than explanation, but still uneven.** Many pairs name assumptions and systematics responsibly; fewer quantify their effect, compare alternatives, connect to a real dataset, or explain how the limitation changes inference.
8. **Template repetition displaces lecture-specific misconceptions.** Generic redshift, calibration, provenance, or inference warnings recur in topics where a different misconception is central.
9. **A small number of copied scientific or mathematical defects recur in both artifacts.** High-priority examples include ASTR420 L02's incorrect 1.5% comparison, ASTR420 L06's mislabeled CMB spectral peak, ASTR470 L01's inconsistent `E=3kT/2` versus `kT=5 keV` calculation, ASTR472 L11's 1000× prose coefficient error, ASTR476 L09's likelihood mislabeling, and ASTR476 L10's reversed conditional-probability label.
10. **Advanced-course depth is frequently below the expected 400-level standard.** Many notes omit governing-equation derivations, model comparison, computational procedure, real-data interpretation, and explicit uncertainty analysis needed for capable undergraduates to study independently.

## Prioritized recommendations

### Blocking revisions

1. **Rewrite the 74 substantial-rewrite notes lecture by lecture.** For each major concept, add at least three appropriate expansion types: intermediate derivation/calculation, physical interpretation or limiting case, a distinct worked/transfer example, observational/computational context, historical motivation, misconception/alternative-model analysis, uncertainty/model dependence, or a verified annotated resource.
2. **Correct copied scientific and rendering defects before reuse.** Address every lecture-specific issue identified in the tables, especially numerical inconsistencies, mislabeled equations/probabilities, missing units/conventions, unescaped mathematical markup, and examples shifted into the wrong lecture.
3. **Implement and validate glossary support.** Select lecture-specific terms; provide keyboard-operable controls, exactly one explanatory panel per term, and print-visible definitions; then expand every term in the matching notes through use, relationship, and boundary/misconception.
4. **Replace copied numeric anchors with complete reasoning.** State inputs, units, assumptions, algorithm or algebra, intermediate results, uncertainty or convergence checks, interpretation, and at least one new transfer case.
5. **Replace generic evidence language with lecture-specific evidence.** Use inspectable figures, datasets, spectra, light curves, residuals, response curves, query results, covariance examples, or simulations with provenance and an explicit inference task.
6. **Make resources directly usable.** Provide at least one verified, exact resource per lecture and state what the student should read, inspect, calculate, reproduce, or compare.
7. **Remove instructor, production, and release-process language from student notes.** Move timing plans and authoring instructions to instructor-facing artifacts; replace them with direct explanation and student study guidance.

### Targeted expansion for ASTR474

The 10 targeted-expansion lectures in ASTR474 should preserve their existing lecture-specific derivations and limitations while adding the missing glossary mapping, complete worked extensions, student-facing study structure, and exact annotated resources. Lectures 04, 05, 07, and 14 still require substantial rewrite because the core promised topic or synthesis is not actually worked through.

## Course-wide patterns

### Blocking patterns

1. **All 14 notes are template-complete but substantively shallow.** Notes contain only six top-level sections and about **310–359 words**, versus **562–621 words** in the already concise decks. Length is not itself the criterion, but here it reflects missing derivation, interpretation, transfer, and resource content.
2. **Near-paraphrase is systematic.** Each notes file repeats the slide’s title claim, governing equation, visual labels, worked result, and limitation. The same ten generic sentences occur in **all 14 notes**, including:
   - **“Define the symbols, units, and assumptions.”**
   - **“Explain why the relation is useful and where it fails.”**
   - **“The axes, labels, and geometry carry the physical inference rather than serving as decoration.”**
   - **“Require students to estimate the answer…”**
   These are instructions to create explanations, not the explanations themselves.
3. **Glossary/term development fails throughout.** There is no interactive slide glossary or term explorer to inventory mechanically. Across the explicit “Define and distinguish” sets, terms are merely repeated in the notes; none receives the required combination of operational use, relationship to another concept/observable, and boundary or misconception.
4. **Derivations are absent.** Every notes file copies one governing relation and follows it with generic imperatives. Intermediate algebra, assumptions, limiting cases, uncertainty propagation, and distinct examples are generally absent.
5. **The worked examples are copied, not extended.** Lecture 10 is only a symbolic proportionality, not a completed calculation; Lecture 13 depends on an undisplayed “supplied power table.”
6. **Student-facing voice is not achieved.** Every notes file contains instructor/production language such as **“Begin with the observational puzzle and ask students…”** and **“Require students…”**. These files are labeled “study notes,” so the directions should be replaced by direct explanations and learner actions.
7. **Resource guidance is inadequate.** The notes have no links and no lecture-specific resource section. The repeated advice to compare with **“the exact OpenStax anchor listed in the reference log”** is vague and forces students to find another artifact. The slides contain chapter/figure ranges, but the notes neither reproduce nor annotate them with a specific study task.
8. **Generic misconception text is repeated regardless of topic.** All 14 notes warn about **“confusing redshift with a unique velocity”**, even in BBN, lensing, structure-growth, and power-spectrum lectures, displacing lecture-specific misconception analysis.

### Scientific, mathematical, and reference concerns visible in the pairs

- **Lecture 02:** the stated 1.5% SR-versus-\(cz\) difference is arithmetically wrong; it is approximately **4.98%** relative to \(cz\).
- **Lecture 06:** **281.97 GHz** is the frequency obtained by converting the \(B_\lambda\) wavelength peak, not the peak of \(B_\nu\), which is approximately **160.23 GHz** at 2.7255 K. The notes copy the error.
- **Lecture 10:** the item labeled “Worked calculation” lacks sufficient numerical inputs and produces no numerical answer.
- **Lecture 13:** the asserted ratio cannot be independently reproduced from either member of the pair because the table values are not shown.
- **Notation quality:** several equations use prose-like tokens inside math (`integral`, `approximately`, `mu`, `log10`) rather than clear mathematical notation, weakening readability and, in some cases, meaning.
- **Reference caveat:** every deck repeats that Chapter 29 source-index labels have offsets and the adopted PDF remains authoritative. That is honest provenance, but the pair itself does not verify the cited figure ranges, and the notes do not provide a usable annotated follow-up resource.
- **Numerical spot checks:** Lectures 01, 03, 05, 07, 08, 09, 11, 12, and 14 reproduce correctly from their stated inputs. Lecture 04 is consistent at about 7.95 Gyr for the stated class of flat-\(\Lambda\)CDM parameters, though the exact parameter values and quadrature details are omitted. Lectures 02 and 06 contain the errors above.

## Course-wide patterns

### Blocking patterns

1. **Near-paraphrase rather than supplementarity (14/14).** Every notes file follows the same six-section template—“Learning objectives,” “Method in context,” “Derivation and assumptions,” “Worked example,” “Misconceptions and study guidance,” and “Verified reading”—and reuses the slide claim, figure caption, formula, example, limitation sentence, and source names. Notes contain only about **212–240 words**, while decks contain about **375–408 words**; they are shorter than the slides and do not function as independent-study explanations.
2. **Template repetition (14/14).** The exact sentences “Define every symbol and explain the failure mode,” “Require an independent recomputation and one perturbation,” and the full generic “Do not confuse a data service…” misconception paragraph occur in every notes file (**42 total occurrences**). Decks likewise repeat the same “Keep observation…,” “Identify which part…,” three generic failure modes, workshop description, takeaways, and “geometry distinguishes inputs…” visual caption across the course.
3. **No interactive glossary/term explorer (14/14).** Consequently there are no controls/panels to test for keyboard, one-panel-per-term, or print behavior. The explicit key-term clusters are merely listed in objectives/captions. None receives systematic treatment of definition, use, relationship, and boundary/misconception in the notes.
4. **Placeholder derivations (14/14).** “Derivation and assumptions” contains the same instruction to define symbols, not actual intermediate reasoning. Several “formal objects” are undeclared mnemonics or heuristics rather than validated equations (especially lectures 02, 08, 10, 12, 13, and 14).
5. **Copied rather than extended examples (14/14).** The notes repeat the same slide example and add the same command to recompute/perturb it. There are no distinct transfer problems, datasets, code fragments, figures, or extended cases. Numerically checkable statements in lectures 02, 04, 05, 06, 09, and 12 recompute correctly under their stated or inferable assumptions, but the surrounding derivation and interpretation remain inadequate.
6. **Misplaced content (lectures 11–13).** Lecture 11’s worked example is about archive choice rather than statistical ethics; Lecture 12 carries the false-positive calculation prompted in Lecture 11; Lecture 13 carries the 90-second-talk example appropriate to Lecture 12. These are strong indicators of generation/template sequencing errors.
7. **Weak resource guidance (14/14).** All notes have zero hyperlinks. Named resources are generally authoritative in category, but most citations are broad, unannotated, and delegated to `reference-log.md`; Lecture 14 is especially vague. The notes do not tell students what exact page/section to inspect, reproduce, or compare, so “Verified reading” overstates what is present in the pair.
8. **Partly production-facing voice (14/14).** The materials are mostly addressed to students, but repeated phrases such as “Define every symbol…,” “Require an independent recomputation…,” and “Use the exact documentation anchor…” read as author/instructor workflow instructions. They should be replaced by the actual explanation and direct student tasks.
9. **Generic visual evidence (14/14).** The same “geometry distinguishes inputs, transformations, and inferential limits” framing is reused with only labels changed. The notes reproduce the same caption instead of interpreting a lecture-specific dataset, figure, residual plot, observing plan, query result, or ethical case.

### Scientific and reference concerns visible in the pairs

- **Lecture 02:** the conditional-probability notation is not defined as a valid screening model.
- **Lecture 04:** the stated formula includes division by background density, but the numerical audit computes only the Gaussian factor; units and normalization are missing.

- **Lectures 11–13:** misplaced examples undermine topical and scientific coherence.
- **References:** no citation can be fully followed from either artifact because all pairs contain no links and rely on an external reference log. Exact editions/sections, verification status, and the intended student action are not available within this pair-only audit.

## Course-wide patterns

### Blocking / release-relevant patterns

1. **No glossary or term explorer in any deck (14/14).** There are no interactive terms to map to notes and no keyboard/panel/print implementation to validate. For an upper-division computational course, core technical vocabulary should be explicitly selected and developed in the notes through use, relationships, and boundary conditions—not merely appear in prose.
2. **Systematic near-paraphrase and template repetition (14/14).** Each notes file has the same section sequence. Mechanical comparison found **8 exact substantive slide paragraphs/lists repeated in every matching notes file**, while at least **16 boilerplate paragraphs/lists are identical across all 14 notes**, including “Define the symbols…,” “Keep physical quantities and numerical arrays…,” the four generic misconceptions, generic study questions, and the two-meeting preamble. Pair-specific derivation/algorithm sections add value, but too much of each file is reusable scaffolding rather than lecture-specific exposition.
3. **Resource guidance is inadequate (14/14).** Every notes file links only to `../reference-log.md` with “References and provenance: course reference log”; it does not tell students what section, documentation page, figure, notebook, or exercise to inspect or reproduce. Every slide deck repeats the exact same nine-item technical-reference list—even where many items are irrelevant (for example, REBOUND papers on hydrodynamics or Bayesian lectures). This fails the rubric’s requirement for a useful, verified, specifically annotated follow-up resource per lecture.
4. **Instructor-facing timing scripts in student study notes (14/14).** “Two-meeting teaching arc” contains minute-by-minute teaching plans (“10 min recap; 20 min…”). These should be moved to instructor notes and replaced with student-facing synthesis, guided practice, or study sequences.
5. **Depth is not commensurate with two 75-minute meetings.** The notes are compact template documents with one pair-specific derivation paragraph and one algorithm/interpretation block. They are useful supplements to the slides but generally not stand-alone upper-division chapters. The gap is most serious in Lectures 04, 05, 07, and 14.

### Strengths

- Every pair preserves lecture order and central equations, and each notes file adds at least some pair-specific derivation or algorithm interpretation.
- Limitations are usually scientifically responsible: solver error is separated from model discrepancy; synthetic data are labeled; the 512-cell shock profile is not called exact; the DE441 residual is not mislabeled as integrator error; posterior precision is kept conditional on the model.
- Worked values checked from the information in the pairs are internally consistent: binary64 spacing; one-AU period; hydrodynamic sound speeds; transfer intensity and thin-limit errors; Monte Carlo standard errors; RV RMS/chi-square relation; and Beta posterior mean. No clear arithmetic reversal was found.
- Mechanical SVG hashes differ across all 14 decks, so there is **no exact repeated SVG layout** across the course. The problem is repeated rhetorical/template structure and generic reference material, not identical figure coordinates.

- **Lecture 09:** separate the `e^-τ` uncollided Bernoulli estimator from the illustrated scattering random walk so students do not infer that they estimate the same transport quantity.
- **Lecture 10:** label the ordinary `0.0013723` standard error as empirical/plug-in for the realized run; the theoretical value from `p=e^-4` is about `0.0013409`.
