# Baseline Lecture Notes and Slide Deck Audit: ASTR101–ASTR320

## Executive summary

This baseline audit compares all **84 committed lecture-note files directly with their 84 matching committed slide decks** across ASTR101, ASTR120, ASTR210, ASTR230, ASTR310, and ASTR320. The evidence boundary is commit `7a8dd53d1a73e4dc74321615337487438a06e962`; every lecture artifact was retrieved with `git show HEAD:<repository-relative-path>`. Mutable working-tree versions were not audited, so concurrent glossary-production edits do not affect these findings.

**Overall verdict: Needs correction.** None of the 84 note/deck pairs meets the lecture-notes supplementarity standard.

| Verdict | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Meets supplementarity standard | 0 | 0.0% |
| Needs targeted expansion | 8 | 9.5% |
| Needs substantial rewrite | 76 | 90.5% |
| **Total** | **84** | **100%** |

The eight targeted-expansion cases are ASTR120 Lectures 01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 07, 11, and 14. Every lecture in ASTR101, ASTR210, ASTR230, ASTR310, and ASTR320 requires substantial rewrite, as do ASTR120 Lectures 03, 08, 09, 10, 12, and 13.

### Baseline judgment

Across the curriculum, notes usually preserve the deck sequence and repeat its central claims, examples, misconceptions, and lab links instead of answering the independent-study questions required by the rubric: why a claim is true, how an equation is derived, under what assumptions it fails, how evidence supports it, how it connects to adjacent topics, and where a student can investigate further. ASTR120 is the strongest course in voice, context, and selected examples, but even its best notes lack sufficiently specific resource guidance and sustained expansion across all major concepts.

### Highest-priority cross-course actions

1. **Rebuild notes as supplements rather than transcripts.** Keep topic and notation alignment, but add intermediate derivations, assumptions, physical interpretation, limiting cases, distinct worked extensions, evidence analysis, uncertainty, and transfer questions.
2. **Implement and deepen glossary support.** The committed decks contain static vocabulary cards or no interactive explorer, not keyboard-operable one-panel-per-term glossaries. Notes commonly repeat term lists without fuller definitions, relationships, uses, or boundary conditions.
3. **Replace copied or pseudo-quantitative examples.** Many notes reproduce slide arithmetic exactly; several label qualitative checklists as worked quantitative examples. Each lecture needs a notes-only calculation, derivation, or explicitly justified nonnumeric analysis.
4. **Make resources actionable and durable.** Broad chapter ranges, bare local paths, unspecified mission pages, and statements such as “live-verified this session” do not tell students what to read, inspect, reproduce, or compare and do not provide durable provenance.
5. **Correct identified scientific and numerical defects before reuse.** Material issues include ASTR120 L06’s absorbed-flux denominator mismatch; ASTR210 L02’s extinction-relative-to-zenith error; ASTR310 L05’s central pressure-gradient claim; and multiple ASTR320 unit, isotope, scaling-law, interpretation, and provenance defects.
6. **Remove instructor, production, and review-process language.** This is especially severe in ASTR101 and recurrent in ASTR310/ASTR320. Student notes should not discuss what “the instructor should” do, generator functions, repository memory, review history, or production verification.

## Audit method and rating key

For every pair, the audit mapped slide objectives, vocabulary, claims, equations, examples, evidence/visuals, misconceptions, and references to the matching notes; identified duplication and useful additions; checked term development; assessed derivation, interpretation, context, limitations, resources, and voice; and independently recomputed at least one presented numerical example when one existed. Absence of a numerical example is stated explicitly. Ratings mean:

- **Strong:** materially supports independent study at the course level.
- **Partial:** useful added value exists, but important concepts or requirements remain underdeveloped.
- **Weak:** absent, mostly duplicated from slides, non-actionable, or materially unsuitable.

The verdicts are lecture-note supplementarity judgments, not package-release approvals.

## Cross-course repeated failure patterns

- **Template mirroring and verbatim reuse:** ASTR101, ASTR210, ASTR230, ASTR310, and ASTR320 show systematic heading-for-heading or block-for-block reuse. ASTR120 paraphrases more often but still relies on the same short shell and repeated evidence architecture.
- **Glossary failure is universal:** none of the 84 committed decks provides a complete interactive term explorer with keyboard controls, exactly one explanatory panel per term, and print-visible definitions. Static term cards are common, and matching notes frequently repeat terms without deeper treatment.
- **Derivations are asserted rather than taught:** equations appear without intermediate algebra, dimensional reasoning, assumptions, limiting cases, or interpretation. Higher-level courses do not consistently increase mathematical or computational depth.
- **Worked examples rarely add note-only value:** examples are commonly copied from the decks. Some “worked quantitative” sections contain only a qualitative checklist or ranking, and several examples overstate precision or adequacy.
- **Context is often a one-paragraph ceiling:** misconceptions and caveats are present, but uncertainty, selection effects, model dependence, alternative models, and observational inference chains are not developed enough for independent study.
- **Resources are citations rather than guidance:** broad OpenStax ranges, local extraction paths, vague agency references, and transient provenance claims dominate. Students are rarely told which exact section, figure, dataset, query, or calculation to inspect or reproduce.
- **Voice quality is inconsistent:** ASTR120, ASTR210, and ASTR230 are generally student-facing; ASTR101 reads like an instructor lesson plan; ASTR310 and ASTR320 leak generator, production, verification-session, or review-process commentary.
- **Numerical checking catches both sound arithmetic and serious defects:** much routine arithmetic is correct, but duplication lets conceptual and numerical errors propagate unchanged between slides and notes. The pairwise evidence below identifies each issue and the required correction.

## Prioritized program-level recommendations

### Blocking revisions

1. Correct every scientific, mathematical, unit, interpretation, and internal-consistency defect identified in the course tables before reusing either member of the affected pair.
2. Rewrite every `Needs substantial rewrite` notes file so each major concept gains at least three appropriate expansion types from the rubric and the notes no longer track the deck as a compressed transcript.
3. Expand the eight ASTR120 `Needs targeted expansion` files at the specific gaps shown in their rows, especially exact resources, uncertainty, named evidence, and additional derivation or transfer work.
4. Build a common accessible glossary component, then require course authors to provide lecture-specific definitions and fuller matching notes treatment rather than reusing generic cards.
5. Require one distinct, independently checkable note-only example or analysis per lecture, with inputs, units, assumptions, interpretation, and uncertainty where appropriate.
6. Replace bare references and ephemeral provenance with stable citations plus a student action. Verify textbook sections, figures, datasets, mission facts, and URLs before instructional release.

### Enhancements after blocking work

- Add guided figure reading that distinguishes measured, calibrated, derived, and modeled quantities.
- Add lecture-specific retrieval and transfer questions with short solution guidance instead of generic repeated synthesis prompts.
- Add explicit cross-lecture dependency links showing how methods and assumptions evolve.
- Add uncertainty propagation and sensitivity checks to otherwise correct examples.
- Vary notes structure when the science demands it rather than forcing all topics into one template.

### Blocking revisions

1. **Rewrite all 14 notes as genuinely supplementary study documents.** Preserve topic sequence, but add intermediate reasoning, assumptions, interpretation, limiting cases, evidence, and transfer examples rather than repeating slide sentences and captions.
2. **Develop every glossary item.** Across 100 term instances, the notes currently provide zero fuller definitions. Each term needs meaning, use, relationship to another concept/observable, and a boundary condition or misconception where relevant. The committed slides also have no interactive explanatory panels; if they are intended to be term explorers, add exactly one keyboard-accessible, print-visible definition panel per term and ensure the notes go deeper.
3. **Remove production/instructor voice from student notes.** Replace “The lecture should begin,” “The instructor should emphasize,” instructor timing, and “make students commit” with direct explanations, study strategies, or student-directed tasks.
4. **Add real derivations and distinct worked examples.** Nine lectures have recomputable numeric statements, but every one is copied from its deck. Lectures 07, 08, 10, 11, and 14 have no numeric worked example at all despite formula/calculation framing. Each lecture needs at least one notes-only worked example or extension with units, assumptions, and interpretation.
5. **Replace the generic evidence-table prompt.** Every observation is paired with the interchangeable question “What measurement, image, spectrum, or sky observation would support this?” Supply actual named observations, plots, spectra, missions, archives, or reproducible measurements and explain how each supports or constrains the claim.
6. **Provide specific annotated follow-up resources.** Broad OpenStax chapter ranges are useful anchors but do not tell students what to inspect, reproduce, or compare. Lecture 14 is blocking on references because it promises unspecified “selected review links” that are absent. Verify cited figure/chapter details against the adopted edition/source index before instructional release.
7. **Correct lecture-specific issues.** Replace Lecture 10’s reused Lecture 09 historical paragraph; quantify the validity/error of Lecture 12’s cz approximation at z≈0.10; stop labeling qualitative discussions as completed calculations in Lectures 07, 08, 10, 11, and 14.

### Enhancements after blocking revisions

- Add notes-only historical narratives tied to why each model was developed, not merely one introductory sentence.
- Turn `Visual Interpretation` into guided figure reading: axes, encodings, observable versus inferred quantity, uncertainty, and a question whose answer is explained.
- Add comparison tables where categories are easily confused: altitude/azimuth vs. RA/Dec; phase vs. eclipse; flux vs. luminosity; morphology vs. evolution; habitable zone vs. habitable environment.
- Add retrieval practice and transfer questions with brief solution outlines rather than repeating the same four generic synthesis questions.
- Cross-link the lab/data activity to exact variables, uncertainties, and deliverables rather than saying only that the lab “continues lecture reasoning.”

### Recurring patterns

- **Template paraphrase dominates:** every notes file has the same section architecture and stock sentences; exact slide sentences recur in `Lecture Arc`, `Model Development`, `Worked Example`, `Visual Interpretation`, `Misconception and Boundary Condition`, and `Lab or Observing Connection`.
- **Vocabulary is present but pedagogically inert:** all term sets are inventories, not definitions or conceptual relationships.
- **Equations are displayed but not derived:** notes commonly add only “This equation or proportionality is not just a formula to memorize,” which is advice rather than explanation.
- **Evidence is asserted rather than examined:** the repeated evidence-table question never identifies an actual dataset, measurement procedure, uncertainty, or inferential chain.
- **Limitations are named but not analyzed:** two-body perturbations, seeing, density degeneracy, atmospheric retention, redshift approximations, and cosmological scatter are mentioned in one sentence but not quantified or illustrated.
- **Resources are anchors, not guidance:** chapter ranges and a reference-log path lack specific read/inspect/reproduce instructions; Lecture 14’s promised review links are missing.
- **Voice is consistently instructor-facing:** the notes read as a lesson-plan scaffold, not an independent student study resource.

### Numeric and scientific/reference check summary

Recomputed at least one presented numeric example wherever one exists: Lectures 01–06, 09, 12, and 13 are arithmetically correct as stated. Lectures 07, 08, 10, 11, and 14 contain no numeric worked inputs/results, so absence is explicitly recorded above. Scientific caveats requiring expansion are the asymmetric inversion of parallax uncertainty (Lecture 03), the unquantified boundary of the low-redshift approximation at z≈0.10 (Lecture 12), and the non-quantitative “Quantitative Tool” in Lecture 14. Reference guidance is too broad throughout, and Lecture 14’s unspecified “selected review links” are not verifiable from the committed notes.

### Re-review criteria

Re-review only after each pair demonstrates: (1) all slide terms receive fuller notes treatment; (2) notes add multiple substantive expansion types to every major concept; (3) at least one notes-only worked/transfer example with checked arithmetic or an explicit justified nonnumeric alternative; (4) assumptions and limitations are developed, not merely named; (5) one verified, specifically annotated follow-up resource; and (6) fully student-facing prose. Any interactive glossary added to slides must provide one and only one explanatory panel per term, keyboard operation, print-visible definitions, and deeper corresponding treatment in notes.
