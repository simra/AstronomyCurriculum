## Rating and verdict interpretation

- **Strong:** The dimension is substantially useful for independent study, though a row may still fail overall because of other blocking defects.
- **Partial:** Some useful content exists, but it remains incomplete, duplicated, insufficiently qualified, or not actionable.
- **Weak:** The content is largely copied, underdeveloped, vague, or affected by a correctness or voice problem.
- **Absent:** The expected feature is missing.
- **Needs substantial rewrite:** Systematic duplication, absent supplementarity, or blocking correctness/production defects require structural redevelopment.
- **Needs targeted expansion:** The notes are basically independent and correct but need bounded additions.
- **Meets supplementarity standard:** The notes materially deepen the deck and satisfy the pairwise rubric.

## Repeated cross-course failure patterns

1. **Slide duplication instead of study-ready notes.** Major notes sections routinely duplicate slide text and sequence. Repeated generic synthesis prompts do not replace lecture-specific explanation.
2. **Glossaries are non-functional or undeveloped.** Static vocabulary cards or missing glossary controls do not provide keyboard-operable term explorers, one explanatory panel per term, print-visible definitions, or fuller definitions in notes.
3. **Derivations stop at formula presentation.** Equations are commonly asserted, followed by copied numerical outputs without intermediate algebra, unit cancellation, assumptions, limiting cases, uncertainty, or a distinct transfer example.
4. **Context and limitations are too shallow.** Misconception boxes often identify one caveat but do not develop model dependence, competing explanations, observational selection, calibration, geometry, or failure regimes.
5. **Resource guidance is not actionable.** Broad OpenStax chapter labels, generator-script paths, incomplete author-year mentions, and claims of session verification do not tell students what section, figure, dataset, archive product, or calculation to inspect or reproduce.
6. **Production language leaks into student artifacts.** Common examples include “computed programmatically,” “live-verified this session,” review instructions, unresolved formatting variables, “have students,” and generic production captions.
7. **Quantitative errors are repeated in both artifacts.** Because notes copy deck examples, incorrect units, ratios, assumptions, or numerical results are reinforced rather than checked or clarified.
8. **Upper-division rigor is insufficient.** The 300- and 400-level notes frequently omit the mathematical, computational, observational, and inferential development expected after class.

## Prioritized cross-course recommendations

### Blocking revisions

1. Re-author each notes file as an independent-study chapter while preserving lecture sequence: explain why each claim follows, derive central equations, state assumptions and units, analyze limiting cases, and add at least one distinct worked example or transfer problem.
2. Correct every scientific, mathematical, graphical, and comparative-magnitude defect identified below before reusing the same material in slides and notes.
3. Implement an accessible glossary model in each deck and develop every slide term more fully in the matching notes, including use, relationship to another concept or observable, and an important boundary condition or misconception.
4. Replace vague or production-facing reference blocks with verified, durable, annotated resources: exact textbook sections, authoritative documentation, primary/review literature, and reproducible archive or software tasks.
5. Remove instructor directions, generation metadata, review commentary, unresolved placeholders, and meta captions from student-facing artifacts.

### Enhancements after blocking corrections

1. Add lecture-specific diagrams, data products, computational notebooks, or observational examples rather than repeating generic visual and prose templates.
2. Include explicit uncertainty propagation, sensitivity tests, model comparison, and selection effects wherever quantities are inferred from observations.
3. Cross-link notes to prerequisite concepts and later lectures so students can see how methods and assumptions evolve through each course.

**Overall finding:** The scientific topic selection and many worked calculations are promising, but **none of the notes currently meet the supplementarity standard**. Several scientific and production defects further block acceptance, especially unresolved formatting placeholders in Lectures 05, 12, and 13; the wrong sign in Lecture 13’s displayed binding-energy equation; unsupported claims that PSR J0348+0432 has a *measured* 13 km radius; and the false Lecture 14 assertion that no neutron star below the Chandrasekhar mass has been observed.

### Recurring blocking patterns

1. **Systematic slide duplication:** In every lecture, the central explanatory, evidentiary, mathematical, example, misconception, activity, and lab sections are copied from the deck rather than expanded.
2. **No functional glossary explorer:** All decks have static term cards—no buttons, keyboard behavior, `aria-controls`, or one-to-one explanatory panels. Notes repeat terms as bare lists.

### Recurring blocking patterns

1. **Notes duplicate rather than supplement the slides.**  
   Across all 14 lectures, the substantive matched sections are generally exact copies. The notes do not answer the independent-study questions “why,” “how,” “under what assumptions,” and “how do we know?” beyond what already appears in the deck.

2. **Glossaries are neither interactive nor fully developed.**  
   Every committed deck uses static `.card` elements: zero buttons and zero definition panels. Notes repeat the terms as bare lists. Particularly weak coverage occurs in:
   - Lecture 01: “Doppler spectroscopy,” “semi-amplitude K,” “inclination degeneracy”
   - Lecture 02: “occultation”
   - Lecture 06: “iron-mass fraction,” “equation of state,” “interior structure model”
   - Lecture 11: “technosignature”
   - Lecture 12: “hyperthermophile,” “psychrophile,” “halophile”
   - Lecture 13: “panspermia”
   - Lecture 14: “scientific epistemic humility”

3. **Displayed equations are usually asserted, not derived.**  
   Intermediate algebra, assumptions, units, limiting cases, uncertainty propagation, and transfer examples are generally absent. Several later lectures use a schematic relation as a “Working Equation” without defining its variables or model assumptions.

4. **Worked examples are copied verbatim into notes.**  
   They therefore provide no additional scaffolding. Lecture 13 has no genuinely quantitative worked example.

5. **Significant correctness defects require correction before use.**
   - Lecture 04: incorrect/unclear “300-fold mass range” claim.
   - Lecture 05: HD 80606 b flux variation should be roughly **760×**, not 30×.
   - Lecture 06: incorrect TRAPPIST-1 density ranking.
   - Lecture 07: scale height and transmission-amplitude calculations are incorrect.
   - Lecture 08: displayed mass-loss expression is dimensionally invalid.
   - Lecture 09: host-independent HZ flux limits are presented too generally.
   - Lecture 11: O₂–CH₄ atmospheric-removal timescale is inadequately supported and likely oversimplified.
   - Lecture 13: Miller/Watson–Crick chronology is reversed.
   - Lecture 14: the stated spread is exactly \(10^{10}\), not more than \(10^{10}\).

6. **Resource guidance is uniformly inadequate.**  
   Every notes file ends with the same lengthy OpenStax paragraph, which admits that its alignment was extrapolated and awaits human checking. Named papers in the body are not supplied as complete, annotated references, and students are not told what figure, table, spectrum, archive query, calculation, or section to examine.

7. **Production-facing language appears in student materials.**  
   Repeated phrases include **“live-verified this session,” “these citations follow the ASTR320/330 precedent,” “flagged for human spot-check,”** and **“Every numeric result above is computed programmatically.”** These should be replaced by stable citations, transparent dataset provenance, and student-directed explanations.

8. **Generic course-wide scaffolding replaces lecture-specific study support.**  
   The same **“Synthesis Questions”** and the same visual-reasoning prompt—“Trace the reasoning chain from raw observation…to inferred quantity”—recur throughout. Although the SVG signatures differ, the instructional prompt and captions are formulaic and are not converted into lecture-specific explanatory analysis in the notes.

### Blocking course-wide findings

1. **The notes are not supplements.**  
   Across every pair, the central evidence, model, equation, example, misconception, activity, and lab sections are copied from the slides. The notes do not supply intermediate reasoning, independent examples, historical development, deeper observational context, or expanded limitations.

2. **Glossary implementation and pedagogy fail the required standard.**  
   The committed decks contain static cards rather than interactive keyboard-operable term controls, with no explanatory panels or print-expanded definitions. Notes repeat lists rather than developing the terms. All 87 terms need auditable definition coverage; 16 currently have no use outside Vocabulary:
   - L01: *convective zone*
   - L04: *thin flux tube, penumbra*
   - L05: *free magnetic energy*
   - L07: *aerodynamic drag*
   - L08: *Parker solar wind, terminal wind speed*
   - L09: *Doppler imaging, remote sensing*
   - L11: *Chapman–Ferraro balance, magnetic compression*
   - L12: *storm main phase, recovery phase*
   - L13: *ionospheric scintillation*
   - L14: *transformer saturation, space-weather forecasting*

3. **Lecture 05 contains a major numerical/interpretive contradiction.**  
   \(4.7\times10^{29}\) J is not “within” \(10^{24}\)–\(10^{25}\) J. The discrepancy is approximately 47,000–470,000 times. The calculation also treats a strong photospheric umbral field as though it filled the entire coronal active-region volume and equates total magnetic energy with free energy.

4. **Lectures 07 and 10 contain unresolved template variables.**  
   L07 exposes `{CME_TRAVEL_TIME_CONSTANT_SPEED_HR:.1f}` and `{STORM_TRANSIT_HOURS_OBSERVED:.0f}`. L10 exposes `{PSP_PERIHELION_RSUN:.2f}` and `{PARKER_RC_RSUN:.2f}`.

5. **Lecture 04’s pressure-balance interpretation is physically incomplete.**  
   Because the calculated magnetic pressure exceeds the quoted ambient gas pressure, the same-depth equation would require negative internal gas pressure. The explanation must explicitly move the comparison to a deeper geometric layer rather than saying the pressure merely approaches zero.

6. **Lecture 12 mixes \(Dst^*\) and Dst without evaluating the approximation.**  
   The displayed DPS relation uses pressure-corrected \(Dst^*\), while the worked calculation substitutes raw Dst. This may be acceptable as an approximation only if stated and bounded.

7. **Resources are not usable study guidance.**  
   Every notes file has **zero hyperlinks**. The repeated OpenStax Chapter 15 paragraph is broad and largely unrelated to the advanced mathematics. Named sources and datasets are not given as complete citations with exact sections/products, and students are not told what to inspect or reproduce.

8. **Production-facing prose appears in student artifacts.**  
   Repeated examples include “cross-checked directly…this session,” “not extrapolated by analogy from a prior course,” and “Every numeric result above is computed programmatically…”. L07 additionally says “a careful reviewer must check.” These should be moved to a reference log or production record.

### Scientific and reference concerns

#### Blocking scientific defects

1. **Lecture 09 contains a nine-order-of-magnitude arithmetic/unit error.**  
   For `a=0.005 pc` and `P=15.8 yr`,
   \[
   M=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{GP^2}\approx 4.394\times10^6\,M_\odot,
   \]
   not `4.3941e+15 solar masses`. Both slides and notes repeat the incorrect result while claiming it was “recomputed independently during review.”

2. **Lecture 01’s visual contradicts its equation.**  
   The SVG polyline rises as “height above plane” increases, although the axis is “stellar density” and the label is `exp(-|z|/h)`. Density should decrease with increasing \(|z|\).

3. **Lecture 03’s “Population Histogram” is potentially backwards.**  
   Its displayed bars make O stars most numerous and K stars least numerous, with M stars omitted. If intended as stellar number counts, this conflicts with the IMF and typical populations; if it represents another quantity, the axis and normalization must say so.

4. **Lecture 12’s calculation is not auditable.**  
   It refers to “seven listed points,” but no points are listed in either committed artifact. Thus `χ²=24.891` cannot be reproduced.

5. **Lectures 08, 13, and 14 use worked examples disconnected from their central equations.**
   - Lecture 08 states an NFW profile but repeats the Lecture 05 circular-mass estimate.
   - Lecture 13 states a lookback-time identity but repeats the same enclosed mass.
   - Lecture 14 presents a model-component set but again repeats the enclosed mass.
   These do not satisfy the stated promise of one lecture-specific auditable calculation.

6. **Lecture 06’s resonance relation is incomplete as general guidance.**  
   `m(Ω−Ωp)=κ` does not by itself present both Lindblad branches or corotation, despite an objective to “locate resonances.”

7. **Lecture 05 does not explicitly state the geometry behind `M(<R)=Rv_c²/G`.**  
   This is an exact enclosed-mass relation for spherical symmetry, not a general exact disk mass estimator.
